## 1. Three kinds of edge

| Kind | What it means | Example from a decision summary | Rows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shown not to help | A subgroup or outcome was studied and the benefit was not observed | reSET: no abstinence benefit in self-identified primary opioid users (5.7 percent with device vs 15.9 percent with standard therapy) | TM138 |
| Stated as a limitation | The indication or labeling limits the claim | EndeavorRx: patients "may not display benefits in typical behavioral symptoms, such as hyperactivity" | TM144 |
| Not studied | Outside the inclusion criteria, dose or setting tested | reSET's indication excludes opioid replacement therapy and alcohol-only users; it is an adjunct to contingency management in outpatient care | TM132 |

"Not studied" is not "shown not to help". The limit text says which.

## 2. Adjunct, not standalone

882.5801 identifies the device as an adjunct to clinician-supervised outpatient treatment, and labeling must warn it is not a standalone therapy (TM119, TM122). 882.5803 is also an adjunct to clinician-supervised treatment (TM123). A product feature or message that presents the therapy as standalone goes past that edge.

## 3. Clinical significance

ORBIT sets milestones on clinical, not statistical, significance and in advance (TM57). 880.5735 labeling discusses the limitations of the clinical significance of the results (TM128). Whether a result is clinically meaningful is the person's judgment with the owner they name for clinical matters.

## 4. Deciding whether to go past an edge: evidence to decision

| Rule | Rows |
|---|---|

## 6. Who reopens

The person names who may reopen each limit and on what evidence. The old rule stands: a limit is not reopened because a stakeholder asked (old DH-01.01.4.1.3). IMDRF N41 recommends independent review of clinical evidence for higher-risk SaMD categories (PL66, PL67); whether it applies is the person's choice to justify (research file "Changes and conflicts": independence is risk-proportionate, not a QMSR rule).
