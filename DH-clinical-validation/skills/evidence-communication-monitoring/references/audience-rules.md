# Audience rules for evidence communication

Interpretive reference. Rows: `promotion-and-claims-research.md` (PC rows) and `clinical-study-and-rwe-research.md` (CV rows), read 19 October 2026. This file decides which rules bind an evidence narrative for each audience and market. It does not clear anything: every narrative for an external audience stops for the owner the person names for regulatory or legal review, or owner not named. Marketing claims and ads are dh-commercial's (pricing-and-value-proposition, pricing-and-value-message-monitoring).

## Why this matters for evidence, not only for ads

- In the US, intended use is shown by labeling claims, advertising and oral or written statements (PC-US-17); in the EU, intended purpose includes promotional or sales materials and statements (PC-EU-03); in Great Britain, promotional material sets medical purpose (PC-UK-01). An evidence narrative that a company hands out is such a statement.
- Labeling covers matter accompanying the article, and FDA treats payor HCEI that meets the definition as labeling (PC-US-02). Misleading includes failure to reveal material facts (PC-US-03).

## United States

| Audience | What binds the narrative | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| Payor, formulary or technology assessment committee, on the cleared or authorized indication | Economic analysis (HCEI) is not false or misleading if it relates to the cleared indication, rests on competent and reliable scientific evidence, and carries a conspicuous statement of material differences from labeling; CARSE covers clinical inputs too; present design, generalizability, limitations, sensitivity analyses and balanced information. Not for prescribers or a public website. The 2018 final guidance is still the final text; the June 2026 revised draft replaces it once final. | PC-US-04 to PC-US-07, PC-US-34 to PC-US-40, PC-US-47 |
| Payor, before clearance or for an uncleared use | Factual results only, no safety or effectiveness conclusions; state that the product or use is not cleared and safety and effectiveness are not established; development stage; study design and limitations; positive and negative findings; for an uncleared use of a cleared product, the cleared indication and current labeling; update when materially outdated | PC-US-08 to PC-US-10, PC-US-43 to PC-US-46 |
| Clinician, cleared use | Consistent with labeling: for 510(k) and exempt devices test against the 510(k) modification rules (PC-US-22); for De Novo and PMA devices the three factors (PC-US-23, PC-US-24). Evidence scientifically appropriate and statistically sound for the claim; disclosure does not cure a study too weak for the claim; material design and limitations disclosed clearly and prominently; unfavorable findings disclosed; review by medical or scientific, legal and regulatory personnel before release | PC-US-22 to PC-US-33 |
| Clinician, uncleared use | Scientific information on unapproved uses (source publications, firm presentations with the source) under the January 2025 final guidance, which is marked not for current implementation pending OMB action; required disclosures; kept separate from promotion; unreliable studies not shared | PC-US-50 to PC-US-56 |
| Consumer or patient | FTC: deceptive acts and false advertisements, including omissions; substantiation before the ad; health benefit claims generally need randomized controlled human testing; a disclaimer cannot contradict a claim. These FTC guidance rows are [WF]. | PC-US-67, PC-US-69, PC-US-70, PC-US-72 to PC-US-74 [WF] |
| Journal or congress | ICMJE authorship and AI rules, CONSORT 2025 and the matching checklist; registration before first consent | CV110 to CV137 (see `disclosure-rules.md`; [PAPER] rows carry their DOI there and in every answer, for example CONSORT 2025, CV110 to CV114, DOI 10.1136/bmj-2024-081123) |

## European Union and Germany

| Audience | What binds the narrative | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| Anyone, device | MDR Article 7: no text, name or picture that may mislead on intended purpose, safety or performance; no functions the device lacks, no false impression on treatment or diagnosis, no hiding of a likely risk, no use outside the assessed intended purpose | PC-EU-01 to PC-EU-03 |
| Consumers | UCPD (2005 text read; amendments not reflected): misleading actions and omissions; false cure claims and false approval claims always unfair | PC-EU-05 to PC-EU-11 |
| Germany, any device advertising | HWG applies to devices; no attributing effects the product lacks; no impression of certain success or no harmful effects; a cited study must say whether it concerns the advertised product, with author, date and source, and quotes, tables and figures reproduced faithfully | PC-DE-01, PC-DE-03 to PC-DE-05 |
| Germany, outside professional circles | Stricter public rules: no suggestion that health improves by use or suffers without it; no disguised advertising; no misleading third-party statements | PC-DE-02, PC-DE-09 to PC-DE-11 |
| Germany, DiGA | Ad-free; DiGA data not used for advertising | PC-DE-15, PC-DE-16 |
| Trial participants and the public (EU CTR trial) | Lay summary with the results summary within one year of the end of the trial; Annex V content not sourced `[GAP]` | CV152 |

## United Kingdom

| Audience | What binds the narrative | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| Anyone, software | Promotion sets medical purpose; disclaimers do not undo it; testimonials count as manufacturer claims; words such as "clinically proven" point toward device status | PC-UK-01 to PC-UK-04 |
| Consumers | DMCCA 2024: misleading actions; false prevent or treat claims and false approval claims always unfair | PC-UK-05 to PC-UK-08 |
| Advertising | CAP Code section 12 [INDUSTRY]: objective claims need evidence; medical claims only with conformity marking; no discouraging essential treatment; substantiated symptom-relief claims acceptable; no unqualified cure | PC-UK-09 to PC-UK-13 [INDUSTRY] |
