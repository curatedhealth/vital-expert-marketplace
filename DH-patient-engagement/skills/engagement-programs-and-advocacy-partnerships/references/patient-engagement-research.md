## Changes and conflicts

- PPI guidance superseded. The task named the 2016 CDRH "Patient Preference Information" guidance. FDA replaced it with a final guidance dated 30 March 2026 (draft 6 September 2024) that covers the total product life cycle (PE-22). Use the 2026 document.
- PFDD Guidance 3 moved from draft (June 2022) to final (October 2025), and CDRH is now a co-issuer (PE-10). Guidance 4 is still a draft dated April 2023 (PE-17). Guidances 1 and 2 are final (June 2020, February 2022). The December 2009 PRO guidance will be replaced only when the whole series is final (PE-18).
- Diversity action plans. The statute requires final guidance within 9 months of the close of comments (comments closed 26 September 2024), so the statutory deadline has passed by this pass's reading (PE-39, PE-40). On 19 October 2026 FDA still lists the guidance as a draft, restored under a court order (PE-41). Because the DAP duty applies only to studies enrolling more than 180 days after the final guidance is published (PE-36), the statutory DAP requirement is not yet triggered while no final guidance exists. This is a reading of the statute and the FDA page, not a statement by FDA.
- CMS Framework for Health Equity: the PDF still downloads from cms.gov, but the CMS framework program page tried (cms.gov/priorities/health-equity/minority-health/equity-programs/framework) returned 404 on 19 October 2026. Its current standing as CMS policy is not established.
- NIH-designated populations: the current NIMHD page lists "Sexual Minority Groups" (PE-125). Earlier NIH material used a broader sexual and gender minority category; that earlier wording was not read in this pass, so only the current wording is reported.
- 45 CFR 84.84 web accessibility dates read 11 May 2027 and 10 May 2028 in the eCFR on 1 October 2026 (PE-112). Whether these replaced earlier dates was not checked in this pass.
- The EMA eligibility criteria document (2018) carries a London address. It was still linked from EMA's key documents page, and the 2022 framework refers to the same criteria.
- Industry codes are self-regulation. The EFPIA Code binds member associations and companies, the ABPI Code is administered by the PMCPA, and neither is law. The AdvaMed and PhRMA U.S. codes cover HCP interactions and have no patient-organisation chapter equivalent to EFPIA Article 21 or ABPI Clause 27.
