# Support to patients and patient groups: the elements the compliance owner weighs

Interpretive reference for task B1. Rows: CP- (`channels-partners-research.md`), PE- (`patient-engagement-research.md`), PC- (`promotion-and-claims-research.md`). Read on 19 October 2026; not owner-reviewed. Not legal advice. The model lays out the elements; it never says an arrangement is protected, compliant or low risk. Every item in this file stops for the person's compliance or legal owner.

## 1. What counts as support

Anything of value given to a patient, caregiver or patient group: devices or phones lent or given, data plans, gift cards, travel, meals, honoraria, free services, copay or cost-sharing help, grants and donations, sponsored events. OIG treated even a restricted loaner phone as remuneration because it could make domestic calls (CP-US-40).

|---|---|---|

| Receiving side | Soliciting or receiving is the same offense | CP-US-02 |

## 4. Beneficiary CMP exceptions

| Exception | Elements | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| Promotes access to care, low risk | Improves the beneficiary's ability to obtain covered items and services; unlikely to skew clinical decisions; unlikely to raise costs through overuse; no safety or quality concerns | CP-US-36, CP-US-37 |
| Equal terms to the general public | Offered on equal terms regardless of insurance status (retail rewards) | CP-US-38 |
| Financial need | Not advertised, not tied to other covered services, reasonably connected to medical care, given after a good-faith determination of financial need | CP-US-38 |

## 5. Advisory opinion 19-02 as a pattern (favorable, binds only its requestor)

OIG approved a drug maker's temporary loan of limited-function smartphones to financially needy patients lacking compatible technology, for an app paired with a sensor-embedded drug (CP-US-39). Safeguards OIG relied on: not advertised to patients; eligibility limited by need and lack of a compatible phone; no more than two 12-week periods. OIG said a phone with general functions (browser, camera, other apps) would likely change the outcome (CP-US-41). The opinion protects only its requestor and the facts it certified (CP-US-43). Use it only to show which safeguards OIG weighed.

## 6. Independent charity patient assistance

Cost-sharing help from bona fide independent charities should not raise anti-kickback concerns even with manufacturer donations; a manufacturer-controlled foundation is not independent (PE-85). Donors exert no influence or control (PE-88); disease funds broad (PE-86); donor reports aggregate only (PE-87).
