# Patient & User Experience (DH)

This pack replaces two packs: dh-patient-engagement (DH-02.03) and dh-user-experience (DH-02.04).

## Pack map

- `patient-and-user-experience-capability-map`: The map of the VITAL Patient & User Experience pack for digital health teams.

## DH-02.03 Patient Engagement & Advocacy

- `patient-research-and-co-design`: Plan, capture and act on patient and carer co-design for a digital health product or program: the research question and the decision it serves, who takes part and why, the ethics and consent questions (Common Rule exemptions, MHRA consent), patient experience and patient preference rules (FDA PFDD, PPI guidance final 30 March 2026, EU HTA patient input, NICE, UK Standards for Public Involvement), session capture in participants' own words, an insight log from capture to decision, and the accept, defer or refuse decision per point with a linked design change.
- `co-design-quality-monitoring`: Check whether patient and carer co-design was done well and actually used in a digital health product or program: trace each design change credited to co-design back to what participants said, flag changes the team had already planned, check that participants matched the research question (patients, not staff standing in), check reporting against GRIPP2 and the UK Standards for Public Involvement, and track repeated points left undecided to an owner and a decision date the person sets.
- `engagement-programs-and-advocacy-partnerships`: Design a patient engagement program and its advocacy partnerships for a digital health product (SaMD, DTx, connected device, health app): purpose, the action it asks and refuses, activities and the content each may use, what program material may say, the boundary and written terms with a patient organization (role, payment, editorial independence, EFPIA and ABPI disclosure, EMA and EU HTA conflict rules), and a check of any support to patients against the US patient engagement safe harbor (2026 cap), beneficiary inducement rules and OIG opinion 19-02.
- `engagement-and-advocacy-impact-monitoring`: Check whether a patient engagement program and its advocacy partnerships deliver, for a digital health product (SaMD, DTx, connected device, health app): result definitions tied to the action the program asked for (not attendance or followers), results per activity from the person's records, unscored activities and off-list claims, dropout and nonusage attrition with a usage half-life, support per patient per year against the 2026 safe-harbor figure, partnership agreement and disclosure gaps (EFPIA, ABPI), and one decision per missed activity with the owner the person names.
- `engagement-kpi-dashboards`: Define, compute and place patient engagement KPIs for a digital health program or product (DTx, SaMD app, patient support program): KPI cards with numerator, valid denominator, period, grain and source; reach, adoption, penetration, effective engagement and attrition computed from the person's own event file; nonusage and dropout attrition curves with usage half-life; and one dashboard per audience with its refresh job, owner and visible timestamp.
- `kpi-reliability-monitoring`: Check whether patient engagement KPIs for a digital health program or product can be trusted: each KPI card's quality (decision, numerator, valid denominator, period, grain, source, missing-data rule), drift between the card and the formula the dashboard actually uses, missing and inconsistent event data, recomputation of a dashboard number from the source, the measurement properties of questionnaire-based KPIs (COSMIN), and a repair plan with the owners the person names.
- `health-equity-and-inclusive-design`: Find who a digital health product or patient program may leave out and shape the change that reaches them: subgroups the person defines (language, disability, age, connectivity, deprivation, site type), the step where exclusion shows, groups the data cannot see, the partner who could see them, and an inclusive change ranked only on the person's scores, with an owner and a version.
- `health-equity-monitoring`: Check whether an inclusive change or a digital health program actually reached the groups it was meant for, from aggregate counts only: the evidence definition (change, group, record of use, denominator, period), reach and outcome rates per subgroup the person defines with intervals, gaps against the reference group they name, small-cell suppression under their rule, the share not recorded, and a follow-up with the owner they name and the next period to score.

## DH-02.04 User Experience Excellence

- `accessibility-standards-implementation`: Work out which accessibility rules apply to a digital health product and turn them into a release requirement.
- `accessibility-compliance-monitoring`: Check a digital health release against the accessibility requirement it set: sample screens and complete processes against the claimed WCAG version and level (2.0, 2.1 or 2.2) or EN 301 549 clause, summarize and age the accessibility issue log (criterion, level, the severity the team assigned, status) against that target, separate day-one tasks from later screens, and lay out the release decision under the team's own rule.
- `design-system-development`: Build and govern a design system for digital health products: write a component contract (states, WCAG 2.2 criteria it must meet such as 24 by 24 CSS pixel targets, test evidence, content it must never invent, allowed surfaces), assess a component change (which products and clinical surfaces to recheck, and the change-control questions when it sits in a medical device user interface under MDR Annex I or FDA human factors guidance), and plan adoption and contribution (lifecycle status, build, promote or deprecate, owners).
- `design-system-adoption-monitoring`: Measure whether a digital health design system is used as built: component adoption across products, version drift (which products run old or forked components, by owner, with clinical and medical device surfaces shown separately), accessibility status of each component version in use, checks of a live surface against the component contract (states, WCAG 2.2 criteria such as target size, content it must never invent), and a review naming the fix owner and the version that carries each fix.
- `patient-and-clinician-user-research`: Plan and run formative user research with patients, caregivers and clinicians for a digital health product: the decision the study serves, user groups and how many per group, methods (interviews, contextual inquiry, task sessions, remote sessions), the ethics screen, session records with stops and participants' words, SUS scoring from the person's file, and turning repeated stops into actions or refusals with owners.
- `user-research-quality-monitoring`: Check whether patient and clinician user research for a digital health product was sound and acted on: whether participants matched the question and user groups, whether the method supports the claim made from it (formative vs human factors validation, think-aloud, employees), protocol completeness, GRIPP2 reporting of patient involvement, insights traced to decisions and shipped changes, open and unowned findings, SUS across rounds, and a review of unactioned stops with the owners the person names.
- `clinical-workflow-ux-design`: Design how a digital health screen and its service fit real clinical work: the task, role and moment; data shown and withheld; the step it replaces or adds; a service blueprint on request; use errors and critical tasks per FDA human factors guidance (reissued 3 August 2026) and MDR Annex I; controls; what CDS Criterion 4 and ONC decision support rules ask the interface to show; options scored by the person; the formative plan or service pilot.
- `usability-monitoring`: Read usability evidence for a digital health product against the person's own acceptance rules: use errors and task success by task and by the severity the person assigned; whether a test meets FDA validation conditions (15 per user group, no employees, no think-aloud, final UI); SUS with published benchmarks as context; whether each risk control is in the shipped build; field signals and comparable recalls; and a release review with repairs and owners.
