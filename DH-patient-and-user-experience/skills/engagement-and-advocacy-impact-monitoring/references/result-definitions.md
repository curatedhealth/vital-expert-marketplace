# Result definitions: what the sources say about measuring engagement

Interpretive reference for task A1. Rows: PE- (`patient-engagement-research.md`), CP- (`channels-partners-research.md`). Rows marked [PAPER] are published methods; cite the DOI given in the row. Read on 19 October 2026; not owner-reviewed. The sources supply definitions and measurement properties. They set no target for any program: targets are the person's.

## 1. The rule kept from the old task

A result is the action named in the program's purpose, not attendance, reach or follower counts (old task "Engagement result definition", CF-12.02.03.2.2.1). Each definition names: the action, the period, and the record that proves it.

## 2. Definitions the sources give

| Term | Definition | Row |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement with a digital behavior change intervention [PAPER] | (1) The extent of usage: amount, frequency, duration, depth; and (2) a subjective experience: attention, interest, affect (Perski et al. 2017, doi:10.1007/s13142-016-0453-1) | PE-105 |
| Effective engagement [PAPER] | Engagement sufficient to achieve the intended outcomes, defined empirically, rather than simply more engagement (Yardley et al. 2016, doi:10.1016/j.amepre.2016.06.015; abstract only) | PE-104 |
| Law of attrition [PAPER] | In any eHealth trial a substantial share of users drop out or stop using the application (Eysenbach 2005, doi:10.2196/jmir.7.1.e11) | PE-101 |
| Dropout attrition and nonusage attrition [PAPER] | Lost to follow-up versus stopped using; report them separately | PE-102 |
| Attrition metrics [PAPER] | Raw attrition proportions over time as curves; relative risk of dropping out or stopping use; a usage half-life | PE-103 |
| Patient activation [PAPER] | Four stages from believing the patient role matters to staying the course under stress (Hibbard et al. 2004, doi:10.1111/j.1475-6773.2004.00269.x) | PE-98 |
| PAM properties [PAPER] | Valid, highly reliable, unidimensional, usable at individual level; a 13-item short form has similar properties (abstract only) | PE-99, PE-100 |
| Patient experience data | Data about patients' experience with a disease, collected by anyone including advocacy organizations | PE-04 |
| Health disparity | A measurable difference in health between population groups (NIH) | PE-124 |

PAM activation level cut points are `[GAP]` (research "Not sourced").

## 3. When the result is measured with a questionnaire

| Property | Rule | Row |
|---|---|---|
| Content validity is the most important property [PAPER] | Items relevant, comprehensive, comprehensible for the construct and population (COSMIN, Prinsen et al. 2018, doi:10.1007/s11136-018-1798-3) | PE-93 |
| Rating | Sufficient (+), insufficient (-), indeterminate (?); evidence graded with a GRADE approach | PE-94 |
| Reliability and internal consistency | ICC or weighted kappa at least 0.70; Cronbach's alpha at least 0.70 per unidimensional scale, with structural validity evidence | PE-95 |
| Structural validity and measurement error | CFI or TLI above 0.95, or RMSEA below 0.06, or SRMR below 0.08; SDC or LoA smaller than the MIC | PE-96 |
| Clinical outcome assessments in regulatory use | Four COA types; a PRO comes directly from the patient | PE-11, PE-12 |
| Meaningful change | An anchor needs a well-justified definition of meaningful change (draft guidance) | PE-19, PE-20 |
| Missing data | Avoid missing COA data and record the reasons (draft guidance) | PE-21 |

These thresholds are the source's criteria for a measurement property, not targets for a program.

## 4. Who the result counts

- Probability sampling makes results more likely to reflect the target population; non-probability samples may not (PE-06). A program's participants are rarely a probability sample: say so beside any rate.
- Who is reached and who is left out is Patient Engagement 4.x; point there for equity analysis. Population figures (for example SVI percentiles, PE-120 to PE-123) are context, never targets.

## 5. Reporting involvement and its impact

- GRIPP2 [PAPER] is the international guidance for reporting patient and public involvement: long form 34 items where involvement is the main focus, short form 5 items (aims, methods, results, outcomes, critical perspective) where it is secondary (PE-89 to PE-92; doi:10.1136/bmj.j3453).
- UK Standards for Public Involvement, "Impact": identify and share the difference involvement makes (PE-68). A framework, not a rule (PE-65).
- EMA reports stakeholder engagement to its Management Board every two years (PE-47); this is EMA's own practice, cited only as an example of a reporting rhythm.

## 6. What a result is not

- Engagement, uptake, satisfaction or attendance is not clinical evidence of benefit (pack rule). A clinical claim from program data stops for the regulatory owner.
- Patient satisfaction or convenience alone is excluded as the basis of outcome measures in the value-based safe harbors (CP-US-13, CP-US-22). Cite this only where the person's program sits in such an arrangement.
