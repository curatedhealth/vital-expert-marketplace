# UX, accessibility and usability: sourced reference

Research pass of 19 October 2026. Primary sources only (statutes and regulations, regulators, standards bodies' official pages, official design-system pages, and one peer-reviewed paper read in full at PubMed Central). Rows marked [WF] would mean the source was read through a web-fetch tool rather than a raw download; this pass has no [WF] rows. Rows marked [PAPER] are peer-reviewed papers read in full text. Nothing in this file has been reviewed by the owner. Facts are written for skill builders: each row is a rule, definition, date, threshold or required element that a skill can apply. Where a source states only a principle or a recommendation, the row says so.

## Method

- All sources downloaded with curl through the configured proxy, TLS verification on. HTML converted to text by stripping tags and collapsing whitespace. PDFs converted with `pdftotext -layout`; the two Federal Register interim final rules and the HTI-5 proposed rule were also converted in reading order (`*_flow.txt`) because they print in three columns.
- eCFR was read through its renderer API (`/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/...`) on 19 October 2026; one batch was refused with a temporary block and succeeded on sequential retry.
- EUR-Lex returned an empty 202 challenge. EU legal texts were fetched from the EU Publications Office cellar (`publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/<CELEX>`, XHTML). The MDR was read in the consolidated version of 10 January 2025 (CELEX 02017R0745-20250110); a later consolidation was not looked for.
- ETSI's deliverables directory was listed to find the newest EN 301 549 edition; both V3.2.1 (2021-03) and V4.1.1 (2026-09) were downloaded.
- iso.org returned 403. The IEC 62366-1 public abstract was read on the IEC webstore page instead.
- The FDA guidance landing pages were read first to confirm current status; both human factors guidances changed in 2026 (see "Changes and conflicts").
- dl.acm.org (Nielsen and Landauer 1993) and link.springer.com (Faulkner 2003) returned bot challenges; uxpajournal.org no longer serves the Journal of Usability Studies archive at the cited URLs (404). Those papers were therefore not read and are listed under "Not sourced". Europe PMC was searched for open-access method papers; one SUS paper was read in full.
- Raw files are in `raw/`. Quotes were checked by script against the raw text (see end of file). Note: `raw/fda_hf2016.*` holds the August 2026 reissue of the FDA human factors guidance (the file name reflects the expected 2016 edition before download); `raw/fda_hfcontent2026.*` is the final May 2026 content guidance.
- Row prefixes: UX-STD (standards), UX-US (United States), UX-EU (European Union), UX-UK (United Kingdom), UX-M (research methods).

## Changes and conflicts

1. ADA Title II compliance dates moved. The April 2024 rule set 24 April 2026 and 26 April 2027. A DOJ interim final rule effective 20 April 2026 (91 FR 20902; regulatory text at 91 FR 20912) moved them to 26 April 2027 and 26 April 2028 (UX-US-08, UX-US-09, UX-US-11). It is an interim final rule with comments closed 22 June 2026; a later final rule could change the dates again. Any source still quoting April 2026 is stale.
2. HHS Section 504 web and mobile dates moved. Original 11 May 2026 and 10 May 2027; an HHS interim final rule effective 7 May 2026 (91 FR 25496; text at 91 FR 25507) moved them to 11 May 2027 and 10 May 2028 (UX-US-16 to UX-US-18). Also interim; comments closed 6 July 2026.
3. Section 1557 (45 CFR 92.204) does not itself state WCAG 2.1 AA. The current text requires websites and apps of recipients to comply with Section 504 "as interpreted consistent with title II of the ADA" (UX-US-21). The WCAG 2.1 AA standard and dates come from the 504 rule (45 CFR 84.84), not from 92.204. The task brief's framing ("WCAG 2.1 AA, compliance date" under 92.204) does not match the regulatory text.
4. Section 508 still cites WCAG 2.0 AA (2017 rule), while ADA Title II and HHS 504 cite WCAG 2.1 AA, and the UK government and NHS require WCAG 2.2 AA. A product serving US federal, US state and health, and UK public-sector buyers should target WCAG 2.2 AA, which conforms to 2.0 and 2.1 (UX-STD-24), while still testing 4.1.1 Parsing where a 2.0 or 2.1 policy applies (UX-STD-08).
5. EN 301 549 has two live editions. V3.2.1 (2021-03, WCAG 2.1) is the edition cited in the OJ under the Web Accessibility Directive (UX-EU-01). V4.1.1 (2026-09, WCAG 2.2) was adopted 24 August 2026 and is written to support the EAA; its national dates run to 31 May 2027 (publication) and 31 May 2028 (withdrawal of conflicting standards) (UX-STD-27). Whether V4.1.1 has been cited in the OJ was not checked.
6. FDA "Applying Human Factors and Usability Engineering to Medical Devices" was reissued on 3 August 2026; the 2016 document is superseded. The 2026 text still sets 15 participants per distinct user population and still bars think-aloud in validation (UX-US-35, UX-US-40). Changes between 2016 and 2026 were not diffed.
7. FDA "Content of Human Factors Information in Medical Device Marketing Submissions": draft of 9 December 2022 finalized 29 May 2026 (UX-US-47). Any source treating it as draft is stale.
8. FDA CDS guidance: the 29 January 2026 version supersedes a 6 January 2026 issue (UX-US-64), which itself replaced the 2022 final guidance (not read here). It now allows enforcement discretion for a single clinically appropriate recommendation (UX-US-74).
9. ONC: HTI-5 proposed rule (29 December 2025) would remove the DSI source-attribute and risk-management requirements, the safety-enhanced design (user-centered design testing) criterion and the accessibility-centered design criterion (UX-US-24, UX-US-60, UX-US-63). No final rule was found in a Federal Register search on 19 October 2026, and the eCFR still shows the current text. Treat these requirements as in force but at risk.
10. MHRA human factors guidance: the landing page shows an update on 15 January 2025, but the linked PDF is still labeled v2.0, January 2021. The PDF content predates the 2024 post-market surveillance amendment it mentions on the landing page.
11. WCAG 2.2 has two Recommendation dates (5 October 2023 and 12 December 2024). Cite 5 October 2023 as first publication and 12 December 2024 as the current dated version. USWDS describes it as "released" in October 2023, consistent with the first date.
12. EAA and health apps: the Directive has no health category (UX-EU-04). Any claim that "health apps fall under the EAA" needs a specific route, such as e-commerce or consumer banking features inside the app, or national gold-plating. Treat blanket claims as unsupported.
