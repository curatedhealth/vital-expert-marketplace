# Governance records: management review, internal audit, roles

Interpretive reference for A2. Row ids point to `quality-systems-research.md` and `qms-requirements-additions.md`. The skill never suggests job titles or names owners: owners and attendees are the person's.

## What changed on 2 February 2026 for the records

| Fact | Consequence for governance design | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| Under the old QS regulation, 820.180(c) shielded management review, quality audit and supplier audit reports from FDA inspection. The QMSR keeps no such exception. | Write management review minutes and audit reports as records an investigator will read. A finding that would have been kept "off the record" now sits in an inspectable document. | QS15 |
| FDA calls robust management review and internal and supplier audit programs fundamental to the culture of quality it expects. | The cadence and its records are themselves evidence. A review that lists no inputs or decisions is weak evidence. | QS16 |
| FDA reasons that these records are kept in the regular course of business and should be readily available; other regulators and MDSAP auditing organizations already see them. | Retrieval by meeting and date is part of the design: index the records. | BX14 |
| Investigators may review QMS records created before 2 February 2026; FDA suggests a comparative analysis showing older records meet the QMSR. | Decide whether older management reviews and audits need a comparative analysis. That decision is the person's. | QS26 |
| Management review sits in the Management Oversight area, which checks that top management maintains an effective QMS with resources and uses risk-based decisions. Model 2 inspections evaluate management review and internal audits at minimum. | Design advice, not an FDA statement: management review records that show risk-based and resource decisions are stronger evidence for the Management Oversight area than status reports alone. Do not attribute this sentence to FDA. | BX7, QS33, QS35 |
| The QMSR uses the ISO 9000 term "top management"; FDA expects leaders with executive responsibilities to embrace a culture of quality. | The forum's membership is described by the person in their own terms; the skill records who attends, as the person names them. | BX13, QS36 |
| FDA says the old explicit duty to feed quality problems and CAPA into management review is still covered through ISO 13485 clauses 8.2.2, 8.5.2 and 8.3.1. | Complaint, CAPA and nonconformity data are inputs the person should decide how to bring to the review. | QS19 |

## Inputs a governance forum can read, and where each comes from

The person decides which inputs each forum reads. This table lists inputs that a sourced row ties to a process, so the person can see what each one answers.

| Input | Process (A1 map) | Why it is tied to governance | Rows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complaints and their investigation status | P11 | Complaint investigation information is one of the routes FDA names into management review | QS19, BX8 |
| Reportable events and reporting timeliness (US 30 days or 5 work days; EU 15, 10 or 2 days) | P12 | Reporting clocks run from awareness; late reports are a process failure | VG4 to VG6, VG17, VG18 |
| Corrections and removals, including non-reported ones with their justification | P12 | Non-reported corrections need a justification reviewed by a designated person | VG22, VG24 |
| CAPA: open actions, depth proportional to risk, effectiveness | P15 | CAPA depth must match the problem and the risk; the MDR requires verifying effectiveness | QS17, QS18, EU4 |
| Internal and supplier audit results | P14, P8 | Inspectable records; FDA calls the programs fundamental | QS15, QS16 |
| Risk file updates from new information | P5 | Risk documentation is reviewed when unforeseen risks or new information emerge | QS22, EU15 |
| Post-market surveillance and trend data | P13 | Trend reporting method and observation period belong in the PMS plan | VG9 |
| Changes (QMS, software, product) and changes in harmonized standards | P10 | Changes must be assessed for risk before implementation; standards changes taken into account in a timely manner | QS34, EU2 |
| Notified body or MDSAP audit findings and their grades | P14 | MDSAP grades 1 to 5; a repeat finding within three years signals an ineffective correction | QS44, QS47 |

## External audit and inspection rhythm (facts the cadence must fit around)

| Fact | Rows |
|---|---|
| EU: notified body audits at least every 12 months; unannounced on-site audits at least once every five years | EU25 |
| EU: plans for substantial QMS changes go to the notified body before they take effect | EU24 |
| MDSAP: three-year cycle with initial audit, partial surveillance audits in the next two years and a recertification audit; special and unannounced audits possible | QS42 |
| MDSAP: the auditing organization informs regulators within 5 working days of a grade 5 or more than two grade 4 nonconformities; full report by day 45 in that case, otherwise day 90 | QS48, QS49 |
| FDA: QMSR inspections are risk-scheduled and do not follow the MDSAP audit plan | QS41 |

These are external clocks. They are facts to plan around, not targets for the person's own cadence.
