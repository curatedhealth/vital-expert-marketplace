# Audit findings: structure, MDSAP grading, and the QMSR inspection approach

Interpretive reference for task A1. Every rule cites its row in `quality-systems-sources.md` (QS, EU) or `primary-text-additions.md` (R). ISO 13485 clause text is paywalled and is not stated here; clause numbers appear only where a regulator's document names them.

## 1. What a finding has to hold

A finding is three things, in this order, each traceable:

| Part | What it states | Where the requirement comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Requirement | The exact requirement, cited by its source: a CFR section, an MDR article, an ISO 13485 clause number as a regulator names it, or the person's own procedure (number and revision) | QS4 to QS11 (US), EU1 to EU4 (EU), the person's QMS |
| Evidence | What was seen: record ids, dates, versions, sample size, who was interviewed. Facts only, no judgment words | The audit itself |
| Gap | The difference between the two, in one or two sentences | The auditor, confirmed by the person |

Write findings as if a regulator will read them. Under the QMSR, FDA may inspect internal audit, supplier audit and management review reports; the old 820.180(c) exception is gone (QS15). FDA calls robust internal and supplier audit programs fundamental to the culture of quality it expects (QS16).

A complaint is not an audit finding. Record it through task A2 (the old skill's rule, kept as a method rule of the VITAL register).

## 2. Grading: MDSAP scale (when the person's procedure adopts it)

The 1 to 5 scale belongs to MDSAP auditing organizations (QS44, MDSAP AU P0037.001, effective 8 September 2021, fda.gov copy). An internal audit uses it only if the person's procedure says so; otherwise the person's own classification rule applies, written down before grading. The model never chooses the grade; it computes a proposed grade from the person's answers to four questions.

| Criterion | Value | Row |
|---|---|---|
| QMS impact | Direct 3 or indirect 1 | QS45 |
| Repeat nonconformity | Yes 1, no 0 | QS45 |
| No documented procedure and failure to implement, combined | Yes 1, no 0 | QS45 |
| Release of nonconforming devices | Yes 1, no 0 | QS45 |

Rules for each criterion:

- **Direct or indirect.** ISO 13485 clauses 4.1 to 6.3 are indirect, except 4.2.3 (medical device file), which is direct. Clauses 6.4 to 8.5 are direct, except 8.2.4 (internal audits), which is indirect (QS46).
- **Repeat.** Found in audits within the previous three years (QS47), looking at the two previous QMS audits that evaluated the same sub-clause (R3). A repeat signals that a corrective action was not adequately taken (QS47). No new repeat finding while accepted, timetabled actions are being implemented; a finding for an ineffective corrective action system may be raised when new occurrences show the actions do not work (R3).
- **No procedure plus no implementation.** May be applied when the documented procedure entirely fails to address the topic, or only references the regulation, and implementation also failed; not when a procedure addresses the topic but incompletely or lacking detail (R4). Where a procedure exists but has no step at all for the element found missing, the call is the auditor's: show the grade both ways and ask.
- **Release.** Not applied when the device was released under concession with adequate technical and scientific justification (R5).
- **Ceiling.** The sum can reach 6; the final grade is 1 to 5, with 5 the highest (R1).
- **Fixes do not lower the grade.** Corrections or corrective actions do not change a grade; only the auditing organization's appeals process can (R2).

## 3. When a grade triggers notice to regulators

For MDSAP audits, the auditing organization (not the manufacturer) must inform the regulatory authorities within 5 working days when the audit team finds one or more grade 5, or more than two grade 4, nonconformities (QS48, MDSAP AU P0027.007, effective 21 November 2022). The full audit report package is then due before day 45 after the audit; in all other cases before day 90 (QS49). For an internal audit these rules do not apply; say so rather than borrowing them.

The fda.gov copies of P0002, P0027 and P0037 may have been superseded on the MDSAP site now managed by Australia's TGA, which was not reachable (QS39, research "Changes and conflicts"). Say "as of the fda.gov copy" when quoting them.

## 4. How FDA inspects under the QMSR (context for audit scope)

- From 2 February 2026 the QMSR applies and FDA inspects under Compliance Program 7382.850; QSIT is retired (QS1, QS28, QS29).
- The program groups requirements into 6 QMS areas and 4 other applicable FDA requirements (QS30): Change Control; Design and Development; Management Oversight; Measurement, Analysis, and Improvement; Outsourcing and Purchasing; Production and Service Provision; plus Medical Device Reporting, Reports of Corrections and Removals, Medical Device Tracking, and UDI (R6).
- Investigators use the manufacturer's risk management documentation throughout the inspection (QS31).
- Model 1 evaluates at least one element in each area and requirement (QS32). Model 2 sets minimum elements, including complaint handling, internal audits and corrective action (QS33). Baseline surveillance without history uses model 2; for-cause inspections use model 1 (R7, check the PDF figure).
- Complaint handling sits in Measurement, Analysis, and Improvement and maps to 21 CFR 820.10(b)(3)(4), 820.35 and ISO 13485 clauses 8.2.2 and 8.2.3; internal audits map to clause 8.2.4 (R8).
- FDA does not accept an ISO 13485 certificate in place of inspection (QS25). FDA may accept MDSAP audit reports in place of routine surveillance inspections; non-surveillance inspections still apply (QS40). QMSR inspections do not follow the MDSAP audit plan (QS41).
- Records made before 2 February 2026 can be reviewed; FDA suggests a comparative analysis showing they meet the QMSR (QS26).

Use this to scope an internal audit that prepares for an FDA inspection: one or more elements per area, with complaint handling, internal audits and corrective action always in scope. It is not a forecast of what an investigator will select.

## 5. EU notified body audits (context)

Notified bodies audit at least every 12 months and perform unannounced on-site audits at least once every five years (EU25). The MDR QMS must include CAPA with verification of effectiveness and vigilance reporting processes (EU4). A notified body's own nonconformity classification scheme is not in the sourced rows `[GAP]`; ask the person for their notified body's rule.
