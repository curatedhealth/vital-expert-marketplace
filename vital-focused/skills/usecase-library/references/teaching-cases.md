# Teaching cases (fictional)

> **Teaching material, never input (DC-H2).** Every company, product, person and number below is invented to show the shape of a FOCUSED walk. Never copy them into a live answer or treat them as the person's decision. These are not the gold use-case cards; those come with the connector.

## Contents
- Case A: competitive launch timing
- Case B: choosing a vendor or agency
- Case C: entering a new market

How to use: pick the case closest to what the person asked, say it is a fictional teaching example, walk the seven steps in plain words (one line per step: the coach's question, then the fictional answer), and end by asking whether they want to walk their own decision. Never draw a conclusion for the person's case.

## Case A: competitive launch timing

Fictional setting: "Norvale Pharma" must decide whether to launch its asthma inhaler "Brisa" before a rival's expected launch.

1. **Frame.** Coach: "What exactly are you deciding, who decides, and by when?" Fictional answer: launch in May or wait for September; the country head, Lena Ortiz, decides by 1 March.
2. **Options.** Coach: "What else could you do, including doing nothing?" Fictional answer: launch in May; wait for September; launch in two pilot regions in May; do nothing until the rival launches.
3. **Criteria.** Coach: "What matters most, and how much?" Fictional answer: first-to-formulary 40, supply security 35, launch cost 25.
4. **Unknowns.** Coach: "What do you know, and what is still unknown?" Fictional answer: known, the packaging line is ready; unknown, the rival's real date.
5. **Score.** Coach: "On your own ratings, how do the options add up, and does the order hold if one priority moves by 10 points?" Fictional result: the pilot-region option has the highest total and holds under a 10-point shift.
6. **Examine.** Coach, one turn each: "How does that sit with your gut?" then "Imagine it failed in a year: what went wrong, and what is the first sign you would see?" then "Who sees it differently?" Fictional answers: gut agrees; failure is a supply gap, first sign is a missed batch release; the supply lead disagrees.
7. **Decide.** Coach: "Which option do you choose?" then "Who owns it and what is the first step?" then "When will you look again?" Fictional answer: Lena chooses the pilot regions, 70% confident; the supply lead owns batch planning; review on 15 June.

## Case B: choosing a vendor or agency

Fictional setting: "Kestrel Medical" chooses a market access agency.

1. **Frame.** "What job must the agency do for you, who decides, and by when?" Fictional answer: a reimbursement dossier in nine months; the access director decides by month end.
2. **Options.** "What else could you do, including doing nothing?" Fictional answer: Agency 1, Agency 2, build the dossier in-house, delay the dossier a year.
3. **Criteria.** "What matters most, and how much?" Fictional answer: dossier track record 50, team continuity 30, price 20 (lower price is better).
4. **Unknowns.** "What is still unknown?" Fictional answer: whether Agency 2's named lead stays on the account.
5. **Score.** "On your ratings, how do they add up, and does it hold?" Fictional result: Agency 1 is highest, but a 10-point shift toward price changes the order.
6. **Examine.** "Your own numbers flip with a small shift toward price. What do they miss?" Fictional answer: price matters more than the weights said.
7. **Decide.** "Which do you choose?" Fictional answer: the access director chooses Agency 1 with a continuity clause; review at month three.

## Case C: entering a new market

Fictional setting: "Halden Diagnostics" weighs entering one new country next year.

1. **Frame.** "What are you deciding, what is the goal, who decides, by when?" Fictional answer: one country for next year, to reach a revenue goal the board set; the CEO decides in Q4.
2. **Options.** "What else could you do, including doing nothing?" Fictional answer: Country X, Country Y, a distributor in both, no new country.
3. **Criteria.** "What matters most?" Fictional answer: time to reimbursement, partner strength, cost.
4. **Unknowns.** "What fact would change your mind?" Fictional answer: the reimbursement timeline in Country X, still unknown; the team agrees to get it from a cited source before scoring.
5. **Score.** Done only after that fact is in.
6. **Examine.** "Who sees it differently?" Fictional answer: the CFO prefers the distributor route.
7. **Decide.** The CEO decides; the CFO's view is recorded in her own words.
