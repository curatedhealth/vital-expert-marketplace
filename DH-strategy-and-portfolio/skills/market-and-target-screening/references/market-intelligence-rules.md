# Market intelligence: how to build an ecosystem map from records

Source: the old DH-01.05.2.1 file "Healthcare Ecosystem Analysis & Market Intelligence Generation" (task sections "Ecosystem Census & Mapping", "Competitive Intelligence Analysis", "Market Sizing & Segmentation"), the SP rows in `strategy-and-portfolio-research.md`, the DL rows in `diligence-research.md`, the DE rows in `hta-and-directory-research.md`, and saved library outputs of 19 October 2026 (`examples/market-intelligence/`, `examples/live/`). Used by task A3 Market intelligence map. Every rule names its row or its saved output.

## 1. What the map is, and what it is not

The ecosystem map lays out, for one market (a product type, an indication if the person gives one, and the jurisdictions the person names), four lanes: **buyers** (who decides to adopt and contracts), **payers** (who reimburses or funds), **peers** (companies and products on record in the same segment), and **rules** (the dated instruments that govern market entry, data and deals). Each cell is a sourced record with its date, or a gap.

The old file's guardrails carry over verbatim in sense: "Draft the ecosystem census for {product} from actors the operator already named", "Do not invent share", "Do not invent a market size, competitor share, or build-partner-buy decision" (old DH-01.05.2.1.1 prompt). The map is not a market model:

- No market size, share, growth rate, revenue or adoption figure from the model. No source in this skill holds one (DL "Not sourced"; SP "Not sourced": "Benchmarks such as portfolio size, hit rates by digital health company or innovation maturity targets: no primary source sets them"). A figure the person brings is labeled "from your material" with its own source.
- No segment choice. Segment labels and which lanes matter are the person's. The old task "Market Sizing & Segmentation" folds here only as segmentation of the record (which product types, jurisdictions and payer schemes the records fall into); sizing is declined in one line.
- Competitive intelligence is the peers lane: what each company's record states (authorizations, listings, studies, filings), never its strategy, revenue, pipeline intent or outcomes. Positioning one's own product against them is `dh-go-to-market` market-segmentation-and-gtm-strategy.

## 2. A shared language for the interventions on the map

Where the person wants products grouped by what they do, use the WHO classification, labeled as WHO's:

- WHO "Classification of digital interventions, services and applications in health", second edition (2023, ISBN 978-92-4-008194-9) is anchored on the "digital health intervention", "a discrete capability of digital technology to achieve health sector objectives" (SP-054, SP-055).
- It has three axes: health system challenges, digital health interventions, and digital services and application types (SP-056). The 2023 edition renamed "Systems Categories" to "Services and Application types" (SP-057); the 2018 first edition is superseded (SP "Changes and conflicts").
- Assigning a product to a WHO category is the model's reading of the product's record: label it "proposed" and quote the record it rests on.

## 4. Reading rules for the map

1. One cell, one record: each entry gives the record, its source named in plain words with its date and then the row id (or the library tool with its save date), and its date. A lane with no record is `[GAP]` classed per `gap-rules.md`, never "no payers" or "no competitors".
2. The person's actors stay theirs: actors the person names (buyers, partners, payers they know) go in with "from your material" and are checked against the record only where a tool or row can check them.
3. Coverage first: every lane carries one line on what its source cannot see (column 4 above). A map whose payer lane rests on a "partial" scheme says so.
4. Status and holder: a company on the peers lane gets its status from `named-company-status.md` where a row covers it (for example Pear Therapeutics: chapter 11, Pear 8-K, 7 April 2023, SP-156 [WF]; Nasdaq delisting notice, Pear 8-K, 10 April 2023, SP-160 [WF]), with what each form does not mean; otherwise "Status: [GAP]: not read" per `screening-rules.md` section 5. Beside each product's holder name as stored, add the current FDA listing holder where a row gives it (Somryst: Nox Medical, listing created 18 December 2024, SP-167). Where no row gives it, add: "holder as stored by the applicant; a parent or predecessor name can hide a later owner (DL-133 (inference))", and offer the filings to open for that company (DL-135 to DL-168) in the same line. Never take a holder or an acquisition from press.
5. The map is dated: "as of" the retrieval date of each tool output; rules carry instrument and date. Market intelligence quality over time is checked in `pipeline-and-market-monitoring` A3 (this pack).
6. The Payers, DE row always carries the DE34 counts with "as of 9 September 2026" and the note that they cover the whole directory; never cut it for length. Cut rules rows first.
7. Rules lane coverage: if the person's question concerns a device product, add one line routing device status and pathway to `dh-regulatory-and-quality` regulatory-pathway-and-classification (section 3, last row). A rules lane that holds only EHDS and deal rows says so in its coverage line.
8. Sources are named so a reader can open them: "Pear 8-K, 7 April 2023 (SP-156 [WF])", "BfArM DiGA page, as of 9 September 2026 (DE34)", "Federal Register, 9 December 2024 (US7)", "VITAL library screen_commercial_peers, saved 19 October 2026"; never a row id alone.
