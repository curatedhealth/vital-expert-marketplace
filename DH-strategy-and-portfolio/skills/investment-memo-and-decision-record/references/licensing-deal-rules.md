# Licensing deal facts a memo flags (EU TTBER 2026/877, DOJ/FTC IP licensing guidelines, SEC material contracts)

Source: rows SP-130 to SP-147 in `strategy-and-portfolio-research.md` (copied unchanged; read 20 October 2026, primary sources: EUR-Lex via publications.europa.eu, the European Commission competition site, justice.gov, eCFR) and DL-019 to DL-021 in `diligence-research.md`. Used by task A2 step 5b when the deal includes a technology license (in-licensing, out-licensing, a co-development with licensed rights, or a data license).

**Every line here is a fact for the owner the person names for legal or competition questions, or owner not named.** Whether parties are competitors, how a relevant market is defined, what the parties' shares are, and whether an agreement is exempt, inside a safety zone or lawful are that owner's conclusions. The skill never writes "this license is exempt", "safe", "inside the safety zone" or "compliant", and never supplies a market definition or a market share. No row in this file is [PAPER] or [WF].

## 1. EU: the Technology Transfer Block Exemption Regulation

| Fact | Rows |
|---|---|
| Regulation (EU) No 316/2014 expired on 30 April 2026. Any text that cites it as current is out of date. | SP-134; SP "Changes and conflicts" |
| Commission Regulation (EU) 2026/877 of 16 April 2026 entered into force on 1 May 2026 and expires on 30 April 2038. | SP-135 |
| Market share conditions: between competitors, the combined market share of the parties must not exceed 20% "on any relevant market"; between non-competitors, the market share of each party must not exceed 30% "on any relevant market". The research file notes the thresholds are unchanged from 2014, whose wording was "on the relevant market(s)". | SP-136; SP "Changes and conflicts" |
| Transition: the Article 101(1) prohibition does not apply from 1 May 2026 until 30 April 2027 to agreements already in force on 30 April 2026 that met the old rules but not the new ones. | SP-137 |
| Technology rights include supplementary protection certificates for medicinal products. | SP-138 |
| Between competitors, restricting a party's own research and development is a hardcore restriction unless indispensable to prevent disclosure of licensed know-how to third parties. | SP-139 |
| The regulation does not cover agreements setting up technology pools. | SP-140 |
| New Guidelines on technology transfer agreements (C/2026/2323) were published in the Official Journal on 21 April 2026 and replace the 2014 Guidelines. | SP-141 |
| Data licensing: the 2026 review confirms the TTBER can apply to data licensing where the licensed data qualifies as one of the existing technology rights, such as production know-how (Commission explanatory note of 16 April 2026). Whether a given data set qualifies is not stated by the source. | SP-142 |

## 2. US: Antitrust Guidelines for the Licensing of Intellectual Property (DOJ and FTC)

| Fact | Rows |
|---|---|
| Issued 12 January 2017; listed on the DOJ page with no withdrawal notice as read on 20 October 2026. | SP-147 |
| Three principles: intellectual property is analyzed like other property; the agencies do not presume that intellectual property creates market power; licensing is generally procompetitive. | SP-143 |
| Safety zone: absent extraordinary circumstances, the agencies will not challenge a restraint that is not facially anticompetitive where the licensor and its licensees collectively account for no more than 20% of each relevant market significantly affected. | SP-144 |
| Research and development markets: the safety zone needs four or more independently controlled entities, besides the parties, with the assets and incentive to do close-substitute research and development. | SP-145 |
| Outside the safety zone is not a presumption of harm: "it is likely that the great majority of licenses falling outside the safety zone are lawful and procompetitive." | SP-146 |

## 3. Disclosure of a license by a listed party

| Fact | Rows |
|---|---|
| A registrant files as an exhibit every material contract not made in the ordinary course that is still to be performed. | SP-130 |
| Even an ordinary-course contract is filed if the business depends on it to a material extent, including a license to use a patent, formula, trade secret, process or trade name. | SP-131 |
| Specific terms may be redacted if not material and customarily treated as private; the exhibit is marked and the SEC may ask for an unredacted copy. A redacted exhibit is not the full term sheet. | SP-132 |
| A material contract executed in a period is filed with that period's 10-Q or 10-K. | SP-133 |
| A material definitive agreement outside the ordinary course is reported on Form 8-K Item 1.01, generally within four business days. | DL-019 to DL-021 |

These tell the person where a counterparty's license terms may be found in the public record (exhibits, 8-K Item 1.01) and what a redacted exhibit does not show. They are not a disclosure decision for the person's own company: that is the owner the person names.

## 4. What the person must give for the licensing flags

Territory (EU, US, other); the technology rights licensed (patent, know-how, software copyright, SPC, data) as the agreement states them; exclusive or non-exclusive; whether the person's legal owner treats the parties as competitors (the skill does not decide); the relevant markets and each party's share as the person's legal owner defines them, with their source (the skill never supplies one); whether the agreement was in force on 30 April 2026 and, if so, whether it was assessed under Regulation 316/2014; any restriction on either party's research and development; whether it sets up or joins a technology pool; whether a party is an SEC registrant. Anything not given is "not given" and is asked for.
