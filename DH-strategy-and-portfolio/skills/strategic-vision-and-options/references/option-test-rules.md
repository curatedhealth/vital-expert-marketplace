# Option tests: prototypes, experiments and early regulated studies

Source: `strategy-and-portfolio-research.md` (SP rows, read 20 October 2026), `product-lifecycle-research.md` (PL rows, read 20 October 2026), `ai-ml-regulation-research.md` (UK-06 to UK-08, read 19 October 2026) and `customer-success-research.md` (CS rows, read 19 October 2026). Interpretive reference for A2. Every line carries its row. A fact not in a row is `[GAP]`.

An option test answers one question the person has about an option before they commit to it. The person writes the question, the success criterion and the decision rule; the model checks that each exists before the test starts and lays out the routes the sources describe.

## 1. The elements every test plan carries (A2 step 2)

No source in this skill sets a general standard for business experiments. The checklist below borrows FDA's words for early feasibility device studies and IDEAL-D's ordering; say so when applying it to a non-device test.

| Element | Source wording | Row |
|---|---|---|
| A specified purpose | Early feasibility studies "should be conducted for specified purpose(s), enroll the appropriate subjects, utilize meaningful endpoints, and capture relevant information" | SP-115 (FDA, for device EFS) |
| Not powered for proof | EFS "are not designed or intended to generate statistically valid results" | SP-115 |
| Development and evaluation together, in order | IDEAL (idea, development, exploration, assessment, long-term follow-up): "development and evaluation can and should proceed together in an ordered and logical manner" | PL111 [PAPER], DOI 10.1097/SLA.0000000000004907 |
| A preclinical stage before first use in people | IDEAL-D adds stage 0, analogous to phase 0 drug trials | PL112 [PAPER], DOI 10.1097/SLA.0000000000004907 |
| Device status framing | IDEAL-D 2016: regulation splits device status into pre-market and post-market | PL121 (abstract only) [WF] |
| Plan the evidence early | Plan evidence that supports the intended purpose and value proposition as early as possible; one study can show safe use and capture time savings | SP-127, SP-128 (NHS AI and Digital Regulations Service, England) |

The success criterion, the decision rule (continue, change, stop), the sample and the date the rule was written are the person's. If any is missing, write "not given" and ask; never propose one unless asked, and then label it "proposed".

## 2. Regulated routes for early clinical tests (A2 step 3)

Cite only the routes for the market in the request.

| Route | What the source states | Row |
|---|---|---|
| FDA early feasibility study (US) | Final guidance October 2013; a limited clinical investigation early in development, typically before design freeze, generally fewer than 10 initial subjects; fits when device changes are expected and the information cannot practically come from nonclinical tests; FDA does not mandate EFS | SP-109 to SP-111 |
| Traditional feasibility and pivotal studies | Near-final design to plan a pivotal study; pivotal collects definitive safety and effectiveness evidence | SP-112 |
| EFS uses | Identify device or procedure changes, refine the intended use population, refine nonclinical tests | SP-113 |
| EFS starting data and change mechanisms | Less nonclinical data is generally needed to start an EFS than a larger study; broader changes under 5-day notice, contingent approval, interactive review | SP-114, SP-116 |
| QMSR note | The EFS guidance predates the QMSR (effective 2 February 2026); FDA notes QMSR terms differ | SP-118 |
| FDA EFS Program steps | Contact the EFS Program, then a Pre-Submission before the IDE; open to PMA, 510(k), De Novo and HDE devices | SP-119, SP-120 |
| FDA TAP pilot | Early, frequent, strategic FDA communication; from 1 July 2026 open across all OHTs for Breakthrough and STeP devices; 159 devices enrolled as of 7 October 2026 (a count that changes; quote with its date) | SP-121, SP-122 |
| CMS and FDA RAPID | Medicare coverage pathway for Breakthrough devices announced 23 April 2026; coverage is `dh-access-and-value` coverage-pathway-and-access-strategy | SP-123 |
| MHRA IDAP (Great Britain) | A pilot for innovative devices meeting an unmet NHS need; eight applicants selected; closed to new applications; the future IDAP is not yet defined on the page read | SP-124 |
| IDAP Unmet Clinical Need Authorisation | Lets non-UKCA or non-CE devices onto the GB market for a set period with conditions; needs data that the product is safe and potentially effective and evidence the maker is working toward approval | SP-125, SP-126 |
| MHRA AI Airlock (UK) | A regulatory sandbox for AI-enabled medical devices launched spring 2024; phase 3 announced 6 October 2026; phase 2 case-study reports are not MHRA guidance | UK-06 to UK-08 |
| EMA and MHRA innovation offices | EMA points developers to Innovation Task Force meetings, scientific advice and qualification; the MHRA Innovation Office takes queries on products that challenge the framework and is not a substitute for Scientific Advice | SP-020, SP-025, SP-026 |

- FDA has no program called a sandbox; TAP and the EFS Program are the nearest (research file, "Not sourced").
- Whether the product is a device, its class and pathway: `dh-regulatory-and-quality` regulatory-pathway-and-classification. The developer decides device status (SP-129).
- A clinical study protocol, endpoints and statistics: `dh-clinical-development` study-protocol-and-endpoint-design.

## 4. Reading a finished test (A2 step 5)

- Report the result against the rule the person wrote before the test started, in their words. A rule written after the start is labeled so.
- An early study that was never designed to give statistically valid results (SP-115) does not prove an option works; say what it taught in the words of the person's question.
- Whether the option continues, changes or stops is the person's. Monitoring of tests across options is `strategy-execution-monitoring` (this pack).
