# Channel rules a go-to-market motion must respect

Interpretive reference for task B1. Every rule cites a CP row (`channels-partners-launch-sources.md`) or PC row (`promotion-and-claims-sources.md`), read 19 October 2026, not owner-reviewed. These rows state what the law and regulators say. They are not legal advice, and the model never says an arrangement is protected: any arrangement listed under "Stops" goes to the person's compliance or legal owner with the elements laid out.

## United States: paying anyone in the channel

| Rule | Row | What it means for a motion |
|---|---|---|
| Offering or paying anything of value to induce referrals or purchases of items paid by a Federal health care program is a felony; the receiving side commits the same offense | CP-US-01, CP-US-02 | Reseller commissions, referral fees and free services to prescribers are inside the statute's reach when Medicare or Medicaid pays (for example DMHT or RPM segments) |
| No knowledge of the statute or specific intent is needed | CP-US-03 | "We did not mean it" is no defense |
| OIG applies the statute where one purpose of a payment is to induce referrals | CP-US-08 | A mixed-purpose payment is still in scope |
| Safe harbor protection applies only where every condition is met precisely | CP-US-07 | List every element and say which are unknown; never call it met |
| Agent or reseller pay: written and signed, at least one year, compensation set in advance at fair market value, not by volume or value of referrals | CP-US-09, CP-US-10 | A percentage-of-sales reseller deal to prescribers fails the volume test as written |
| Outcomes-based payments to an agent: device manufacturers and distributors are excluded from the protection | CP-US-11 to CP-US-14 | An outcomes bonus paid by a device maker to a partner gets no safe harbor |
| Patient engagement tools from device makers: only digital health technology, in kind, recommended by the patient's clinician, cap $623 per patient for calendar year 2026, no marketing or recruitment use | CP-US-25 to CP-US-28 | Free devices or apps to patients as a launch tactic stop for compliance |
| Offering anything of value to a Medicare or state-program beneficiary likely to influence the choice of provider or supplier is subject to civil money penalties | CP-US-34, CP-US-35 | Free trials or gifts to Medicare patients |
| Advisory opinions bind only their requestor | CP-US-43 | AO 19-02 (loaned phones) and AO 26-15 (pay-to-receive referral software) show OIG reasoning; they protect no one else (CP-US-39 to CP-US-42, CP-US-33) |

## United States: contracting and data in each channel

| Channel | Rule | Row |
|---|---|---|
| Health system or practice | BAA when the vendor handles PHI: permitted uses, safeguards, breach reporting, subcontractor flow-down, termination right | CP-US-44 to CP-US-48 |
| Health system or practice | Business associate breach notice no later than 60 calendar days after discovery | CP-US-49 |
| Health system data exchange | Information blocking reaches a vendor only with certified health IT modules | CP-US-50, CP-US-51 |
| Direct to consumer | FTC Health Breach Notification Rule; unauthorized disclosure is a breach; 60-day notice | CP-US-54 to CP-US-56 |
| Any US launch | Establishment registration within 30 days of starting the operation, device listing at that time; registration is never approval in marketing | CP-US-71, CP-US-72, CP-US-74 |
| Public partner | A material definitive agreement is disclosed on Form 8-K within four business days; a dependent partner may have to file the contract | CP-US-79 to CP-US-83 |

## Germany: DiGA channel

| Rule | Row |
|---|---|
| Contract physicians may not steer DiGA prescriptions or make deals with manufacturers about allocating or transmitting them | CP-DE-06 |
| DiGA manufacturers may not make agreements with drug or aid makers that could restrict patient choice or therapeutic freedom | CP-DE-07 |
| Delivery is electronic or on data carriers; app stores only where that is not possible; needed hardware is usually lent | CP-DE-05 |
| A DiGA must be free of advertising; DiGA data may not be used for advertising | PC-DE-15, PC-DE-16 |
| Device advertising outside professional circles follows stricter HWG rules | PC-DE-02, PC-DE-09 |

## England: NHS buying

DTAC review in procurement (CP-UK-01 to CP-UK-04); DCB0129 duties on the developer and DCB0160 on the adopter (CP-UK-06 to CP-UK-10); frameworks and the Dynamic Purchasing System (CP-UK-12 to CP-UK-14). CP-UK-11 rests on an official service blog, not the standard: say so if used.

## European Union

Processor contracts with hospital controllers (CP-EU-01 to CP-EU-09); EUDAMED actor and device registration before placing on the market (CP-EU-10 to CP-EU-16).

## Industry codes, not law

AdvaMed (US) and MedTech Europe codes on consulting, product training, evaluation and demonstration products (PC-US-89 to PC-US-96, PC-EU-13 to PC-EU-18) are [INDUSTRY]. Say so whenever one shapes a motion.

## Stops (for the person's compliance or legal owner)

- Any free item, service or device to patients or providers.
- Any payment to a partner, reseller, consultant or clinician that is tied to referrals, volume or outcomes.
- Any agreement with a drug or aid maker around a DiGA.
- Any partner agreement that may be material to a public company.

For each, lay out the elements from the rows above, mark which the facts meet, miss or leave `[GAP]`, label the page "draft for the owner", and stop.
