# Claim rules for a launch claim list

Interpretation of rows in `promotion-and-claims-research.md` (PC rows, read 19 October 2026). Each rule names its row; the row holds the URL and quote. These rules let the model flag a claim and draft safer wording for the owner. They never clear a claim: FDA itself recommends review by qualified medical or scientific, legal and regulatory personnel before a promotional communication goes out (PC-US-33). Rows marked [INDUSTRY] are codes, not law; rows marked [WF] were read through a summarizing fetch tool and must be re-checked on the page before being quoted as verbatim.

Apply the rows of the market the material is for. For US material, cite PC-US and CP-US rows (for example CP-US-74, PC-US-72 [WF], PC-US-31). Cite PC-EU, PC-DE or PC-UK rows only for material used in those markets, or say they are cited by analogy.

## 1. Marketing copy sets what the product is

- US: intended use is the firm's objective intent, shown by labeling claims, advertising and oral or written statements (PC-US-17); FDA applies this to apps, so the same function can be a device or not depending on how it is promoted (PC-US-20). Knowledge that clinicians use a device off-label does not by itself create a new intended use (PC-US-18).
- EU: intended purpose includes promotional or sales materials and statements (PC-EU-03); software for a listed medical purpose is a device (PC-EU-04).
- UK: medical purpose is set by labeling, instructions and promotional materials, including adverts, app store description and category, landing page and social media (PC-UK-01); "not a medical device" disclaimers fail if medical claims appear elsewhere (PC-UK-02); testimonials repeated in product literature count as the maker's claims (PC-UK-03).
- Wellness (US): a product keeps general wellness status only if labeling, advertising, UI and functionality name no disease or diagnostic threshold, give no clinical alerts or treatment guidance, and claim no clinical grade, accuracy or equivalence (PC-US-63, PC-US-65). Disease references only as "may help reduce the risk of" or "may help living well with" a chronic disease (PC-US-61). Wellness status is not a safety or effectiveness claim (PC-US-66).

Consequence for the claim list: a claim that changes indication, population or use is not a wording issue; it is a regulatory status question for the person's regulatory owner.

## 2. Rules by market and audience

| Market | Audience | Rule | Rows |
|---|---|---|---|
| US | HCPs (promotion) | For 510(k)-cleared or exempt devices, a claim that would need a new 510(k) is inconsistent with labeling. For PMA and De Novo devices, three factors: no change to indication, population, limitations or regimen; no added potential for harm; directions in labeling enable the represented use. | PC-US-22 to PC-US-25 |
| US | HCPs | Head-to-head comparison for the same indication, subgroup data inside the cleared population, adherence and convenience can be consistent; use outside the cleared population is not. | PC-US-26, PC-US-27 |

| US | HCPs, unapproved uses | Scientific information on unapproved uses follows the SIUU guidance (final January 2025): separate from promotion, required disclosures. The final text says it is not for current implementation until the OMB notice; no notice was found. | PC-US-50 to PC-US-56 |

## 3. Flags the model applies to each claim

These are the skill's labels for the owner's review, not legal findings.

| Flag | When | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| On record | The claim restates the authorized indication and population as the person showed it (labeling, IFU, decision summary). | PC-US-22, PC-US-23 |
| Check against labeling | Within the authorized population but adds information (comparison, subgroup, adherence). | PC-US-26, PC-US-28 |
| Beyond the authorization | New indication, population, regimen or a use outside the intended purpose. Stop: regulatory owner. | PC-US-22, PC-US-27, PC-EU-02 |
| No authorization shown | Therapeutic or diagnostic wording with no authorization the person can show. Stop: regulatory owner. | PC-US-17, PC-EU-04, PC-UK-01 |
| Blacklist risk | Cure, guaranteed success, no side effects, or an approval or endorsement that is false or overstated (for example "FDA approved" for a 510(k), or registration as approval). | PC-EU-10, PC-EU-11, PC-UK-07, PC-UK-08, PC-DE-04, CP-US-74 |
| Needs evidence | No source given, or the source does not support the claim in the same direction. | PC-US-28, PC-US-30, PC-US-72 [WF] |
| Audience mismatch | Economic information aimed at HCPs or consumers; pre-clearance information outside payors; public advertising in Germany using professional-only content. | PC-US-35, PC-US-08, PC-DE-02 |
| Coverage or price statement | "Reimbursed", "covered", "free for patients", a price. Must match the current register record with its date; never present coverage as secured; payer matters go to Market Access. | PC-EU-07 |
| Discourages treatment | "Replaces therapy", "no need to see a doctor". | PC-UK-11 [INDUSTRY], PC-EU-02 |

## 4. Risk words (used by `scripts/claim_screen.py`)

| Words (case-insensitive) | Why flagged | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| cure, cures, cured | Blacklisted when false; unqualified cure not generally acceptable | PC-EU-10, PC-UK-07, PC-UK-13 [INDUSTRY] |
| guaranteed, guarantee, always works, 100% | Certain success | PC-DE-04, PC-UK-13 [INDUSTRY] |
| no side effects, risk-free, completely safe, absolutely safe | No harmful effects | PC-DE-04, PC-UK-13 [INDUSTRY] |
| FDA approved, FDA-approved, FDA registered | Registration is not approval; 510(k) clearance and De Novo are not approval | CP-US-74, PC-EU-11 |
| certified, endorsed, recommended by | Approval or endorsement claims must be true and complied with | PC-EU-11, PC-UK-08 |
| clinically proven | Device-indicating word; needs evidence | PC-UK-04, PC-UK-10 [INDUSTRY] |
| clinical grade, medical grade, clinically accurate | Takes a product out of wellness | PC-US-63 |
| diagnose, diagnoses, detects, treats, prevents | Device-indicating words; wellness disqualifiers | PC-UK-04, PC-US-63 |
| replaces, instead of your doctor, no need to see | Discourages essential treatment | PC-UK-11 [INDUSTRY] |
| reimbursed, covered by, free of charge, kostenlos | Coverage or price statement | PC-EU-07 |
| HIPAA compliant, HIPAA certified | A HIPAA-style seal was part of the GoodRx action [WF] | PC-US-78 |
| testimonial, "said", five stars | Testimonials are the maker's claims; misleading use banned in public German advertising | PC-UK-03, PC-DE-11 |
| free trial, subscription, auto-renew | Opaque offers barred for a DiGA | PC-DE-17 |

A word hit is a prompt to read the claim, not a finding. A claim without any hit can still be beyond the authorization.
