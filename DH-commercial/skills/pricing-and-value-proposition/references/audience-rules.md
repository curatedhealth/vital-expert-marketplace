# Audience rules for value messages

Interpretive reference for task B1 (and for the claims audit in the sibling monitoring skill). Every rule cites its row in `promotion-and-claims-research.md`. Read on 19 October 2026; not owner-reviewed. Rows marked [INDUSTRY] are self-regulatory codes, not law; rows marked [WF] were read through a summarizing tool and must be re-checked on the page before reliance.

## Rules for every audience and market

| Rule | Rows |
|---|---|
| Marketing copy sets intended use, and so regulatory status: claims, advertising and oral or written statements show the firm's objective intent (US); intended purpose includes promotional and sales materials (EU); medical purpose is set by labeling and promotion, including app store text and landing pages (GB) | PC-US-17, PC-US-20, PC-EU-03, PC-UK-01 |
| A message is misleading by what it omits as well as what it says; the overall presentation counts even where each statement is true | PC-US-03, PC-US-70, PC-EU-07, PC-EU-08, PC-UK-06 |
| No message may ascribe functions the device lacks, suggest uses outside the assessed intended purpose, or hide a likely risk (EU) | PC-EU-01, PC-EU-02 |
| False approval or endorsement claims ("FDA-cleared", "CE-certified", "DiGA-listed" when untrue or beyond the terms) are always unfair to consumers in the EU and UK | PC-EU-11, PC-UK-08 |
| Falsely claiming a product can cure, prevent or treat disease is always unfair to consumers | PC-EU-10, PC-UK-07 |
| Disclaimers do not rescue a medical claim made elsewhere (GB) and cannot contradict a claim (FTC) [WF] | PC-UK-02, PC-US-74 |
| Testimonials repeated in product literature are the manufacturer's own implied claims (GB) | PC-UK-03 |
| A listing, a code or a coverage decision is not evidence that the product works; uptake is use, not clinical effect | Governance rule (`governance.md`) |

## By audience

### Payor, formulary or technology assessment committee (United States)

| Rule | Rows |
|---|---|
| The payor audience is public and private payors, formulary and technology assessment committees, PBMs, TPAs and similar entities deciding on a population basis | PC-US-34 |
| Health care economic information (HCEI) is any analysis, including its clinical inputs and assumptions, of the economic consequences of use; it may compare with another product or none | PC-US-06 |
| HCEI to a payor is not false or misleading if it relates to a cleared or authorized indication, rests on competent and reliable scientific evidence, and states material differences from labeling conspicuously. Since Pub. L. 117-328, section 3630, this covers devices (510(k), De Novo, PMA) | PC-US-04, PC-US-11 |
| Analysis relating only to an uncleared indication is not HCEI and gets no safe harbor | PC-US-07 |
| Substantiation must be made available to FDA on request | PC-US-05 |
| HCEI should present study design, generalizability, limitations, sensitivity analyses and balanced information clearly and prominently; the evidence standard covers clinical inputs too | PC-US-38, PC-US-39, PC-US-40 |
| HCEI forms include a dossier, a reprint, a model with a user manual, a budget-impact model, slides or a payor brochure | PC-US-36 |
| Not for prescribers or consumers: the HCEI policy does not cover HCEI sent to HCPs making individual decisions or to consumers, for example on a public website | PC-US-35 |
| Device HCEI is not submitted to CDRH at dissemination | PC-US-49 |
| Guidance status: the June 2018 final is still the final guidance; the June 2026 revised draft implements section 3630 and replaces it once finalized (comments were due 3 August 2026) | PC-US-41, PC-US-47, PC-US-48 |

### Payor, before clearance or for an uncleared use (United States, 502(gg))

| Rule | Rows |
|---|---|
| Truthful, non-misleading product information about an investigational device or use may go to a payor-type entity | PC-US-08 |
| Required: a clear statement that it is not cleared or approved and that safety and effectiveness are not established; development stage; study design and limitations; updates | PC-US-08, PC-US-45 |
| Must not represent that it is cleared or approved, or found safe or effective | PC-US-09 |
| Permitted content: description and features, indications investigated, anticipated timeline, pricing, utilization projections, product-related programs, factual study results | PC-US-10, PC-US-43 |
| Factual results only, no conclusions on safety or effectiveness | PC-US-44 |
| For an uncleared use of a cleared product: state the cleared indication and attach current labeling | PC-US-46 |

### Clinician (United States)

| Rule | Rows |
|---|---|
| Labeling is false or misleading in any particular is misbranding | PC-US-01, PC-US-02 |
| Consistent with labeling: for 510(k) and exempt devices a claim that would need a new 510(k) is inconsistent; for PMA and De Novo the three factors apply (indication, population, use limitations, regimen; potential for harm; adequacy of directions) | PC-US-22, PC-US-23, PC-US-24 |
| Can be consistent: head-to-head comparison in the same indication, subgroups within the cleared population, adherence, convenience | PC-US-26 |
| Not consistent: use outside the cleared population | PC-US-27 |
| Evidence must be scientifically appropriate and statistically sound for the claim; approval-standard evidence is not required; disclosing limitations does not cure an inadequate study; disclose unfavorable or inconsistent findings | PC-US-28 to PC-US-31 |
| Review by qualified medical or scientific, legal and regulatory personnel before release is recommended | PC-US-33 |
| Scientific information on unapproved uses: only under the SIUU guidance, separate from promotion; the final January 2025 text is marked not for current implementation pending OMB action, and no OMB notice was found | PC-US-50 to PC-US-56 |
| Restricted device advertising carries the established name and a brief statement of intended uses and risks | PC-US-12 |

### Consumer or patient (United States)

| Rule | Rows |
|---|---|
| FDA oversees labeling for all devices but advertising only for restricted devices; other device advertising falls under the FTC Act | PC-US-13, PC-US-67, PC-US-69 |
| Advertisers need a reasonable basis before an ad runs; health benefit claims generally need randomized, controlled human clinical testing [WF] | PC-US-72, PC-US-73 |
| General wellness (revised final 6 January 2026): wellness claims may not reference specific diseases, diagnostic thresholds, clinical alerts, clinical grade or equivalence to an authorized device; chronic-disease references only as "may help reduce the risk of" or "may help living well with" where the link is generally accepted | PC-US-57 to PC-US-65 |
| Wellness status is not a safety or effectiveness claim | PC-US-66 |
| Health apps outside HIPAA fall under the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule; sharing health data with advertisers without authorization can be a breach | PC-US-79 to PC-US-84 |
| Covered entities need authorization to use PHI for marketing | PC-US-85 to PC-US-87 |

### European Union, Germany, United Kingdom

| Rule | Rows |
|---|---|
| EU consumers: unfair, misleading and blacklisted practices; judge from the vulnerable group's view where patients are targeted | PC-EU-05 to PC-EU-12 |
| Germany: HWG applies to device advertising; misleading advertising is a criminal offense; no promise of certain success or no harmful effects; study citations must name author, date and source and say whether the study concerns the advertised product | PC-DE-01, PC-DE-03, PC-DE-04, PC-DE-05, PC-DE-14 |
| Germany, outside professional circles (Fachkreise): no suggestion that health is impaired without the product or improved with it; no disguised ads or misleading testimonials; no ads aimed mainly at children under 14; no device advertising for listed diseases (notifiable infections, addictions except nicotine, pregnancy complications) | PC-DE-02, PC-DE-09 to PC-DE-13 |
| Germany, DiGA: free of advertising; data not used for advertising; no opaque offers | PC-DE-15 to PC-DE-17 |
| Germany: the HWG text read may not reflect the change by Art. 5 of the law of 26 June 2026 | Research file, Changes and conflicts |
| UK consumers: DMCCA 2024 sections 225 and 226 and Schedule 20 in force 6 April 2025 | PC-UK-05 to PC-UK-08 |
| UK ads [INDUSTRY]: CAP Code 12: objective claims need evidence, medical claims only with conformity marking, do not discourage essential treatment, no unqualified "cure" | PC-UK-09 to PC-UK-13 |
| UK ASA rulings [INDUSTRY]: a blood pressure app's color ranges and an ADHD "take the test" ad were medical claims needing device status; automated ad assets remain the advertiser's responsibility | PC-UK-14 to PC-UK-16 |

## Gaps that bind messages

- The UCPD text read is the 2005 original; later amendments (Directive (EU) 2019/2161) are not reflected. `[GAP]` for amended wording.
- No MHRA rule on device advertising comparable to the medicines Blue Guide was found; a search result, not a confirmed absence.
- German UWG and BGH case law on HWG for apps: not read.
- FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance: issue month not confirmed; FTC rows are [WF].
