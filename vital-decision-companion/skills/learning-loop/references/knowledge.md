# Rules for Learning loop

Pack: vital-decision-companion. Method version: 0.4.0. Content pack: 2026-10-29.1

- **R1.** The stage completes only when review_date, success_signal, and authored_by are all present.
- **R2.** authored_by is the person recording the plan. Never ai, model, assistant, or a role title.
- **R3.** Record the expected outcome and the person's probability before the outcome is known. This is the decision-journal rule: a forecast written later is hindsight.
- **R4.** A relative date such as next quarter is stored as said. Do not convert it to a day they did not choose.
- **R5.** At review, show what was expected before asking what happened.
- **R6.** Judge the process and the outcome separately. A bad outcome is not proof of a bad decision, and a good outcome is not proof of a good one. The person places the cell.
- **R7.** Repeated surprises across decisions are a reason to review the process, not to blame luck again.

Hard rules DC-H1 through DC-H8 in `SKILL.md` still apply.

# Patterns for Learning loop

Sources for the method: the Playbook Learning Loop (Part 11: decision journal, decision learning review, team review); outcome bias (Baron & Hershey 1988); after-action review (US Army TC 25-20); double-loop learning (Argyris 1991).

## Pattern 1

Person gives a review date and a success signal but no name. Ask whose name the plan is stored under. Store nothing until they answer.

## Pattern 2

Person says they are 80% sure the outcome will happen. Store probability 0.8 as their figure. Do not adjust it.

## Pattern 3

At review the outcome was poor. Person says it was a bad call. Show the stored assumptions first, then ask whether the process was sound given what was known.

## Pattern 4

Person wants to skip the review. If rigor is full process, the learning loop is required. Say so and ask for a review date instead.
