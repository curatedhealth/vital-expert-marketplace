# Positioning rules: what a positioning line may and may not say

Interpretive reference for positioning work (B2 in the segmentation skill; A1 and B1 in the positioning monitoring skill). Every rule cites a PC row (`promotion-and-claims-sources.md`) or CP row (`channels-partners-launch-sources.md`), read 19 October 2026, not owner-reviewed. These rules flag risk; they never clear a line. A line that makes a claim goes to the Commercial claims audit (DH-02.01.3.2) and stops for the person's regulatory or legal owner.

## Rule 1. The line is part of the intended use

What marketing says sets what the product is for, in every market read:

| Market | Rule | Row |
|---|---|---|
| United States | Intended use is shown by labeling claims, advertising and oral or written statements | PC-US-17 |
| United States | FDA applies this to apps: the same function can be a device or not depending on how it is promoted | PC-US-20 |
| European Union | "Intended purpose" includes promotional or sales materials or statements | PC-EU-03 |
| Great Britain | Medical purpose is set by labeling, instructions and promotional materials, including adverts, app store description and category, landing page and social media | PC-UK-01 |

Consequence for a line: it may not describe a use, population or result beyond the intended use the person states. If the intended use is not stated, the line is `[GAP]` until it is.

## Rule 2. Wellness lines stay wellness

| Rule | Row |
|---|---|
| A wellness claim names no disease: weight management, physical fitness, relaxation or stress, mental acuity, self-esteem, sleep management, sexual function | PC-US-59 |
| Treating or diagnosing anxiety disorders, eating disorders, obesity or autism is not wellness | PC-US-60 |
| A chronic disease may be referenced only as "may help reduce the risk of" or "may help living well with", where the link is generally accepted | PC-US-61 |
| References to specific diseases or diagnostic thresholds, clinical alerts, or "clinical grade", "clinical accuracy", or equivalence to an authorized device take a product out of wellness | PC-US-63 |
| Promotion must not exceed the stated wellness use; wellness status is not a safety or effectiveness claim | PC-US-65, PC-US-66 |
| The software exclusion for healthy-lifestyle software is lost with disease claims | PC-US-15 |

## Rule 3. Never claim an approval that does not exist or is not met

| Rule | Row |
|---|---|
| Representing that FDA establishment registration means approval is misbranding | CP-US-74 |
| Claiming approval, endorsement or authorization by a public or private body when untrue, or without complying with its terms, is always unfair (EU; covers "CE", "FDA-cleared", "DiGA-listed" wording) | PC-EU-11 |
| Same in the UK under the DMCCA 2024 | PC-UK-08 |
| A listing or a coverage decision is not clinical evidence (pack governance) | `governance.md` |

## Rule 4. No misleading effect, no guaranteed result

| Rule | Row |
|---|---|
| EU devices: no text, names, pictures or signs that mislead on intended purpose, safety or performance; no functions the device lacks; no uses outside the assessed intended purpose | PC-EU-01, PC-EU-02 |
| EU consumers: misleading by overall presentation even if factually correct | PC-EU-07 |
| EU consumers: misleading omission | PC-EU-08 |
| EU consumers: vulnerable groups judged by their average member | PC-EU-06 |
| Germany: no therapeutic effect the product lacks; no false impression of certain success or no harmful effects | PC-DE-03, PC-DE-04 |
| Germany, public advertising of devices: no suggestion that health suffers without, or improves with, the product; no unclear advertising purpose; no misleading testimonials | PC-DE-09 to PC-DE-11 |
| Germany, studies cited must say whether they concern the product itself, with author, date and source | PC-DE-05 |
| UK: overall presentation may deceive even when true; false prevent or treat claims are always unfair | PC-UK-06, PC-UK-07 |
| US: false or misleading in any particular; omission counts | PC-US-01, PC-US-03, PC-US-70 |
| US FTC: a reasonable basis must exist before the ad; health benefit claims generally need randomized controlled human testing; a disclaimer cannot contradict a claim | PC-US-72 to PC-US-74 [WF] |

## Rule 5. Comparisons and evidence

- A head-to-head comparison for the same indication can be consistent with FDA labeling; claims about patients outside the cleared population are not (PC-US-26, PC-US-27). Disclosing a weak study's limits does not fix a misleading message (PC-US-30).
- Economic information to payors has its own channel and rules and is not for prescribers or consumers (PC-US-04, PC-US-34, PC-US-35). Payer-facing value wording is Market Access and Commercial pricing work, not a positioning line.
- UK CAP Code: objective claims need evidence, if relevant trials on people; medical claims only with the applicable conformity marking (PC-UK-10 [INDUSTRY]).

## Rule 6. Testimonials and disclaimers

- Testimonials repeated in product literature are the manufacturer's own claims (PC-UK-03); German public device advertising bars misleading third-party statements (PC-DE-11).
- "Not a medical device" or "for information only" disclaimers fail if medical claims appear elsewhere (PC-UK-02).

## Words that signal device status (Great Britain)

PC-UK-04 states that MHRA lists words likely to have contributed to a device determination, with context deciding. The list on the same MHRA page (read in the research pass, raw file `uk_borderline.txt`, not quoted in the row) is: aids, alleviates, avoids, can benefit those who suffer from, clears infestations, clinically proven, combats, compensates for, counteracts, cure and cures, diagnoses and assists diagnosis, eases symptoms, eliminates, heals, help and help with, investigation, monitors, pain relief and relief from pain, prevents, protects against, repairs, stops, traditionally used for, treats, treatments. The page says the list is not exhaustive. A hit is a flag to check against the intended use, never a verdict.

## What the line is not allowed to do in this skill

- Make a clinical or comparative claim that is not already in the person's cleared claim list.
- Name a payer, price or coverage status as a benefit.
- Describe a competitor beyond its public record.
