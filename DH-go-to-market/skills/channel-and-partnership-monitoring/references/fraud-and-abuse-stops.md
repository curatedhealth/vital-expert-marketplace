# Fraud-and-abuse stops and the elements laid out for the owner

Interpretive reference. Every element cites its row in `channels-partners-launch-research.md` (CP prefix, read 19 October 2026). This file tells the model when to stop and what to lay out. It never decides whether an arrangement is protected.

## The rule

When a channel or partner design reaches any trigger below, the model:

1. Stops and names the person's compliance or legal owner (in the person's words; "owner not named" if none, and asks).
2. Lays out the elements of the safe harbor or exception that might apply, as a checklist with the row for each element, and marks each element "stated in your terms", "not stated" or "not applicable", from the terms the person gave. It never marks an element "met".
3. Labels the output "draft for the owner".
4. Never writes that the arrangement is protected, compliant, low risk or safe. Safe harbor protection applies only when every condition is met precisely; other arrangements are judged case by case (CP-US-07). That judgment is the owner's.

Why: the statute is a felony with a maximum $100,000 fine and 10 years (CP-US-01); it runs to the receiving side as well (CP-US-02); no knowledge of the statute or specific intent is needed (CP-US-03); a tainted claim is a false claim (CP-US-04); civil penalties of up to $100,000 per act plus three times the remuneration and exclusion are available (CP-US-06, statutory amounts before inflation adjustment; adjusted amounts `[GAP]`). OIG applies the statute where one purpose of a payment is to induce referrals (CP-US-08).

## Triggers

| # | Trigger in the person's design | Why it stops | Elements to lay out |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Payment to a partner, reseller, agent or contracted clinician that varies with referrals, prescriptions, patients enrolled or units sold | AKS (CP-US-01, CP-US-08) | E1 personal services |
| T2 | Outcomes-based payment to an agent or partner | AKS; outcomes-based payments by device makers and distributors are excluded from the safe harbor (CP-US-13) | E2 outcomes-based payments |
| T3 | Paying a vendor or platform to receive referrals or leads from providers | AO 26-15 reasoning (CP-US-33); opinion binds only its requestor (CP-US-43) | E3 referral-service conditions as quoted in AO 26-15 |
| T4 | Free or below-cost item, device, phone, connectivity or service to a patient | Beneficiary inducement CMP (CP-US-34, CP-US-35) and AKS | E4 patient engagement (hh); E5 CMP exceptions |
| T5 | Free or subsidized technology, staff time or software to a provider or health system | AKS | E6 care coordination (ee); E7 EHR (y); E8 cybersecurity (jj) |
| T6 | Discount, rebate or GPO fee | Statutory exceptions have conditions (CP-US-05) | E9 discount and GPO |
| T7 | Arrangement inside a value-based enterprise or a CMS model | Value-based safe harbors exclude device makers except as limited technology participants (CP-US-21, CP-US-23) | E6, E10 CMS model |
| T8 | Germany: any arrangement touching who prescribes, how prescriptions are routed, or a drug or aid maker | CP-DE-06, CP-DE-07 | E11 DiGA restrictions |

## Elements

**E1 Personal services and management contracts, 42 CFR 1001.952(d)(1)** (CP-US-09, CP-US-10): agreement in writing and signed; covers all services; term at least 1 year; compensation methodology set in advance, consistent with fair market value, not determined by the volume or value of referrals or business generated; aggregate services not beyond what is reasonably necessary for a commercially reasonable purpose.

**E2 Outcomes-based payments, (d)(2)** (CP-US-11 to CP-US-14): legitimate outcome measures selected on clinical evidence or credible medical support, with benchmarks; a writing stating services, outcome measures, the evidence relied on and the monitoring schedule; term at least 1 year; regular monitoring of performance and its effect on quality of care; benchmarks and pay reassessed to stay at fair market value; policies to correct material performance failures. Excluded: payments made by pharmaceutical manufacturers, PBMs, lab companies, device or medical supply manufacturers, device distributors and DMEPOS suppliers; payments based only on internal cost savings or only on patient satisfaction or convenience (CP-US-13). If the payer is a device maker, say the row excludes it and stop.

**E3 Referral-service conditions as quoted in AO 26-15** (CP-US-33): fees assessed uniformly against all participants; based only on the cost of operating the referral service; not varying with the volume or value of Federal program referrals. Quoted from the opinion; the regulation text itself is not in the rows `[GAP]`.

**E4 Patient engagement and support, (hh)** (CP-US-25 to CP-US-28): offered by an eligible party (a device or medical supply maker only if the tool is digital health technology, and not if it reported or expects to report physician ownership under Open Payments); in-kind, no cash or cash equivalent; directly connected to care coordination; recommended by the patient's licensed health care professional; advances adherence, disease management or safety goals; annual retail value cap of $500 in the regulation, $623 for calendar year 2026 per OIG (CP-US-27); not used to market other reimbursable items or for patient recruitment; availability not determined by insurance type; records kept 6 years.

**E5 Beneficiary inducement CMP exceptions** (CP-US-36 to CP-US-42): promotes access to care and poses low risk (unlikely to skew clinical decisions, unlikely to raise costs through overuse, no safety or quality concerns); or offered on equal terms to the general public; or not advertised, not tied to other covered services, reasonably connected to medical care and given after a good-faith financial-need determination. AO 19-02 (a requestor-only opinion, CP-US-43) shows the safeguards OIG relied on for a loaned limited-function smartphone: not advertised, need-based eligibility, cap of two 12-week periods, and a phone with general functions would likely change the outcome (CP-US-39 to CP-US-41). Drug makers are not "providers, practitioners, or suppliers" for the CMP unless they own billing entities (CP-US-42); for device makers the rows are silent `[GAP]`.

**E6 Care coordination arrangements, (ee)** (CP-US-16 to CP-US-22): parties are a value-based enterprise and its participants; target population chosen on legitimate, verifiable criteria written in advance; remuneration in-kind only, used predominantly for care coordination, not for marketing or patient recruitment; signed writing with the listed contents; recipient pays at least 15 percent of cost or fair market value (in advance for one-time costs); device makers only as limited technology participants exchanging digital health technology, not conditioned on exclusive use or minimum purchase; measures monitored and not based solely on satisfaction or convenience; records 6 years. Substantial downside risk and full risk safe harbors exclude device makers (CP-US-23, CP-US-24).

**E7 EHR items and services, (y)** (CP-US-30, CP-US-31): software interoperable when provided (ONC-certified is deemed interoperable); recipient pays 15 percent of the donor's cost before receipt, without donor financing; signed agreement listing items, donor cost and recipient contribution.

**E8 Cybersecurity technology, (jj)** (CP-US-29): donor does not tie eligibility or amount to referrals; recipient does not make the donation a condition of doing business; signed written description; no recipient contribution required.

**E9 Discounts and GPO fees, statutory exceptions** (CP-US-05): discount properly disclosed and reflected in costs claimed; GPO fee in a written contract stating the amount or percentage, with GPO disclosure to members.

**E10 CMS-sponsored model arrangements, (ii)** (CP-US-32): only where CMS made it available for the model; advances model goals; does not induce referrals outside the model; signed writing describing activities and remuneration.

**E11 Germany DiGA restrictions** (CP-DE-06, CP-DE-07): contract physicians and psychotherapists may make no deals with DiGA makers about allocating or transmitting prescriptions; DiGA makers may make no agreements with drug or aid makers that could restrict patient choice or therapeutic freedom. These are prohibitions, not safe harbors: lay out the facts of the arrangement against them and stop.

## Not sourced

Stark Law exceptions (physician-owned partners), inflation-adjusted penalty amounts, state anti-kickback and fee-splitting laws, and any non-US anti-bribery rule. Say `[GAP]` and route to the owner.
