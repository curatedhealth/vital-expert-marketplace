# Go-to-Market: capability map

Framework position: **DH-02.01 Commercial & GTM Strategy** and **DH-02.02 Customer Success & Implementation**, two cores of the VITAL Digital Health capability framework, now one pack (`dh-go-to-market`). This skill replaces the two old capability maps (`commercial-gtm-capability-map`, `customer-success-capability-map`), the two old core menus and their eight heading menus. It does one job: show the person what the pack covers and send them to the one competency skill that fits the request.

## What this skill does and does not do

It reads the request, matches it to a competency, names that skill, and says in one line why. If two skills fit, it names both and the order. It does not answer the commercial or customer success question itself, call a connector, or state any price, market figure, competitor fact, promotion rule, benchmark or threshold. Those live in the competency skills, each with its own sourced references. Customer health, net revenue retention, churn and expansion rules are the person's own; no skill in this pack supplies them.

If the named skill is not available in this assistant, say so and name the pack to install (`dh-go-to-market`). Do not do the competency's work from this map.

## The framework tree

Each heading has two competencies: one that does the work (design and execution) and one that checks it over time (assessment and monitoring). The monitoring skill always works from measures and thresholds the person sets; it never sets its own target.

**DH-02.01 Commercial & GTM Strategy** (winning the account)

- **DH-02.01.2 GTM Strategy**
  - `market-segmentation-and-gtm-strategy` (DH-02.01.2.1). Which segments could the product serve, which go-to-market motion fits each, how is it positioned, and what do commercial peers on record show?
  - `gtm-positioning-monitoring` (DH-02.01.2.2). Against the positioning the person chose, do the live materials still say it, and where do they drift?
- **DH-02.01.1 Commercial Execution**
  - `launch-planning-and-sales-enablement` (DH-02.01.1.1). How is the launch sequenced, how ready is it, which claims go into launch materials, and who on the sales side is enabled to use them?
  - `launch-performance-monitoring` (DH-02.01.1.2). Against the launch measures the person set, how is the launch going, what has changed in the commercial record, and who owns a miss?
- **DH-02.01.3 Pricing & Value Communication**
  - `pricing-and-value-proposition` (DH-02.01.3.1). What is the unit of value, where could the offer price sit against what is on record, how does money flow between buyer, payer and user, and what is the value message?
  - `pricing-and-value-message-monitoring` (DH-02.01.3.2). Against the price outcomes the person set, what was realized, and do the value and promotional claims hold up against the sourced rules? It flags claims and drafts safer wording; it never clears a claim.
- **DH-02.01.4 Channel & Partnership Strategy**
  - `channel-and-partner-design` (DH-02.01.4.1). Which channels and partners fit, what role does each play, which terms need confirming, and how do the options the person scored rank?
  - `channel-and-partnership-monitoring` (DH-02.01.4.2). Against the channel measures the person set, how is each partner performing, and what action follows?

**DH-02.02 Customer Success & Implementation** (keeping the account)

- **DH-02.02.1 Implementation Delivery**
  - `customer-implementation-planning` (DH-02.02.1.1). What is in scope for this customer, in what order do sites or teams go live, and what bar must be met before go-live?
  - `implementation-quality-monitoring` (DH-02.02.1.2). Against the deployment checks the person set, how did each site go live, and what needs repair?
- **DH-02.02.4 Adoption & Change Enablement**
  - `training-and-change-enablement` (DH-02.02.4.1). Which tasks do customer staff need to learn, how is each session recorded, and what makes the change stay?
  - `adoption-monitoring` (DH-02.02.4.2). Against the adoption measures the person set, did use follow training, and what follow-up is needed?
- **DH-02.02.3 Customer Health & Value Realization**
  - `outcome-tracking-and-customer-health` (DH-02.02.3.1). Which signals the person chooses mean an account is healthy, how does each account read against them, and what value note goes back to the customer?
  - `value-realization-monitoring` (DH-02.02.3.2). Do the health thresholds still hold, does the evidence support the value the customer was told about, and who owns an unsupported note?
- **DH-02.02.2 Expansion & Renewal**
  - `account-growth-and-renewal` (DH-02.02.2.1). When does each renewal fall, what growth offer fits, and what does the renewal risk note record for the person to judge?
  - `expansion-and-renewal-monitoring` (DH-02.02.2.2). Using the renewal-rate definition the person sets, how do cohorts compare, and what did expansion deliver?

## Which skill do I need

| If the person asks about | Go to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Target segments, ideal customer, GTM motion, positioning line, competitor or peer landscape | `market-segmentation-and-gtm-strategy` | Choosing where and how to compete |
| "Is our messaging still on position", positioning drift across website, decks, sales materials | `gtm-positioning-monitoring` | Checking positioning against what was chosen |
| Launch plan, launch sequence, launch readiness, commercial brief, sales leave-behind, sales enablement | `launch-planning-and-sales-enablement` | Building and running the launch |
| Launch KPIs, "how is the launch going", changes in the commercial record since launch | `launch-performance-monitoring` | Tracking the launch over time |
| Value proposition, unit of value, offer or list price positioning, money flow, value message | `pricing-and-value-proposition` | Designing the offer and its message |
| Realized vs planned price, discounting, "can we say this in our ad", claims audit | `pricing-and-value-message-monitoring` | Checking price outcomes and claims |
| Channel design, resellers, distribution, health-system or pharma partners, ranking partner options | `channel-and-partner-design` | Designing channels and partner roles |
| Partner scorecards, channel performance, what to do about an underperforming partner | `channel-and-partnership-monitoring` | Checking channels and partners |
| Implementation plan, scope, site sequence, go-live criteria, deployment coordination | `customer-implementation-planning` | Planning the deployment |
| "How did the rollout go", site-by-site go-live review, deployment problems | `implementation-quality-monitoring` | Checking the deployment |
| Training plan for customer staff, session records, change management, super-users | `training-and-change-enablement` | Enabling the customer's people |
| "Are they using it after training", adoption funnel, low-use sites | `adoption-monitoring` | Checking adoption against the person's measures |
| Health signals, account health review, outcome tracking, value note or QBR content | `outcome-tracking-and-customer-health` | Tracking agreed outcomes and account health |
| "Is our health score any good", is the claimed value supported, threshold review | `value-realization-monitoring` | Checking health and value evidence |
| Renewal calendar, upsell or expansion offer, renewal risk note | `account-growth-and-renewal` | Running renewal and growth |
| Renewal rate, churn by cohort, net retention, expansion results | `expansion-and-renewal-monitoring` | Checking renewal and expansion over time |

Tie-breakers:

1. Follow the account. Before the contract: strategy before execution (no segment or positioning chosen yet, start with `market-segmentation-and-gtm-strategy` before `launch-planning-and-sales-enablement`). After the contract: deployment before adoption, adoption before health, health before renewal. If an earlier step has no record yet, start there.
2. Design and execution come before monitoring. If nothing has been set, launched or set up, the monitoring skill has nothing to check; send the person to its partner first.
3. A price the company offers or positions is here (`pricing-and-value-proposition`). A price, code or payment a payer sets, negotiates or reimburses is `dh-access-and-value` (see Related packs). If a request mixes both, name both and the order.
4. Outcomes a customer agreed to see are tracked here (`outcome-tracking-and-customer-health`). If payment depends on those outcomes, the contract and its measurement belong to `dh-access-and-value` `outcomes-contract-monitoring`; name both and the order.
5. Sales-team enablement is `launch-planning-and-sales-enablement`; training the customer's staff on the product is `training-and-change-enablement`. Pricing an existing customer's expansion offer is `account-growth-and-renewal`; list and offer price positioning stays with `pricing-and-value-proposition`.
6. Any claim question ends with the person's regulatory or legal owner. The pack drafts and flags (`pricing-and-value-message-monitoring`); it never clears. Any free item, rebate, payment or incentive offered to patients or providers to win or keep an account stops for the person's compliance or legal owner.
7. If the request is vague ("help with commercial", "help with a customer"), ask one question: is this about where to compete, how to launch, what to charge and say, or who to sell through; or is the customer deploying, adopting, being measured, or renewing? Then route.

## Out of scope for this pack

No skill in this pack does these. Say so in one line and route where a home exists. Do not do the work from this map.

- Customer acquisition cost, acquisition funnels and lifetime value models (CAC to LTV, JTBD-GM-001): not covered by this pack.
- Referral program mechanics: not covered. A referral incentive is a fraud-and-abuse stop handled in `channel-and-partner-design`; the program design itself is out of scope.
- Omnichannel journey tracking and orchestration (JTBD-CSL-002, JTBD-PSD-003): not covered; it runs on the person's private channel data and systems.
- Patient-level personalization (JTBD-PSD-002) and behavior-change intervention design (JTBD-BSL-001): route to `dh-digital-therapeutic-design` (personalization engine; engagement mechanics).
- Organization-wide culture-change programs (JTBD-DAL-002): not covered. Training a customer's staff on the product's tasks and checking adoption is covered (`training-and-change-enablement`, then `adoption-monitoring`).

## Routing examples

- "We have a DiGA-listed insomnia app and want to enter the US employer market. Where do we start?" Route to `market-segmentation-and-gtm-strategy`. It lays out segments and motions for the person to choose; it does not pick one.
- "Our launch is planned in three countries. Can you check launch readiness and build the sales leave-behind?" Route to `launch-planning-and-sales-enablement`.
- "Marketing wants to say our app 'reduces HbA1c by 1 point'. Can we?" Route to `pricing-and-value-message-monitoring`. It flags the claim against the sourced rules and drafts safer wording; clearance stops for the person's regulatory or legal owner.
- "We want to offer free devices to clinics that sign up patients." Route to `channel-and-partner-design`, and say that any free item or payment tied to referrals or volume stops for the person's compliance or legal owner; the skill lays out the facts, never that an arrangement is protected.
- "Our reseller in Germany is missing targets. What now?" Route to `channel-and-partnership-monitoring`.
- "We signed a 12-hospital system for our sepsis alert. Where do we start?" Route to `customer-implementation-planning`.
- "Nurses at three of the hospitals stopped logging in a month after training." Route to `adoption-monitoring`; if the training itself needs rebuilding, `training-and-change-enablement` next.
- "Build me a customer health score for our employer accounts." Route to `outcome-tracking-and-customer-health`, and say that the signals, weights and thresholds come from the person; the skill computes against them.
- "Our renewal with a large payer is in two quarters and usage is down. What do we prepare?" Route to `account-growth-and-renewal`. Renewal risk is the person's judgement; the skill records the facts.
- "The contract pays us only if readmissions fall 10 percent. How do we track that?" Route to `dh-access-and-value` `outcomes-contract-monitoring` for the payment-linked measure; `outcome-tracking-and-customer-health` for the account view alongside it.
- "Our launch landed a health system; now they want a rollout plan and we need to know if our value message still holds." Route to `customer-implementation-planning` for the rollout, and `pricing-and-value-message-monitoring` for the value message; deployment first.

## Related packs

Send the person to another pack when the question is not go-to-market:

- **Market Access and value** (`dh-access-and-value`): anything a payer sets, codes, negotiates or reimburses, and contracts where payment depends on outcomes. Start with `access-and-value-capability-map`. Skills: `coverage-pathway-and-access-strategy`, `coverage-strategy-and-access-rate-monitoring`, `payer-engagement-and-hta-submission`, `hta-submission-and-payer-engagement-monitoring`, `coding-pricing-and-payer-contracting`, `coding-pricing-and-contract-monitoring`, `outcomes-based-contracting`, `outcomes-contract-monitoring`. Most used from here: `coding-pricing-and-payer-contracting` for payer price and codes, `outcomes-based-contracting` and `outcomes-contract-monitoring` where payment depends on outcomes. Economic models and value dossiers are also there.
- **Regulatory and quality** (`dh-regulatory-and-quality`): intended use, classification and what the label permits. Start with `regulatory-and-quality-capability-map`; for intended use, `regulatory-pathway-and-classification`. A claim that changes intended use is a regulatory question first.
- **Patient and user experience** (`dh-patient-and-user-experience`): patient advocacy partnerships and patient programs, patient engagement KPIs, and accessibility, design systems, user research and usability, including when low adoption traces to workflow fit. Start with `patient-and-user-experience-capability-map`; for advocacy partners, `engagement-programs-and-advocacy-partnerships`; for clinical workflow, `clinical-workflow-ux-design`. Commercial channel partners stay here.
- **Clinical development** (`dh-clinical-development`): the clinical evidence behind a claim. Start with `clinical-development-capability-map`.
- Other Digital Health packs, by pack name: `dh-interoperability` (EHR integration and clinical workflow enablement during deployment), `dh-cybersecurity` (business associate and data processing terms), `dh-digital-therapeutic-design` (behavior change and personalization). Use each pack's own map or skill list.

## Rules that hold here

- Short mode: 450 words maximum, counted without tables' source column; cut in this order: context paragraphs, secondary rules, full-mode sections; offer the full version in one line. A routing answer is usually under 150 words: the skill name, one line on why, and the next question the skill will ask.
- Answer first: name the skill in the first sentence.
- People decide. This map never proposes a segment, a price, a target, a partner, a claim verdict, a health score, a renewal risk rating or a threshold, even as an example.
- Use the owner or role the person names, in their words. Only if they named none, write "owner not named" and ask. Never suggest a job title.
- American English, dates in full, no urgency framing.
