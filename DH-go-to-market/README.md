# dh-go-to-market

This pack replaces two packs: `dh-commercial` (DH-02.01 Commercial & GTM Strategy) and `dh-customer-success` (DH-02.02 Customer Success & Implementation).

## DH-02.01 Commercial & GTM Strategy (was dh-commercial)

- `launch-planning-and-sales-enablement`: Plan the commercial launch of a regulated digital health product (DTx, DiGA, SaMD, remote monitoring, digital mental health) and equip the field: the launch sequence against peers, the prerequisites each market sets before selling (FDA registration and UDI, EUDAMED, DTAC and DCB0129, DiGA routes, Medicare billing conditions), the claim list each launch may use, the sales leave-behind and the enablement roster.
- `launch-performance-monitoring`: Monitor a regulated digital health product's launch after go-live (DTx, DiGA, SaMD, remote monitoring, digital mental health): public signals for the product and peers (listings, authorizations, status changes, clocks after launch), launch measures from the team's own field records (accounts reached, meetings, briefings, claims used, unapproved claims in the field), and a launch review against the team's thresholds with an owner and next step for every miss.
- `market-segmentation-and-gtm-strategy`: Lay out the segments a regulated digital health product (DTx, SaMD, DiGA, remote monitoring, digital mental health, software sold with a drug) could serve: who buys, uses and pays, which payer route and channel rules exist per market, which peers are on record; then rank the segments the person scored and draft a GTM motion and a positioning line per chosen segment.
- `gtm-positioning-monitoring`: Check whether a digital health product's go-to-market positioning is used in market and still holds: which artifacts must carry the line (website, app store page, leave-behind), whether each in-market copy has it present, altered or absent, which words cross the sourced promotion rules, what changed in peers' public records, payer routes and wording rules, and which artifact must change, owned by whom.
- `pricing-and-value-proposition`: Build the commercial price position and value proposition for a regulated digital health product (DiGA, DTx, SaMD, remote monitoring, digital mental health): who uses it, who buys it and who pays, what unit the price attaches to, what registered comparator prices and value-based anchors say about a corridor, and value messages per audience that stay inside the sourced promotion rules (FD&C Act 502(a) and 502(gg) for payors, FDA CFL, FTC, MDR Article 7, HWG, CAP Code).
- `pricing-and-value-message-monitoring`: Check whether a regulated digital health product's pricing and value messages hold up: which deals landed inside, below or above the corridor and at what realized price, which live claims in ads, app store text, payer decks or websites cross the sourced promotion rules (FD&C Act 502(a) and 502(gg), FDA CFL and general wellness, FTC, MDR Article 7, UCPD, HWG and DiGAV, DMCCA, CAP Code), with safer wording drafted for the regulatory or legal owner, and whether the corridor or the deals need to change.
- `channel-and-partner-design`: Design the channels and partners that carry a regulated digital health product (DTx, SaMD, DiGA, remote monitoring) to its buyers: which routes reach the buyer in each market and the rules each route sets, what each partner may do, the handoff to your team, the terms that must be true before a partner starts (BAA, GDPR Article 28, claim use, SEC disclosure), and which partners or initiatives to pursue first from the scores you set.
- `channel-and-partnership-monitoring`: Check whether the channels and partners that carry a regulated digital health product (DTx, SaMD, DiGA, remote monitoring) are working: define what a healthy partner looks like in your own measures, score each partner for the period from your handoff record rather than the partner's own report, catch claim breaches and data-term duties (breach notices, sub-processors, patient engagement caps), read public signals on a partner, and decide hold, continue or change terms against your own rules.

## DH-02.02 Customer Success & Implementation (was dh-customer-success)

- `customer-implementation-planning`: Plan how a digital health product (SaMD, DTx, remote monitoring, clinical software) is deployed at a customer site: the implementation scope and blueprint (sites, modules, users, day-one workflow, integrations now and later, milestones, measures), the deployment sequence with its dependencies and the contract and data steps that gate it (BAA, GDPR Article 28, minimum necessary access, DTAC and DCB0160 for the NHS), training placed after configuration, and the go-live bar the site signs.
- `implementation-quality-monitoring`: Check whether customer deployments of a digital health product (SaMD, DTx, remote monitoring, clinical software) met their go-live bar and how they perform: evidence per bar item per site, implementation outcomes computed from the person's own site file (penetration of eligible patients and of providers, setting adoption, usage against the profile agreed at commissioning, fidelity to the milestone plan, milestone and go-live slippage), and a site review with open items, owners and repairs.
- `account-growth-and-renewal`: Run renewals and account growth for a digital health product sold to health systems, practices, employers or payers: which renewals fall inside your horizon and which accounts are ready to expand, what a renewal or expansion offer may contain under US fraud-and-abuse rules (discounts, free modules or services, anything reaching patients), what renewal decks and account materials may claim, and a risk note with actions for accounts that may not renew.
- `expansion-and-renewal-monitoring`: Measure renewals and expansion for a digital health product sold to health systems, practices, employers or payers: write down the renewal and retention definitions the team will report (cohort, what counts as renewed, exclusions, value basis), compute the renewal rate, value retained with and without expansion, and losses by reason from the account file with the formula shown, and check whether growth offers turned into signed expansions.
- `outcome-tracking-and-customer-health`: Track the outcomes a digital health customer agreed to see and read each account's health against targets the person sets: choose usage and outcome signals with sourced definitions (RE-AIM, Proctor penetration, NICE ESF usage reporting, PHQ-9, GAD-7 and PAM with what is and is not sourced), compute attainment and a health view per account from the person's account file, and draft the value note or next action per account.
- `value-realization-monitoring`: Check over time whether a digital health company's customer health thresholds still fit and whether the value it reports to customers is backed: targets no account has met, signals no longer stored, shares that are not reach, targets eased after results turned accounts green, value notes matched to signal rows (supported, value differs, no row), effect or cost wording no usage figure can carry, and a correction plan with owners the person names.
- `training-and-change-enablement`: Plan and run customer training and change enablement for a digital health product (SaMD, DTx, remote monitoring, EHR-integrated tools) at a health system, clinic, payer, employer or pharma customer: task cards per role built from the day-one workflow, a training plan that matches what NICE ESF and FDA human factors guidance expect, session records that keep attendance apart from task completion, train-the-trainer and champion rosters, and the plan that makes the old step stop.
- `adoption-monitoring`: Measure whether a digital health product is actually adopted after training and go-live at a customer (health system, clinic, payer, employer or pharma organization), and decide what to do where it is not: an adoption rule stated as the trained task done in production, adoption and penetration computed over time from the person's own usage file with every denominator shown (Proctor, RE-AIM), the old step still in use, activity after training, and a follow-up that names whether the task, the training or the workflow is wrong.
