# B1 Decision support design and delivery

Framework task: DH-05.02.2.1, DH-05.02.2.3, DH-05.02.3.2 and DH-05.01.3.3, Part B, task B1. Carries the old tasks "Executive question", "Dashboard layout", "Executive review" (DH-05.02.2.1.1 to .3), "Commentary claim", "Driver", "Commentary text" (DH-05.02.2.3.1 to .3), "Workflow insertion point", "Action from the number", "Embed owner" (DH-05.02.3.2.1 to .3), "Recommendation statement", "Delivery to the owner" and "Unresolved question" (DH-05.01.3.3.1 to .3).

## What this task delivers

The person's design choices for how an analytic reaches its decision, written down and checked: the executive dashboard (decision metric first, comparison, data timestamp, the metric kept off, the executive review record); the commentary built from one sentence and a computed driver; the embedded analytic (host workflow and step, what appears, what is removed, the action without leaving the step, the metric owner, the workflow owner and who stops a stale number); the recommendation in the decision maker's choice; and, beside the design, the decision support facts that apply in the person's market (FDA CDS Criterion 4, ONC decision support interventions, 45 CFR 92.210, EU AI Act oversight, DECIDE-AI, fairness criteria). It answers "how does the number reach the decision, what may it say, who stops it when it is wrong, and which rules sit beside it?"

Why it matters: a link out to a dashboard is not an embedded analytic, a commentary that explains a move no breakdown accounts for invents a driver, and a recommendation shown to a clinician under time pressure meets different FDA facts than one shown with its basis (UX-US-66). The design is the person's; the facts that bear on it must be on the page.

## Read the request

- Which deliverable: dashboard, commentary, embedded analytic, recommendation, or the decision support fact sheet? Run only those steps; the fact sheet (step 5) runs whenever an analytic informs a clinical decision.
- Who sees the output: a health care professional, a patient or caregiver, or business staff? Patient- or caregiver-facing recommendations meet the device definition under the FDA CDS guidance (UX-US-73): lay that fact out and route.
- Which market? FDA, ONC and 45 CFR 92.210 rows for the US; AI Act rows for the EU (with their dates); NICE ESF rows for the NHS.
- Is commentary generated by a model? Then the generative AI risks in step 2 apply.

## Workflow

### Step 1. Executive dashboard (old DH-05.02.2.1)

Write back the executive (or role), the decision, the few metrics that decide it and the one kept off. Check the layout the person gives: decision metric first, period and comparison shown (the person's prior period or target; the model supplies no target), data timestamp visible. Only certified metrics appear; a draft metric shows its status (A2). Record the executive review as given: the decision made from the page, the metric asked for and missing, the metric to remove. A dashboard not reviewed with its executive is "not yet official" (old DH-05.02.2.1.3).

### Step 2. Commentary (old DH-05.02.2.3)

Write the one sentence: the metric move, its comparison, from the certified table. Then run the driver on the person's breakdown:

```
python3 scripts/metric_check.py driver --file BREAKDOWN.csv --share <the person's rule>
```

Without the person's rule, no driver is named; with it, name only the segments the script prints, and those that moved against the total. If none accounts for the move, the commentary says so. The action is a question unless the decision frame named one. Date the commentary and name its author. One claim only. If a model drafts the text, every number must match the metric table and every driver must come from the script output; confabulation and automation bias are named risks (NIST AI 600-1, July 2024, DA-20, DA-21; WHO LMM guidance, news release 18 January 2024, DA-34).

### Step 3. Embedded analytic (old DH-05.02.3.2)

Write back the host workflow and step, the number or recommendation that appears, and what is removed from that step. Name the action the user can take without leaving the step and the write-back or task it creates; an action that still needs another system or an export is stated, and "workflow changed" is not claimed. Name the metric owner, the workflow owner and which one stops the display when the metric is stale, with the stale rule in the person's words (or "not given"). A stale number on a clinical step with no stop owner is flagged. Where PHI moves into the host system, the minimum necessary rows apply (CS103 to CS105); the integration build is `interoperability-and-ehr-integration` (this pack).

### Step 4. Recommendation and delivery (old DH-05.01.3.3)

Write the recommendation in the decision maker's choice as the person states it: the action, the alternative, the evidence with its source, and the condition that would change it. If the person has not stated the action, draft it from their analysis labeled "proposed", or write the action as a question. An action that depends on data marked `[GAP]` is not recommended; it is parked with the missing data, its owner and whether the action waits. Record delivery as given: the person, the date, the question asked that could not be answered, the action chosen.

### Step 5. Decision support facts beside the design

Lay out, for the person's market and audience, the facts that bear on the design, each with source, date and row, and what the person's design shows for each ("shown", "not shown" or "not given"):

- US, HCP-facing: FDA CDS guidance, 29 January 2026 (UX-US-64): Criterion 4 independent review of the basis (UX-US-65); time-critical use does not meet it (UX-US-66); inputs, algorithm basis, knowns and unknowns, avoiding overload (UX-US-67 to UX-US-70); usability testing may be needed (UX-US-71); automation bias (UX-US-72); single clinically appropriate option (UX-US-74).
- US, patient- or caregiver-facing: meets the device definition (UX-US-73).
- US, certified health IT: 45 CFR 170.315(b)(11) (UX-US-54 to UX-US-59, UX-US-62), with the HTI-5 proposal of 29 December 2025 stated as proposed only (UX-US-60, UX-US-63).
- US, covered entities: 45 CFR 92.210 (PE-113 to PE-115): list the tool's inputs that measure race, color, national origin, sex, age or disability, from the person's input list.
- EU, high-risk AI systems: Articles 13 to 15 (DA-56 to DA-62), applying from 2 December 2027 (Annex III) or 2 August 2028 (Annex I) as amended (DA-67).
- Any AI decision support in live use: DECIDE-AI items on agreement, errors and usability (DA-80 to DA-84, DOI 10.1136/bmj-2022-070904); NHS silent mode (CS60) and communication of limits (CS65) where the NHS applies.
- A product that ranks or allocates: the fairness criteria in conflict and the impossibility row (Paulus and Kent 2020, DA-119, DOI 10.1038/s41746-020-0304-9); the criterion that fits is the person's.

Never conclude device status or compliance: device status routes to `dh-regulatory-and-quality` regulatory-pathway-and-classification; compliance conclusions stop for the owner the person names for legal or compliance matters. A draft guidance (DA-35, DA-46) is never a requirement.

## Build the output

Follow `assets/decision-support-design-and-delivery-output.md`. Answer first: what the design shows and what it lacks against the old method (timestamp, comparison, stop owner, export), the driver result under the person's rule, and the decision support facts that bear most on this design with "for your regulatory owner" where device status is in play. Short mode: 450 words maximum, counted without the calls list and the Source and Rows columns. Before handing over, count the words with `python3 scripts/metric_check.py words ANSWER.md` (counts the body without the calls list and leaves out Source and Rows columns); without code execution, count the same way by hand. The note that the connector was unavailable goes in the calls list, not the body. In short mode a table keeps only rows tied to the person's question; the rest go to the full version. If over 450, cut in this order until under: context paragraphs, repeated row ids in prose, secondary rules, rows not tied to the person's question. Never cut a gap, a stop or the record line. Refusals follow the same 450-word cap.

## Before you hand it over

- The design, the comparison, the driver rule, the stop owner and the recommendation are the person's, and the record says so.
- No device conclusion, no compliance conclusion, no target.
- Drafts and proposals (DA-35, UX-US-60) are labeled as such; AI Act dates are stated as amended.
- No driver is named without the person's rule; generated commentary carries only numbers from the metric table.
- Every fact names its source and date in plain words with the row id after it; [PAPER] rows carry their DOI.
- `python3 scripts/metric_check.py words ANSWER.md` prints "within the short-mode cap".
- The record line is the very last line; the calls list and the offer of the full version come before the record block: Clears it: [the person named, in their words, or owner not named]. The record is built with `verify.build_record` once that named person clears it; until then, uncleared draft.
