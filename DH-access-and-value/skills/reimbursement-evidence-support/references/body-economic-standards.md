# What each body asks of the economic case and the evidence

Interpretive reference for A1 and A2. Every line cites a row in `health-economics-methods-research.md` (`HE-` ids) or `evidence-synthesis-research.md` (`ES-` ids). All rows were read on 19 October 2026 (`ES-171` to `ES-193` on 20 October 2026) and are not owner-reviewed. Re-read the source before external use. Where a body's rule is not here, it is `[GAP]`. Rows marked [PAPER] cite their DOI when used; "abstract only" rows say so.

## England: NICE

NICE has three texts that bear on a digital product's economic case. Which one governs depends on the guidance type and when the topic started.

### Which text applies

| Situation | Text | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| HealthTech guidance (devices, diagnostics, digital including AI) | PMG48, published 14 July 2025, last updated 17 December 2025 | `HE-064`, `HE-065` |
| Early-use guidance (formerly "early value assessment") | PMG48 section 3; it supersedes the EVA interim statement | `HE-067` |
| Topics started before the cutoffs | Medtech and diagnostics before 14 July 2025: pre-July 2025 PMG36; early use before 14 July 2025: the EVA interim statement; existing use before 23 October 2025: the LSA interim statement | `HE-066` |
| Utilities for cost-utility analysis | PMG51 interim statement of 27 August 2026, for HealthTech topics launched after it | `HE-057` to `HE-063` |
| Reference case | PMG36 section 4.2, which PMG48 refers to; PMG36 last updated 13 October 2026 | `HE-033`, `HE-070` |
| Commissioner yardstick for digital technologies | Evidence Standards Framework (ECD7), last updated 9 August 2022; voluntary | `HE-078`, `HE-079` |

### Routine use (HealthTech)

| Element | Rule | Row |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluation type | Cost-utility analysis or cost-comparison analysis are the preferred value-for-money methods | `HE-073` |
| Reference case | Every economic evaluation includes a reference-case analysis; non-reference-case analyses are justified and kept separate | `HE-035` |
| Costs | NHS and personal social services perspective | `HE-037` |
| Outcomes | All health effects, for patients and, when relevant, carers | `HE-036` |
| Productivity | Excluded from the reference case; may be shown separately | `HE-038` |
| Time horizon | Long enough to capture all important differences in costs or outcomes | `HE-040` |

| Utilities (new topics) | EQ-5D-5L with the UK 5L value set (Rowen et al. 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.jval.2026.03.008); 3L data mapped with the DSU/EEPRU model; no 3L utilities in sensitivity or scenario analyses | `HE-058`, `HE-060`, `HE-061`, `HE-127` |
| Utilities (topics started before 27 August 2026) | Continue to map 5L data to 3L utility values | `HE-063` |
| Mapping | A departure from the reference case; the function must be described, justified and shown to fit | `HE-045` |
| Discounting | 3.5% a year for costs and health effects; 1.5% only if all three PMG36 criteria hold | `HE-047`, `HE-048` |
| Results | ICER as expected additional cost over expected additional QALYs; expected net health benefit also shown | `HE-041` |
| Cost comparison | Cost-neutral or cost-saving technologies are generally considered cost effective under cost comparison | `HE-076`, `HE-042` |
| Severity modifier | Not applied, initially, to HealthTech guidance | `HE-075`, `HE-053` |
| Rapidly iterating technologies | Threshold analyses identify a parameter switching value | `HE-077` |

**Thresholds: an open conflict.** PMG48 section 2.4.4 states that an ICER below £20,000 per QALY is generally considered cost effective, with a stronger case needed between £20,000 and £30,000 and particularly above £30,000 (`HE-074`). PMG36 section 6.3 uses £25,000 and £35,000 (`HE-050`, `HE-051`) and says the committee uses no precise maximum (`HE-049`). On 19 October 2026 the HealthTech text still states £20,000 to £30,000. Show both, with their dates, and say NICE decides which applies. Never present either range as a target or as a verdict on the person's model.

**Budget impact in PMG36.** NHS England may seek a longer implementation and offer commercial talks when net budget impact is expected to exceed £40 million a year in any of the first 3 years (raised from £20 million in June 2025; PMG36 section 5.10.4) (`HE-055`). This is a PMG36 process rule; whether it applies to a HealthTech topic is `[GAP]` in the sourced rows.

## European Union: joint clinical assessment (Regulation (EU) 2021/2282)

**JCA is clinical only.** Pricing, reimbursement and conclusions on value stay with Member States (`HE-093`). Cost and economic evaluation is a non-clinical domain, outside JCA (`HE-094`). JCA reports describe relative effects and the degree of certainty, without value judgments on overall clinical added value (`HE-095`, `ES-149`). An economic model is therefore never a JCA requirement; the economic case goes to each national body.

| Element | Rule | Row |
|---|---|---|
| Devices in scope | Class IIb or III devices with an expert-panel opinion, and class D IVDs, subject to selection | `HE-096`, `ES-146` |
| Selection | By implementing act; criteria include incorporation of AI or machine learning; at least one of six criteria; selection takes place quarterly | `HE-097`, `ES-147` |
| First device selection | 6 October 2026: two transcatheter mitral valve systems; no digital-only product | `HE-101` |
| Device procedure rules | Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2086 of 17 October 2025 | `HE-100`, `ES-154` |
| Dossier | Complete for available studies, appropriate methods, transparent, with underlying documentation | `ES-148` |
| Device evidence sources | Sponsor and third-party studies including ongoing ones; at least MEDLINE and CENTRAL; registries; HTA reports; the clinical evaluation report; device and patient registries | `ES-155` |
| Search cut-off | At most 3 months before dossier submission | `ES-156` |
| Risk of bias | RoB 1 for RCT outcomes (conflicts with Cochrane's RoB 2; domains and criteria below); ROBINS-I for comparative non-randomized outcomes | `ES-163`, `ES-164`, `ES-171` to `ES-193` |
| PICO | One population, one intervention, one comparator, at least one outcome; scoping aims for the fewest PICOs | `ES-167` |
| AI use | No dossier step fully automated without a responsible human; all AI-assisted steps documented | `ES-169`, `ES-170` |

### RoB 1 as the JCA applies it (read 20 October 2026)

The HTACG validity guidance asks JCA assessors to use RoB 1 for RCT outcomes (`ES-163`); RoB 2 remains Cochrane's current tool for randomized trials (research file, "Changes and conflicts"). RoB 1 is the Cochrane Handbook 5.1.0 tool: for each domain, a support for judgment (what happened, with verbatim quotes, and an explicit statement when no information exists) and a judgment of Low, High or Unclear risk (`ES-171`, `ES-172`, `ES-174`, `ES-189`). Judgments concern material bias; insufficient detail usually means Unclear (`ES-176`). Two people judge independently (`ES-190`); quality scales are not used (`ES-191`). `ES-189` to `ES-193` are [PAPER] rows: Higgins et al., BMJ 2011, DOI 10.1136/bmj.d5928.

| Domain | Low risk | High risk | Unclear | Rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random sequence generation | A random component described (random number table, computer generator, coin, cards, dice, lots; minimization) | A non-random rule (date of birth, admission date, record number) or allocation by judgment or preference | Insufficient information | `ES-180`, `ES-181` |
| Allocation concealment | Central allocation, identical numbered containers, numbered opaque sealed envelopes | Open schedule, unsafeguarded envelopes, alternation, date of birth, record number | Method not described in enough detail | `ES-182` |
| Blinding of participants and personnel | Blinding ensured, or no blinding but the outcome is unlikely to be influenced | No or broken blinding and the outcome likely influenced | Insufficient information, or outcome not addressed | `ES-183` |
| Blinding of outcome assessment | Assessment blinded, or measurement unlikely to be influenced | No blinding and measurement likely influenced | Insufficient information | `ES-184` |
| Incomplete outcome data | None missing, reasons unrelated or balanced, impact not clinically relevant, appropriate imputation | Outcome-related reasons with imbalance, clinically relevant impact, as-treated with substantial departure, inappropriate simple imputation | Attrition or exclusions insufficiently reported | `ES-185` |
| Selective outcome reporting (per trial) | Protocol available and all pre-specified outcomes reported as planned | Pre-specified primary outcomes missing, changed, added or reported too incompletely to meta-analyze | Expected for most studies | `ES-186`, `ES-193` |
| Other bias | Apparently free of other bias | At least one important risk (design-specific bias, claimed fraud) | Insufficient information or rationale | `ES-187` |

Entries: one per study for sequence generation, allocation concealment and selective reporting; per outcome where needed for the two blinding domains and incomplete outcome data (`ES-173`). Summary per outcome: Low if all key domains are low; Unclear if low or unclear for all key domains; High if any key domain is high (`ES-192`). Which domains are key is the assessor's call. The model lays out the criteria; the judgments are the reviewers'.

The review method behind a JCA dossier belongs to dh-clinical-development `literature-and-real-world-evidence-review`. For this skill, the JCA matters in two ways: whether the product could be in scope at all, and that the economic case is national.

## United States: ICER and AMCP

| Element | Rule | Row |
|---|---|---|

| ICER-PHTI horizon | One year, with 2-to-5-year scenarios where offsets accrue later; one cohort followed from inception | `HE-122` |
| ICER-PHTI perspectives | The DHT user, and the payer or plan sponsor (main) | `HE-123` |

| ICER budget impact threshold | $821 million for 2025-2026 applies to prescription drugs, not DHTs | `HE-119` |

The ICER-PHTI threshold price is ICER's computation for its own report. This skill states the method; it never computes such a price for the person's product (pricing positions belong to dh-access-and-value and stop for the owner).

## Reporting and model good practice (all bodies)

- CHEERS 2022 replaces CHEERS 2013 and covers e-health and devices; 28 items; budget impact analyses are outside it; it is not a score (`HE-001` to `HE-005`; [PAPER] Husereau et al. 2022, DOI 10.1177/23814683211061097).
- Budget impact analysis elements, method choice, local data, scenarios, minimum validation and reporting (`HE-018` to `HE-024`; [PAPER] Sullivan et al. 2014, DOI 10.1016/j.jval.2013.08.2291, abstract only).
- Model validation types and transparency (`HE-016`, `HE-017`; [PAPER] Eddy et al. 2012, DOI 10.1016/j.jval.2012.04.012, abstract only).
- EQ-5D value sets by country (`HE-126` to `HE-129`).
