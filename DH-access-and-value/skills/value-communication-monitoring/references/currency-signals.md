# Currency signals: what can make a claim or dossier element stale

Interpretive reference for task A2. Rows from `evidence-synthesis-research.md` (ES), `health-economics-research.md` (HE) and `promotion-and-claims-research.md` (PC), read 19 October 2026. A signal means "re-read and let the person decide", never "the claim is now false".

## Source-level signals (per DOI)

| Signal | What to do | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| Retraction or expression of concern | Hold every claim that rests on it; name the notice. | ES-119, ES-121, ES-124, ES-125 [WF] |
| Correction (erratum) | Re-read the corrected passage; an erratum corrects honest errors that do not invalidate conclusions, but the figure may have changed. | ES-121 |
| Retraction status shown inconsistently | Say which source was checked (the library checked Crossref only on 19 October 2026). | ES-123 |
| Comments on the paper | Do not discard; they may warn of a future retraction. | ES-122 |
| New studies since the narrative was built | Candidates to read, found by search with `date_from`; the person or a review decides whether they change a finding. A review update is the work of dh-clinical-development (literature-and-real-world-evidence-review). | ES-101 to ES-103 |
| Age of a review the narrative cites | In one study of 100 reviews the median time to need an update was 5.5 years; 23 percent were out of date within two years. Context, not a rule for the person's review. | ES-102 |
