## Changes and conflicts

- Germany, statute vs framework: § 134 SGB V makes performance components mandatory (at least 20 percent) from 1 January 2026, but the latest listed framework agreement (§ 9) still treats them as optional ("können") and leaves the measures to the parties. Until a newer framework agreement or arbitration ruling is found, the operative measure set and attainment method are undefined at framework level.
- Germany, timing mismatch: performance pricing applies from 1 January 2026, while the first AbEM report to BfArM is due 15 April 2027 (DiGAV § 23a(3)). The GKV-Spitzenverband's own 2025 report (1 April 2026) flags this.
- Germany, scope limit: AbEM data are reported only for permanently listed DiGA, and PROM data are not reported where the DiGA proved its benefit only through structural and procedural improvements (DiGAV § 23a(1)). Performance clauses built on AbEM PROMs therefore cannot apply uniformly to all DiGA.
- United States, payee: ACCESS pays Part B enrolled care organizations, not device or software makers. A DTx or remote-monitoring maker reaches it only as a participant (if enrolled) or as a vendor to one.
- ACCESS documents differ on the oversight role: the model page uses both "Medicare-enrolled Medical Director" and "physician Clinical Director". The RFA (v1.1) changed payment frequency from quarterly to monthly, as the payment-amounts document notes.
- England: outcome-based agreements exist in the NHS commercial framework for medicines only. For medtech and digital, the route is NICE conditional recommendation plus evidence generation, and the MedTech Funding Mandate pays on cost and volume, not outcomes ([WF]).
