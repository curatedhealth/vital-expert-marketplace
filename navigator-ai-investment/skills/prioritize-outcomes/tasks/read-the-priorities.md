# B Read the priorities

Framework task: 2.2 Outcome Prioritization, Part B.

## What this task delivers

A plain reading of the scores: which outcomes look most underserved and why (high importance, low satisfaction, or both), where scorers disagree and who holds the other view, whether the ranking holds when any one scorer is left out, how the owner's own view differs, and what that means for the value drivers. Nothing in it is a decision.

Why it matters: a ranked list hides as much as it shows. Two outcomes can share an opportunity of 14 for opposite reasons; an average can sit between two camps that each hold a clear view; a top outcome can depend on one scorer. The owner needs to see all three before attesting (method.md O11 to O13).

## Workflow

### Step 1. Show the group's ranking

| Rank | Outcome | Importance | Satisfaction today | Opportunity | Band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Each figure is the group's average, labeled "computed, scored in the room". Name the scorers; say "indicative" if fewer than three.

### Step 2. Say why each top outcome is where it is

For the top three to five, one line each:

- **Underserved:** important and poorly served (importance 8, satisfaction 3). This is where change pays most.
- **Important, fairly served:** high because important, little gap. Improvement is welcome but not urgent.
- **Overserved:** satisfaction above importance. The gap counts as zero; effort here may be more than needed.

Where the mean and the median of scorers' own opportunity differ by 2 or more, say the average is pulled by one or two scorers (skill practice, H).

### Step 3. Show disagreement as views, not noise

For every outcome with a spread of 3 or more on either criterion, by criterion: every scorer at the low end and every scorer at the high end (not only the most extreme), and ask the holder of the minority view for one line (session question 3). Record it in the dissent section. Do not ask them to rescore. Disagreement on satisfaction often means people see different parts of today; disagreement on importance often means they serve different parties.

### Step 4. Does it hold?

Read the sensitivity result and say only what it tested: when each scorer is left out in turn, does the first-ranked outcome change (a flip, which triggers the field test), does the top set change, does any lower pair swap (reported only)? Say it plainly ("the leader and top three hold whoever is left out; without Ben, outcomes B and C swap places"). Never say "the order holds" unless the full order was compared and held. A change caused by an exact tie is named as a tie.

### Step 5. Then the owner's view

After the group's result has been seen: the owner's own ranking, and where it differs on the top set. Do not reconcile the two; both go in the record.

### Step 6. Connect to value

Group the top outcomes by the value driver they contribute to. A chosen driver with no outcome in the top set is worth saying: its value depends on outcomes the room ranks lower.

## Boundaries

- No "so we should invest in", "drop" or "the clear winner". Bands describe.
- No combining of dimensions into one weighted score.
- Never present a room score as measured.

## When you are unsure

If the scores are flat (everything 9 and 9), say the scores do not separate the outcomes and ask the owner whether to score again with a forced spread or to field-test; do not pick for them.
