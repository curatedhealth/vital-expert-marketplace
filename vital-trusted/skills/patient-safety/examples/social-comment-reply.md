# Worked example: a social media comment the brand team wants to answer

Invented case for teaching. "Corvelan" is a fictional product; the commenter, the page
and the brand team's draft are made up.

## Request

"Brand team here. Someone commented on our disease-awareness post. Can you make our
reply friendlier? We want to reassure her and keep the conversation positive."

## Comment (supplied)

> @jules_r, 18 October 2026: "Been on Corvelan 3 weeks and my hair is falling out in
> clumps?? Also pretty sure it stopped working for my joints. Is this normal??"

## Brand team's draft reply (supplied)

> "Hi Jules! Hair thinning is a known and temporary side effect, so nothing to worry
> about. Keep taking it, it usually starts working again after a few weeks. DM us your age
> and dose and we'll look into it!"

## What the skill does

1. **Part B first.** The comment describes two things: hair loss after starting the
   product, and the product no longer working. Both are flagged (S1, S2). The company has
   now seen it on its own page (S5).
2. **Repair the reply (Part A, patient language, and Part B repair).** The draft grades
   the event ("known and temporary", P2, P4), reassures ("nothing to worry about", L1),
   advises treatment ("keep taking it", L3) and asks for health details in public (P10).
   All four go.
3. **No contact route is invented.** The person did not supply one, so it is a gap (L9).

## Output (as the person sees it)

> **Before the draft: something to pass to your safety reporting procedure**
>
> The comment by @jules_r on your page, 18 October 2026, mentions:
> - "my hair is falling out in clumps" after "3 weeks" on Corvelan — possible side effect
> - "pretty sure it stopped working for my joints" — the product may not be working
>
> Please pass this to your safety reporting procedure as soon as you can, in the time it
> sets. Send a screenshot or link of the original comment, unchanged.
>
> I have not judged whether it is related to Corvelan, how serious it is, or whether it
> needs reporting. Your safety team decides that, and they will follow up for any detail
> that is missing.
>
> **Reply, reworked**
>
> "Hi Jules, thank you for telling us, and we're sorry you're going through this. Please
> talk to your doctor or pharmacist about what you're experiencing before making any
> change to your treatment. [GAP: needs a decision — the private contact route this reply
> gives for side-effect reports — from your safety reporting procedure or social media
> procedure]"
>
> **What I changed and why (draft, not yet reviewed)**
>
> Taken out
> 1. "a known and temporary side effect". Whether it is a side effect, and what kind, is
>    for your safety team, not the reply.
> 2. "nothing to worry about". A reply cannot give that comfort; her doctor can.
> 3. "Keep taking it, it usually starts working again". This tells her what to do about
>    her treatment, which only her doctor can decide.
> 4. "DM us your age and dose". Follow-up is for your safety team, by the route your
>    procedure sets, and health details should not be gathered in a public thread.
>
> Questions for you
> 1. Which contact route should the reply give?
>
> Next step: the reply goes through the review your company uses for social media
> replies, and the comment goes to your safety reporting procedure. Kept friendly, as you
> asked, without reassurance the reply cannot give.

## Why this is the right depth

A public reply leaves the company (TR-H12). "Make it friendlier" is honored in tone; the
request to "reassure her" is declined in plain words because reassurance about a
possible side effect is a clinical judgment (TR-H6).

## Behind the output (reference only, not shown to the person)

Rules S1, S2, S5. Patterns P2, P4, P9, P10, L1, L3, L9. Check lines:

```
Safe · patient wording: 0 passages for patients with no route to a doctor, nurse or pharmacist (not tool-checked)
  - original reply graded the event, reassured, advised to keep taking it and asked for health details in public; all removed
Safe · possible side effects: 2 mentioned in the draft or the material, to pass to your safety reporting procedure (not tool-checked)
  - comment: hair loss after three weeks; product may have stopped working
```
