# Worked example: call notes that mention a pregnancy

Invented case for teaching. "Zavira", the doctor and the label text are fictional.

## Context card (from trusted-intake, already in the conversation)

```
TRUSTED context: summary of a call with a doctor, for the team

Reader          the medical team                                    (stated)
Market          Switzerland                                         (stated)
Product status  approved in Switzerland per your label              (read)
Use             internal only                                       (stated)
Deliverable     short call summary                                  (stated)
Material        call notes of 5 November 2026; label excerpt
Stakes          internal and quick
Next process    your safety reporting procedure, for the possible event
```

## Request

"Summarize my call notes for the team."

## Material supplied

> **Call notes, 5 November 2026:** Dr. Roth, cardiology, asked about Zavira in older
> patients. She feels Zavira is safe in the elderly. She also said one of her patients
> became pregnant two months after starting Zavira and stopped it herself.
> **Label excerpt (as supplied, Swiss product information, revision date July 2026):**
> 4.2 Elderly: "No dose adjustment is required in patients aged 65 years and over.
> Clinical experience in patients over 85 years is limited."

## Safety flag (as the person sees it, first)

Before the draft: something to pass to your safety reporting procedure

Dr. Roth, cardiology, in your call notes of 5 November 2026, mentions:
- "one of her patients became pregnant two months after starting Zavira and stopped it
  herself": pregnancy while on treatment

Please pass this to your safety reporting procedure as soon as you can, in the time it
sets. Forward the original notes as received; your safety procedure decides what
personal details it needs.

I have not judged whether it is related to Zavira, how serious it is, or whether it
needs reporting. Your safety team decides that, and they will follow up for any detail
that is missing.

## Summary for the team (as the person sees it)

> **Call with Dr. Roth, cardiology, 5 November 2026**
>
> Dr. Roth asked about Zavira in older patients. The label says: "No dose adjustment is
> required in patients aged 65 years and over. Clinical experience in patients over 85
> years is limited." (section 4.2, revision date July 2026)
>
> One possible pregnancy exposure report was passed to the safety procedure.

## Note to the person

What I changed and why (Uncleared draft)

Based on: your call notes and the Swiss product information excerpt you supplied.

Changed
1. "She feels Zavira is safe in the elderly" → the label's own words on older patients,
   in quotation marks. The team reads this summary, and "safe" is not what the label says.

Kept
1. One line saying a possible pregnancy exposure report was passed to the safety
   procedure, with no details of the event. This is an internal team summary, so it keeps
   that line; the flag above carries the details. You can remove the line yourself if
   you prefer.

I compared the draft with the label and the notes you gave me; I did not check that the
label is the current approved version.

## Why it is written this way

- The flag comes first and is unconditional. It quotes the notes word for word and
  judges nothing about cause, seriousness or reporting.
- The summary quotes the label for the older-patient statement and does not repeat the
  doctor's opinion as fact.
- The internal team summary carries only the line "One possible pregnancy exposure report
  was passed to the safety procedure", with no event details, and the person may remove
  it themselves. Readers in the safety function or medical information team handling the case (not a medical leadership team) could get the event as one of
  the items they asked for. A newsletter or other broad or
  external piece would carry no individual case details; the flag handles the report.
- No clinical advice was given about the patient who became pregnant.

  - call notes: pregnancy while on treatment
