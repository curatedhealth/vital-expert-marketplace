# Intake: patient-safety

Read every input for a possible safety report first, before this intake and before any
other task. If one is there, the flag goes out first, as its own short note
(`tasks/side-effect-flag.md`). No question below comes before it, and none delays it.

Then read the context card from `trusted-intake` (`../trusted-intake/templates/context-card.md`).
If the conversation holds one for this work, take the reader, market, product status and
stakes from it and do not ask again for anything it holds.
When the conversation holds no card (this skill used alone), take what the message says, ask at most the questions below, and do not run intake separately.

## What this skill needs

| Need | Comes from | If it is missing |
|---|---|---|
| Every input from outside the draft: emails, notes, posts, comments, transcripts, complaints. Aggregate adverse event results from a published study or a label are evidence, not a possible safety report (TR-H14) | The conversation | Read them all. Nothing to ask |
| The label for the market, with its date | A document the person supplied | Ask (question 1) when the draft states a dose, warning, interaction or special-population statement. No label: leave a visible gap |
| Reader: patient, carer, public, or a health professional | The card or the request | Ask (question 2) when patient wording could apply |
| Market | The card or the label | Ask (question 3) only when the label or patient wording depends on it |

## Questions

Pick from these. When drafting, ask at most one combined question, after any flag has
gone out and alongside the draft, with label content left as gaps (draft first, TR-H7).
None for a quick internal note with no safety content. Plain words, with the reason.

1. "Can you paste the label for this product, for that country, with its date? Dose and
   warnings are quoted from it word for word, never from memory."
2. "Is this for patients or the public, or for a doctor or pharmacist?" Ask only when the
   card and request do not show it.
3. "Which country is it for?" Ask only when the label or the patient wording depends on it
   and neither the card nor the label shows it.

## Never

- Ask whether an event is related to the product, serious, expected or reportable. The
  company's safety function decides that.
- Ask for the patient's or reporter's name, age or history.
- Put any question ahead of the flag.
