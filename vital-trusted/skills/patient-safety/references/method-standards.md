| S2 | "Other observations" include exposure associated with pregnancy or breastfeeding; lack of efficacy or lack of effect; overdose, abuse, misuse, medication error, occupational exposure; and off-label use. They can occur with or without an adverse event. | ICH E2D(R1), section 2.1.5 and 5.1.3 (same source as S1). | ICH regions | VERIFIED | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Used so that a pregnancy, an overdose or "it stopped working" is flagged even with no symptom described. |  |
| S3 | A case needs four minimum criteria to be submitted (an adverse event or other observation, a suspect product, an identifiable patient, an identifiable reporter). Cases missing a criterion do not qualify yet, and "due diligence should be exercised to collect the missing criteria". | ICH E2D(R1), section 2.2 (same source as S1). | ICH regions | VERIFIED | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Used only to stop the skill saying "not enough detail to report". Collecting what is missing is the safety function's job, so the flag goes on whatever detail is present. |  |
| S4 | The reporting clock starts (day zero) "on the date when any personnel of the MAH (including third parties, such as service providers and other contractual partners, acting on behalf of the MAH) obtains sufficient information" to meet the minimum criteria. Cases meeting regional criteria for expedited reporting go to the authority "as soon as possible, but not later than 15 calendar days after day zero". | ICH E2D(R1), sections 2.3 and 5.2 (same source as S1). | ICH regions; regional and local rules decide which cases are expedited. | VERIFIED | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Used to explain, in plain words, why the person passes information on quickly. The 15 days is a company-to-authority limit, never the person's deadline; the company procedure sets the internal one. |  |
| S5 | Digital platforms (social media, websites, forums, chat rooms, apps): the company screens platforms under its responsibility; it is not expected to screen platforms not under its responsibility, but adverse events it identifies are recorded, managed and assessed. If the company becomes aware of a report in a non-company-sponsored digital medium, it is assessed. | ICH E2D(R1), sections 4.3, 4.3.1 and 4.3.2 (same source as S1). | S7 | US: each applicant must "promptly review all adverse drug experience information obtained or otherwise received by the applicant from any source, foreign or domestic, including information derived from commercial marketing experience", and must have written procedures. Serious and unexpected experiences go to FDA "as soon as possible but no later than 15 calendar days from initial receipt of the information by the applicant". | 21 CFR 314.80(a), (b) and (c)(1)(i), eCFR, current text read 20 October 2026. | US, drugs with an approved application under part 314. | S8 | EU: reports from non-medical sources (for example the lay press or other media) are managed as spontaneous reports. A suspected adverse reaction associated with a quality defect or a falsified product is submitted as a valid case, and the company should have a system to investigate suspected quality defects in a timely way. Special situations include use in pregnancy or breastfeeding, use in children or the elderly, overdose, abuse, misuse, medication error, occupational exposure and lack of therapeutic efficacy. | GVP Module VI Rev 2, sections VI.B.1.1.3, VI.C.2.2.4 and VI.B.6 (same source as S5). | EU | PARTIAL | Confirmed 20 October 2026. EU submission timeframes (15 days serious, 90 days non-serious) are quoted in GVP VI.C.3 from Directive 2001/83/EC Articles 107(3) and 107a(4); the Directive itself was not read `[CHECK]`. Not given to the person as a deadline in any case. |  |
| S9 | Market research industry guidance: "Adverse Events" covers adverse events, special reporting situations (including overdose, off-label use, misuse, abuse, medication errors, lack of or unexpected effect, interactions) and product quality complaints (for example damaged packaging, wrong strength or color, broken delivery devices, counterfeits). The company's safety function "is accountable for ... determining the severity of each Adverse Event, and for assessing causality"; agencies do not. Agencies report to the company's safety function "within one business day" of becoming aware. Events reported by a carer, relative or friend are passed on without that individual's personal details. Where AI is used (moderation, transcription, translation), human oversight is built into the reporting process. | EPHMRA Adverse Event Reporting Guidelines, revised July 2026, sections 3.1.4, 4.2.3, 10.8, 12.5 and Appendix A. ABPI and BHBIA Guidance notes on collecting adverse events, product complaints and special reporting situations during market research, version 5.0, February 2021 (one business day). | Industry guidance, not law. EPHMRA: healthcare market research commissioned by the product's authorization holder, global. ABPI and BHBIA: UK. | PARTIAL | EPHMRA confirmed 20 October 2026 from the July 2026 PDF. ABPI and BHBIA: the one-business-day line confirmed from the February 2021 PDF; the rest of that document not read, and whether it has been superseded `[CHECK]`. The one business day is the agency-to-company step in market research; it is never presented as the person's deadline. |  |
