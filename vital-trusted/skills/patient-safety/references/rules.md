# Primary rules behind this skill

Each rule names its primary source, the date and how it was confirmed. A rule marked
`[CHECK]` has not been confirmed against the primary document and is applied as good
practice, not cited as a requirement (TR-H8). Jurisdiction is stated for each.

Last reviewed: 20 October 2026.

## The rule that governs first

| Id | Rule | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 | Whether an event is related to the product, whether it is serious or expected, whether it must be reported, to whom and by when, is decided by the company's safety (pharmacovigilance) function under its own written procedure. The procedure sets the internal timing for passing information on. | The person's own company safety reporting procedure. US: 21 CFR 314.80(b) requires the applicant to "develop written procedures for the surveillance, receipt, evaluation, and reporting of postmarketing adverse drug experiences" (see S7). | Owner-stated design rule (concept §8.3, TR-H6, TR-H11). The procedure is never assumed or named; the person is pointed to it. |

The public rules below explain why a flag matters and what kinds of information count.
They are never used to decide seriousness, causality, expectedness or reportability on the
company's behalf, and no timeframe below is ever given to the person as their deadline.

## Part B: what counts as a possible safety report, and why it is passed on promptly

| Id | Rule | Primary source | Scope | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | An adverse event is "any untoward medical occurrence in a patient exposed to a medicinal product and which does not necessarily have a causal relationship with the medicinal product", including an abnormal laboratory finding, symptom or disease "whether or not considered causally related". | ICH E2D(R1) Guideline on post-approval safety data, final version adopted 15 September 2025, section 2.1.1. EMA copy EMA/CHMP/ICH/59123/2024, final adoption by CHMP 18 September 2025, in effect in the EU 18 March 2026. | ICH regions. EU: EMA's implementation strategy (EMA/11141/2026, adopted by PRAC 20 February 2026) allows a transition up to 18 September 2026, after which E2D(R1) applies. US and Swiss adoption dates `[CHECK]`. | Confirmed 20 October 2026 from the EMA copy of the final guideline and the EMA implementation strategy. |
| S2 | "Other observations" include exposure associated with pregnancy or breastfeeding; lack of efficacy or lack of effect; overdose, abuse, misuse, medication error, occupational exposure; and off-label use. They can occur with or without an adverse event. | ICH E2D(R1), section 2.1.5 and 5.1.3 (same source as S1). | ICH regions | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Used so that a pregnancy, an overdose or "it stopped working" is flagged even with no symptom described. |
| S3 | A case needs four minimum criteria to be submitted (an adverse event or other observation, a suspect product, an identifiable patient, an identifiable reporter). Cases missing a criterion do not qualify yet, and "due diligence should be exercised to collect the missing criteria". | ICH E2D(R1), section 2.2 (same source as S1). | ICH regions | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Used only to stop the skill saying "not enough detail to report". Collecting what is missing is the safety function's job, so the flag goes on whatever detail is present. |
| S4 | The reporting clock starts (day zero) "on the date when any personnel of the MAH (including third parties, such as service providers and other contractual partners, acting on behalf of the MAH) obtains sufficient information" to meet the minimum criteria. Cases meeting regional criteria for expedited reporting go to the authority "as soon as possible, but not later than 15 calendar days after day zero". | ICH E2D(R1), sections 2.3 and 5.2 (same source as S1). | ICH regions; regional and local rules decide which cases are expedited. | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Used to explain, in plain words, why the person passes information on quickly. The 15 days is a company-to-authority limit, never the person's deadline; the company procedure sets the internal one. |
| S5 | Digital platforms (social media, websites, forums, chat rooms, apps): the company screens platforms under its responsibility; it is not expected to screen platforms not under its responsibility, but adverse events it identifies are recorded, managed and assessed. Unsolicited cases from the internet or digital media are handled as spontaneous reports. If the company becomes aware of a report in a non-company-sponsored digital medium, it is assessed. | ICH E2D(R1), sections 4.3, 4.3.1 and 4.3.2 (same source as S1). EMA Guideline on good pharmacovigilance practices (GVP) Module VI Rev 2, EMA/873138/2011 Rev 2, adopted 28 July 2017, in effect 22 November 2017, section VI.B.1.1.4. | ICH regions; GVP for the EU | Confirmed 20 October 2026 from both documents. EMA has announced a revision of GVP Module VI to integrate E2D(R1) (implementation strategy, 2026); check for a newer revision before relying on GVP wording `[CHECK]`. |
| S6 | Market research programs: the company should review all information received in a market research program for adverse events; those it becomes aware of are recorded, managed and assessed for reporting. Safety reports from market research and patient support programs are treated as solicited reports. | ICH E2D(R1), section 4.6 (same source as S1). GVP Module VI Rev 2, section VI.C.2.2.11 (same source as S5). | ICH regions; GVP for the EU | Confirmed 20 October 2026. |
| S7 | US: each applicant must "promptly review all adverse drug experience information obtained or otherwise received by the applicant from any source, foreign or domestic, including information derived from commercial marketing experience", and must have written procedures. An adverse drug experience includes one "whether or not considered drug related", from overdose, abuse or withdrawal, and "any failure of expected pharmacological action". Serious and unexpected experiences go to FDA "as soon as possible but no later than 15 calendar days from initial receipt of the information by the applicant". | 21 CFR 314.80(a), (b) and (c)(1)(i), eCFR, current text read 20 October 2026. | US, drugs with an approved application under part 314. Biologics (21 CFR 600.80) and devices (21 CFR part 803) `[CHECK]`, not read. | Confirmed 20 October 2026 from eCFR. |
| S8 | EU: reports from non-medical sources (for example the lay press or other media) are managed as spontaneous reports. A suspected adverse reaction associated with a quality defect or a falsified product is submitted as a valid case, and the company should have a system to investigate suspected quality defects in a timely way. Special situations include use in pregnancy or breastfeeding, use in children or the elderly, overdose, abuse, misuse, medication error, occupational exposure and lack of therapeutic efficacy. | GVP Module VI Rev 2, sections VI.B.1.1.3, VI.C.2.2.4 and VI.B.6 (same source as S5). | EU | Confirmed 20 October 2026. EU submission timeframes (15 days serious, 90 days non-serious) are quoted in GVP VI.C.3 from Directive 2001/83/EC Articles 107(3) and 107a(4); the Directive itself was not read `[CHECK]`. Not given to the person as a deadline in any case. |
| S9 | Market research industry guidance: "Adverse Events" covers adverse events, special reporting situations (including overdose, off-label use, misuse, abuse, medication errors, lack of or unexpected effect, interactions) and product quality complaints (for example damaged packaging, wrong strength or color, broken delivery devices, counterfeits). The company's safety function "is accountable for ... determining the severity of each Adverse Event, and for assessing causality"; agencies do not. Agencies report to the company's safety function "within one business day" of becoming aware. Events reported by a carer, relative or friend are passed on without that individual's personal details. Where AI is used (moderation, transcription, translation), human oversight is built into the reporting process. | EPHMRA Adverse Event Reporting Guidelines, revised July 2026, sections 3.1.4, 4.2.3, 10.8, 12.5 and Appendix A. ABPI and BHBIA Guidance notes on collecting adverse events, product complaints and special reporting situations during market research, version 5.0, February 2021 (one business day). | Industry guidance, not law. EPHMRA: healthcare market research commissioned by the product's authorization holder, global. ABPI and BHBIA: UK. | EPHMRA confirmed 20 October 2026 from the July 2026 PDF. ABPI and BHBIA: the one-business-day line confirmed from the February 2021 PDF; the rest of that document not read, and whether it has been superseded `[CHECK]`. The one business day is the agency-to-company step in market research; it is never presented as the person's deadline. |

| Id | Rule | Primary source | Scope | Status |

## How to use these rules in output

- In what the person reads, give the reason in plain words: "safety rules count the time
  from when anyone in the company first sees this, so pass it on as soon as you can, in
  the time your safety procedure sets." Cite the guideline only if asked.
- Never state a timeframe from S4, S7, S8 or S9 as the person's deadline. The company
  procedure sets it (S0). If the person asks "how long do I have", answer: "your safety
  reporting procedure sets that; if you cannot find it, ask your safety function today."
- Never use S1 to S9 to decide that something is or is not reportable, serious, expected
  or related. They show why the flag is raised, not what happens to it.
- Never extend a rule beyond its scope. S7 is US drugs; S9 is industry guidance for
  market research, not a legal requirement on the person.
- A `[CHECK]` rule is described as good practice, never as a requirement.
