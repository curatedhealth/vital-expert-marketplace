# Output format: safety flag

Shown to the person first, before the work they asked for, whenever Part B finds
something. Plain words, no rule ids, no guideline names (TR-H13). It is a note to the
person, not a safety report and not a check output, so it does not carry the closing
statement.

Language: give the flag in the language the person writes in, with the same content and
order as below. Name the procedures in plain words in that language; if the person uses
their company's own terms, use theirs. The quoted event words stay as they appear in the
input.

## Flag (one or more mentions)

```
Before the draft: something to pass to your safety reporting procedure

<Source line, see below> mentions:
- "<exact words from the input, without patient identifiers>" (<product name and strength, as given>): <kind in plain words, e.g. possible side effect>
- "<exact words>" (<product and strength>): <kind, e.g. pregnancy while on treatment>

Please pass this to your safety reporting procedure as soon as you can, in the time it
sets. <Forwarding line, see below>

<Fixed sentence, see below>
```

First line: "Before the draft" when a draft or summary follows. In a check summary, or
when nothing is drafted after the flag, it reads "Before anything else: something to pass
to your safety reporting procedure".

## What the flag keeps and leaves out

This is the one list. Every other file points here.

- **Never repeated, in a flag or in a summary that mentions the event:** the patient's
  name, initials, age, date of birth, record or patient number, town, and exact dates
  about the patient (date of the event, of an admission, of a dose).
- **Kept:** the product name and strength as given ("Dermavex 2% cream"), the event
  words, who reported it and the setting ("a nurse at Hillcrest clinic", "Dr. Morel,
  at her hospital"). The date of the email, call or notes in the source line is the
  date of the report, not a patient detail, and stays. The product and strength go in
  brackets after the quote when the quoted words do not already name them; the
  strength only when the input gives it.
- When the event words carry an identifier, quote the part without it, or describe the
  event in plain words. The forwarding line carries the original, with every detail,
  to the safety procedure.

Source line, by source. It keeps who reported the event and the setting, and leaves out
the patient's identifiers (list above):

- **Default:** "<Who reported it, with role and place if given>, in <the email | the call
  notes | the transcript> of <date>", for example "A nurse at Hillcrest clinic, in Dr.
  Weber's email of 14 October 2026". No date in the input: name the sender and the
  source only.
- **Said at a meeting:** "<Who said it, with role if given>, at <the meeting, e.g. the
  investigator meeting>, as relayed in <your slide | your notes | your summary>".
- **A social post or comment:** "The <post | comment> by <handle> on <where>, <date>".
- **Market research:** "<A carer | a patient | a doctor>, in <interview or group label>
  of <the study, as the person names it>". The carer's or relative's own details stay
  out (S9).

Fixed sentence, by what was reported:

- **An event that happened to a person** (side effect, overdose, medication error that
  reached the patient, lack of effect): "I have not judged whether it is related to
  <product>, how serious it is, or whether it needs reporting. Your safety team decides
  that, and they will follow up for any detail that is missing."
- **A product defect with no patient event** (a cracked cap, a dial that jumps, empty
  blister pockets, with nothing said about a person): "I have not judged what the problem
  is or whether it needs reporting. Your quality and safety functions decide that, and
  they will follow up for any detail that is missing." No "whether it is related"
  sentence: nothing happened to a person to relate it to.
- **A use or exposure with no event** (off-label use reported for a specific patient or
  patients, a pregnancy, a near miss caught in time): "I have not judged whether this use is outside
  the label or whether it needs reporting. Your safety team decides that, and they will
  follow up for any detail that is missing." For a pregnancy or a near miss, leave out
  "whether this use is outside the label".

When one flag lists an event and a product defect, give one combined sentence instead of
the two, so nothing is said twice: "I have not judged whether it is related to
<product>, how serious it is, what the problem with the <product | pen | pack> is, or
whether either needs reporting. Your safety and quality functions decide that, and they
will follow up for any detail that is missing." Other combinations: use the event
sentence alone; it covers a use or exposure listed with it.

Optional lines, only when they apply:

```
This came from an AI summary; your safety team will want the original <recording | transcript | notes>.
If <product> is not one of your company's products, your safety procedure will say whether anything needs to happen.
For a product quality problem or a device failure, also pass it to your complaint procedure, as your procedure sets.
If this concerns a patient in a clinical trial, the trial's own safety procedure may set a different route: follow it.
```

The trial line is for an event raised at an investigator meeting, in a site
communication, or about patients described as in a study.

Forwarding line, one per flag, by source:

- **Default** (an email, notes, a transcript, a complaint, a call log, a slide that
  relays a meeting): "Forward the original <email | notes | transcript | slide and any
  notes from the meeting> as received; your safety procedure decides what personal
  details it needs."
- **A social post or web comment:** "Send a screenshot or link of the original <post |
  comment> as received; your safety procedure decides what personal details it needs."
- **Market research, where a carer, relative or friend describes the event** (S9):
  "Forward the original <transcript | notes> as received, leaving out that person's own
  details unless your procedure asks for them."

Do not ask the person to strip anything else before reporting.

The quoted words name the event only. When they carry an identifier from the list
above, quote only the part that describes what happened, or describe it in plain words.
The forwarding line carries the original, with every detail, to the safety procedure.

If asked why it is urgent, one line:

```
Safety rules usually count the time from when anyone in the company first has the information, so pass it on as soon as you can. Your procedure sets the exact time.
```

## A question about a use that has not happened (not a flag)

A use a doctor asks about for a specific patient that has not happened yet ("can I give it to my 15-year-old patient?") is not a flag: it is a medical information question, so point to medical information. A use that already happened is a report and gets the flag. One plain line instead:

```
This is a question about a use that has not happened yet, so it goes to your medical information function, which answers questions about specific patients.
```

## A practice trend with no individual (not a flag)

Off-label use described as a general pattern, with no patient and nothing that happened
to anyone ("doctors are using it in teens"), does not get the flag (TR-H14). Nor does a
general observation from several doctors with no patient described ("fatigue seems more
common in older patients"). One plain line instead:

```
<Source> describes how <product> is being used ("<exact words>"). Pass this to your medical information function, and to your safety function as your company's process sets.
<Source> describes a pattern across their patients ("<exact words>"). Pass this to your safety function and to medical information, as your company's process sets.
```

If any patient is named or described, or anything that happened to one, it is a flag,
not this line. Pair: "Three doctors said fatigue seems more common in their older
patients" gets this line; "Dr. Morel said one of her older patients stopped after two
weeks because of fatigue" gets the flag.

## Immediate danger (always first)

```
If someone may be in immediate danger, contact emergency services and follow your company's urgent escalation route now.
```

## Nothing found (only when the person asked, or at full depth)

```
I read <the email | the notes | the transcript> for anything to pass to your safety reporting procedure and found nothing. A read is not a guarantee: if you know of something I missed, pass it on.
```
