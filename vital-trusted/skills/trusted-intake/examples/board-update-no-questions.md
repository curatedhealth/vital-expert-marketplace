# Worked example: a request that already carries enough

Invented case for teaching. "Zelorastat" and the affiliate are fictional.

## Request

"Draft a one-page update for our Swiss affiliate leadership team on the zelorastat
launch readiness, internal only, from the notes below. Prose, no bullets."

> (Notes, supplied) Launch readiness: reimbursement dossier submitted; field team 80%
> hired; two key opinion leader meetings held; supply confirmed for Q1.

## Reading the request

| Field | What the request shows | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Reader | Swiss affiliate leadership team | stated |
| Market | Switzerland, internal | stated |
| Product status | Not stated; not needed, the update makes no product claims | unknown, does not change the answer |
| Use | Internal only | stated |
| Deliverable | One page, prose | stated |
| Material | Launch readiness notes | read |
| Stakes | Leadership | read |
| Inputs | Internal notes | read; internal planning notes, no confidential third-party or patient data described |

Nothing missing would change the draft. No questions.

## What the person sees

> Working from: Swiss affiliate leadership team, internal only, from your launch notes.

Then the draft.

## Why this is the right intake

- Zero questions: every field that could change the draft was given.
- Product status was left unknown rather than asked, because the update says nothing
  about the product's uses (C3, the question that changes nothing).
- The format preference was followed, not questioned (TR-H9).
- Internal planning notes do not raise the data question on their own. If the notes had
  named a partner's confidential terms, the question would have been asked first.
