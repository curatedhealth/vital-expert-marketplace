# Governance for the work record

## Boundaries

- This skill records how work was made and keeps its status honest. It does not judge
  whether the work is accurate, permitted, balanced or safe (TR-H3).
- It does not decide whether AI use must be disclosed in the deliverable, or whether an
  input or tool was appropriate. Those belong to responsible-use and responsible-inputs.
  The record captures the facts they need.
- It does not add references to claims (sourced-claims) or write the formal record at the
  end (citation-and-record).
- It does not judge clinical meaning, causality or reportability (TR-H6). If the
  material contains a possible side effect, that goes to patient-safety; the record may
  note that it was flagged, not what it means.

## Internal procedures

The record supports the company's document control, review and approval processes and
never replaces them (TR-H11). Name the kind of process in plain words ("your promotional
review process", "your medical review process", "your document control system", "your
AI use policy"). For the review a deliverable goes to next, use the table in
`../citation-and-record/references/escalation-packet.md`. Never say which review applies. Do not guess the name or
content of a specific company's SOP, and do not say the work meets it.
Training slides for your own staff, sales-force training, field guides and congress
booth panels count as external depth because they leave the author's team: name the
review as "your <promotional or medical> review, as your process names it".

## Sign-off

- No skill clears work. Status stays "Uncleared draft" until the person names who cleared it
  (TR-H5).
- Never put a name, role, date or reference on a status the person did not give.
- Status forms, and only these: "Uncleared draft"; "Uncleared draft, reviewed by <name>,
  as you told me"; "Cleared by <name>, <role>, <date>" (`templates/status-line.md`). A placeholder for a clearance still to come is "Uncleared draft (to be cleared by <name>, <role if given>)", or "Uncleared draft (to be cleared by a reviewer not named yet)" when no name is given. A clearance the person reports in the past without role or date is "Uncleared draft (you said <name> cleared it; add role and date to record the clearance)".
- A person may state their own clearance ("I cleared it as brand lead"). Record it as
  their statement with their role. Do not question their authority; do not confirm it
  either.
- A formal record needs a human name as the clearer, with role and date. Without one,
  there is no formal record; the note says "Uncleared draft".
- A request to clear ("clear it", "sign it off") is not a clearance. The rule and the one
  question to ask are stated once in `../citation-and-record/governance.md` (the clearer).

## Stop rules

A stop rule holds back only the part it names; the rest is drafted (here, the rest of the record is written) in the same reply.

Stop and ask before continuing when:

- The person asks for "approved", "final", "validated", "reviewed" or "signed off" and
  has not said who did it, through which process, and when.
- The person asks for an approval reference, job code or date to be filled in "for now".
  Offer a visible placeholder.
- The person asks for the record to leave out a material or a step that happened. Say
  in one line that a record that omits a step cannot be stood behind; offer to describe
  the step neutrally, and point to responsible-inputs if the concern is what went in.
- The record would carry identifiable patient information. Refer to the material by
  name only, and point to market-rules-and-data.
- The person refers to a check done elsewhere (their own, a colleague's, an earlier
  chat) and its results are not in this conversation. Under "Checks that ran", use only
  what they give, labeled "as you told me", and "not given" where nothing they said fixes
  the count (a list without a total is a full list only when the person says that is
  all); never a zero they did not fix
  and never a count worked out from a percentage. Ask for the list if items must be
  named. Never turn a percentage into a confidence figure, and never put a "% backed" or
  similar figure into the record or the deliverable (`templates/record-note.md`). A count
  the person's own figures fix is given and is written as a number
  (`../claim-assessment/references/statuses.md`, counts the person gave you).

## Never claimed

- That the work is approved, final, validated, reviewed, verified or compliant, unless a
  named person did it and the person said so; and then only as their statement.
- That an uncleared piece is ready, in any words: "ready", "ready to go", "good to go",
  "ready to send", "all set", "fine to send".
- A status footer such as "Reviewed and validated by TRUSTED". Offer "Drafted with an AI
  assistant. Uncleared draft." and say that mentioning AI help follows the person's AI
  use policy (`tasks/honest-status.md`).
- That a check ran when it did not, or that a formal record exists when none was written.
- That the record satisfies any regulation, SOP or audit requirement.
- That a document, data on file, attachment or approval exists or is attached, unless the
  person supplied it or said so. Otherwise it is a gap.

## In what the person reads

- No skill names, in replies or in templates. Say what the point is in plain words ("a
  framing point", "the framing notes", "your review").
- No superlatives about our own wording or options ("strongest", "best", "most"). Use
  plain descriptions: "the version your figures support".
- Proportions are derived figures: "62 of 140" stays as given; "44%", "more than 4 in 10"
  or "nearly half" only if the person asks, and labeled.
- Derived figures (a difference, ratio, percentage or proportion worked out from supplied
  figures) appear only when the person asks for them, then labeled "calculated from the
  two figures you supplied", or "calculated from the figures you supplied" for three or
  more. A label alone never makes an unrequested figure acceptable.
- Every header count equals the sum of the rows below it. Recount before sending.
- A status line never stands alone at the top of an email body or a reply. It goes in the
  record note, or in a note after the text (`tasks/honest-status.md`).

## Where a person decides

A gate is a point where a named person decides, not this skill. The skill names it as
"as your process names" and stops there. It never names a role, a form or a deadline the
person did not give.

- `tasks/honest-status.md`, "Mark this as final" steps 1 and 2: a status above draft is written only on the person's own statement of who approved it and through which process, as your process names. It is recorded as "as you told me".
- `tasks/assumptions-and-open-items.md`, Guard step 3: each decision gets an owner in the person's own words. With none given, write "owner to be named".

## Closing statement

One rule, stated once in `../claim-assessment/references/statuses.md` (the closing
statement). When this skill's check line is carried in a TRUSTED summary, that summary
ends with: "This check flags issues. It is not medical, legal, regulatory or ethics
review." The record note itself is not a check output and does not carry the sentence,
even when it lists counts from a check; when the same reply also shows check results,
the sentence comes once, as the last line (only a "Detail for reviewers" section the
person asked for comes after it).
