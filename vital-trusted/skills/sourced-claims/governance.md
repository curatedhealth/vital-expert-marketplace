# Governance for sourced claims

## Boundaries

- This skill decides which claims need a source, which kind of source fits the use, and
  whether a citation is specific enough to find. It does not establish that a claim is
  true or that a source says what is claimed (TR-H3).
- Matching statements to the supplied material is `grounded-statements`. Comparing a claim
  with its passage, by hand, is `claim-assessment`. A source card for a specific source
  (what it can carry, peer review, funding, currency), by hand and with no grade, is
  `source-assessment`. Plain reference lines and the record, by hand, are
  `citation-and-record`.
- Whether a claim is permitted at all (label, promotional boundary, comparative claims)
  is `claims-and-label`. Whether evidence is shown in proportion is `balanced-evidence`.
- Citation style is the person's choice (TR-H9).

## Internal procedures

This skill prepares claims for the company's own review. It never says that a claim is
substantiated to the standard of a code, or that a source is acceptable to the
company's reviewers (TR-H11). When work leaves the company, the note names the kind of
process it goes through next in plain words: "your promotional review process", "your
medical review process", "your process for providing substantiation or data on file on
request". Do not guess the name or content of a specific company's procedure.

## Stop rules

Stop and ask before continuing when:

Each stop holds back only the part it names. Draft the rest of the request in the same
reply, with a gap where the held part would go (draft first).

- The person asks for references to be added and supplied none. Decline to write
  references, explain in one plain sentence, and give the rest of the work in the same
  reply with the list of claims that need sources and the kind each needs
  (`tasks/specific-citations.md`).
- The person asks to "just put in a typical reference" or "something that looks right".
  Offer a visible gap marker, for example
  `[GAP: not supplied; reference for the trial publication; the citation details from you]`.
- The person asks whether a source is "good enough" or "acceptable" for a payer,
  regulator or review committee. Say what that type can and cannot carry, and that the
  decision belongs to their review process; offer `source-assessment` for a plain read of what the source can and cannot carry. Neither
  skill gives the source a grade.
- The person's own supplied data contradict a claim in the draft. Say so plainly, and
  ask before putting the contradicting figure into anything external.
- A source appears to contain identifiable patient information (hand to
  `market-rules-and-data` and `responsible-inputs`).

## Never claimed

- That a claim is substantiated, verified, true or adequately referenced.
- That a source is reliable, valid, peer reviewed, current or not retracted. No tool
  checked any source, and no grade is given.
- That a citation exists as written, unless built from the supplied document.
- That the work meets any code of practice or regulator's expectation.

## Where a person decides

A gate is a point where a named person decides, not this skill. The skill names it as
"as your process names" and stops there. It never names a role, a form or a deadline the
person did not give.

- `tasks/every-claim-sourced.md`, Repair step 3: a claim with a named source that was not supplied waits for the document. The person supplies it, or the review your process names decides.
- `tasks/source-type-fit.md`, Repair step 3: whether a weaker kind of source may carry a claim for this reader is decided at the review your process names.

## Closing statement

When this skill's check lines go into a TRUSTED summary, that summary ends with: "This
check flags issues. It is not medical, legal, regulatory or ethics review." Direct
replies and drafts do not carry it.
