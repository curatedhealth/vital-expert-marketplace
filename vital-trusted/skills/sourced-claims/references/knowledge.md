# Knowledge: sourced-claims

## Migrated rules

# Primary rules behind this skill

Each rule names its primary source, the date and how it was confirmed. A rule marked
`[CHECK]` has not been confirmed against the primary document, or its scope or currency
is open; it is applied as good practice, not cited as a requirement (TR-H8).

Industry codes bind their members in the markets they cover. Outside that scope, apply
the rule as good practice only. The company's own review process decides which code
applies to a piece of work (TR-H11).

Last reviewed: 20 October 2026.

## How to use these rules in output

- In what the person reads, state the practice in plain words ("a payer will expect to
  see the data behind this figure"). Name a rule only if they ask (TR-H13).
- Never extend a rule beyond its scope. R1 to R4 are UK code rules; R9 is US payor
  communications only; R10 is journal practice.
- Never say a draft meets a code. Say what is sourced and what is open.

# Failure patterns

## Sourcing

**S1 "Studies show".** Collective authority with no study named.
Why: the reader cannot find, weigh or check "studies"; a reviewer will ask which.
Before: "Studies show Lumivra improves quality of life."
After: "In <named study, supplied>, …" or "Lumivra improved quality of life [GAP: not supplied — the study this rests on — supply the publication]." (TR-H1, TR-H2, R1)

**S2 "Experts agree".** Consensus asserted with no panel, survey or guideline.
Before: "Experts agree early treatment is essential."
After: name the guideline or survey and its date, or gap it; if it is an advisory board, say who and how many. (TR-H1, R6)

**S3 "Per FDA guidance".** A regulator invoked with no document.
Before: "Per FDA guidance, payers can receive this data."
After: "[GAP: not supplied — title and date of the guidance meant — name the document]". (TR-H8, R9)

**S4 The unsourced superlative.** "Leading", "first", "only", "most prescribed".
Why: absolute claims need qualification and substantiation; they are the first thing a reviewer checks.
Before: "Lumivra, the leading treatment for moderate psoriasis."
After: the specific measure, market, period and data provider ("highest share of new patients in <market>, <period> (<data provider>, supplied)"), or gap it. (R6, TR-H1)

**S5 The undated "current".** "Current standard of care", "latest data", "now reimbursed".
After: "as of <date>, per <source>", or `[GAP: out of date or unclear date — …]`. (R2, R6)

**S6 The figure with no source.** A number with nothing beside it.
Before: "Condition X affects 1 in 20 adults."
After: the number with its source, or the gap. (TR-H1, R1)

**S7 The opinion sourced to look like evidence.** A recommendation given a reference it does not need, or a plan stated as fact.
Before: "Payers will prioritize Brand A (internal strategy deck)."
After: "We expect payers to prioritize Brand A, because <sourced reason or gap>." (TR-H2)

**S8 The source named but not supplied.** "According to the ZELORA-2 publication" when the publication was not given.
Why: you cannot tell what the paper says from its name.
After: ask for the document; keep the claim with `[GAP: not supplied — the publication — send it so the claim can be matched]`. (TR-H1)

## Source type

**T1 News as efficacy evidence.** A competitor's trial result taken from a news story.
Before: "Corvanta reduced flares by 60% (Example Health Wire, 4 May 2026)."
After: "Example Health Wire reported on 4 May 2026 that Corvanta reduced flares by 60% [GAP: not supplied — the trial publication or registry results — find the primary record before using this externally]." (R10, R6)

**T2 Blog as fact.** An analyst or physician blog used for a market or competitor fact.
After: attribute it as a view ("one commentator wrote…") or find the primary record; never use it for a factual claim leaving the company. (R6)

**T3 Press release as comparison.** A competitor's or one's own press release used for a head-to-head claim.
After: the press release supports "the company announced"; the comparison needs the study. (R5, R6)

**T4 Bare data on file for a payer.** "Data on file" with no name, date or route to see it, on a payer slide.
Why: a payer cannot examine it, and the company may be asked to provide it.
After: "Data on file: <specific data set>, <company>, <date>; available on request through <your medical information process>", and only if it will be provided. (R1, R8, R9)

**T5 Abstract where a paper exists.** A conference abstract cited for results later published in full.
After: cite the full publication; if only the abstract exists, label it a conference abstract. (R10)

**T6 Preprint as settled.** A preprint cited without saying so.
After: label it a preprint; do not use it as the basis for a claim to a doctor, payer or patient without your medical review deciding. (R10)

**T7 Internal document as evidence.** "As shown in the brand plan" for an external fact.
Why: the internal document repeats a claim; it is not the evidence for it.
After: cite the primary source the brand plan relied on, or gap it. (TR-H1, R10)

## Citations

**C1 The remembered reference.** A plausible author, year and journal written from memory.
Why: the most damaging failure in this skill; it looks like evidence.
Before: "(Meyer et al., 2023)". After: cite only supplied sources; otherwise a gap. (TR-H1)

**C2 The partial citation.** "Smith 2023" with no title, journal or identifier.
After: complete from the supplied document, or gap the missing fields. (R7, R10)

**C3 The unnamed guidance.** "EMA guidelines", "NICE says".
After: issuing body, title, document type, date, section. (TR-H8)

**C4 Citation to the paper, not the passage.** A long publication cited for a specific figure with no table or section.
After: add the table, figure or section the figure comes from. (R3)

**C5 The paraphrase in quotation marks.** Quoted words the source did not use, or a quote with no source.
After: quote exactly with its precise source, or paraphrase without quotation marks. (R4)

**C6 The filled-in DOI.** A DOI, PubMed ID or year completed from memory "to finish the reference".
After: leave the field as a gap until it comes from the document. (TR-H1)

**C7 References added to order.** "Add references to these claims" answered with a list of plausible references when none were supplied.
After: decline in one plain sentence, list the claims that need sources and the kind each needs, and gap them in the text. (TR-H1, TR-H4)
