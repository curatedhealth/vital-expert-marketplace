# Worked example: agency copy for an HCP email (full depth)

Invented case for teaching. "Lumivra" is a fictional product; no real company, study or
person.

## Request

"Brand manager here. The agency sent this copy for our HCP email, can you confirm it's
ok to send so I can approve it?

'Introducing Lumivra: the most effective once-daily option for moderate psoriasis. 9 out
of 10 patients saw clearer skin. Well tolerated, with side effects similar to placebo.
Ask your rep for samples.'"

No study, label or market was supplied. All seven letters are on. VERIFY is not
connected.

## Depth

Copy for doctors, written by an agency: it leaves the company. Full depth. The person
asked "is it OK to send", so the summary opens with what to fix and who decides.

## Lines collected (reference only, not shown to the person)

```
Tangible · substance: 0 statements too vague to act on, 0 passages of filler or repetition, 0 sections with no "so what" (not tool-checked)
Rule-compliant · comparisons: 1 comparative or superiority claim, 1 without a head-to-head source (not tool-checked)
Rule-compliant · market: market not stated, 1 statement that may not be allowed there (not tool-checked)
Rule-compliant · label and intended use: not checkable — no label supplied (2 statements affected)
Unbiased · balance: 3 benefit statements, 0 risk or limitation statements, 3 benefits with no risk or limitation nearby (not tool-checked)
Safe · safety statements: 1 statement on tolerability, not checkable against the label, label not supplied (not tool-checked)
True · invented details: 3 not in your material, 0 changed from the source, 0 where sources disagree (not tool-checked)
Ethical · what went into the assistant: 0 confidential, unpublished, client or patient items, policy not stated, 0 questions for your AI policy owner (not tool-checked)
Defensible · record: 0 sources listed, tools and AI used not named (not tool-checked)
Defensible · status: uncleared draft, 0 statements that call the draft approved, validated or final (not tool-checked)
```

## The summary as the person sees it

```
TRUSTED check: HCP email copy from the agency, for doctors. Draft, not yet reviewed.

Before it goes, fix these 4 things and answer 1 question. Your promotional review process decides whether it can be used.

Tangible        nothing flagged in what was checked
Rule-compliant  1 to fix, 1 question for you
                "The most effective once-daily option" compares Lumivra with every other option; nothing you supplied compares them directly.
                Which market is this for? It changes what the email may say and what it must include, including the sample offer.
Unbiased        1 to fix
                The email gives three benefits and no risk, limitation or safety information.
Safe            1 to fix
                "Well tolerated, with side effects similar to placebo" is a safety statement with no source and no label to compare it with.
True            3 missing
                "9 out of 10 patients saw clearer skin" has no study, measure, time point or comparison group in what you gave me.
                "The most effective" and "side effects similar to placebo" have no source.
Ethical         nothing flagged in what was checked
Defensible      1 missing
                No source is named for any claim, so a reviewer cannot trace them. Uncleared draft.

Open for a person
1. Which market the email is for.
2. The study behind "9 out of 10" and the tolerability statement, or they come out.
3. The approved label, so the indication and the safety wording can be compared with it.

Could not be checked
- Whether "moderate psoriasis" and the tolerability wording match the approved label: no label was supplied, and there is no label tool yet.
- Whether the claims match their sources: no sources were supplied and VERIFY is not connected. With both, VERIFY would test each of the 3 claims against the passage it rests on and check that the study has not been retracted or replaced.

Next step: this goes through your promotional review process before it is used.

This check flags issues. It is not medical, legal, regulatory or ethics review.
```

## Why it is written this way

- The question "can you confirm it's ok to send" is answered with what to fix and who
  decides, never yes or no (TR-H3, TR-H5, TR-H11).
- The tolerability sentence counts under Safe (a safety statement not compared with the
  label) and under True (no source), each for its own reason, and the note says which.
- The label check reads "could not be checked", not "nothing flagged" (TR-H4).
- The market is asked, not assumed (TR-H7). No country's rules are cited.
- The sample offer is not judged. It is named as something the market question
  affects, and the promotional review process decides.
