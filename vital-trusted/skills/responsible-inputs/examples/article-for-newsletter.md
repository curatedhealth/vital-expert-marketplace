# Worked example: a paid journal article for an external newsletter

Invented case for teaching. The journal, article and product are fictional.

## Request

"Here's the full PDF of a paid article from the Fictional Journal of Dermatology about
treatment options in psoriasis. Rewrite it in plain English for our customer newsletter
that goes to dermatologists, same structure, about the same length."

The pasted text carries "© 2026 Fictional Journal Press. All rights reserved."

## What the skill recognizes

- Copyrighted text from a subscription journal, pasted in full.
- The request is a close rework of the whole article ("same structure, same length"),
  not a summary.
- The result goes outside the company, to doctors.
- Stakes: high.

## Reply (as the person sees it)

> This is a full article from a subscription journal, and a same-length rewrite that
> follows its structure would still reproduce most of it for an external newsletter.
>
> What I can do instead: a short summary in our own words, around 200 words, with the
> full citation and a link to the article, and one brief quotation if you want it. If
> you need more of the original, your library or licensing team can say whether your
> license covers external use.
>
> Because the newsletter goes to dermatologists, it will also go through your
> promotional or medical review process before it is sent.
>
> Shall I write the summary?

## What it did not do

- It did not say whether the rewrite would be fair use or infringe.
- It did not assume the company's subscription covers external use.
- It did not refuse to help; it offered the route that gets the newsletter written.

## Check line (to trusted-check only)

```
Ethical · other people's work: 1 item reproduced in full or close to it, 1 licensed item, 1 question for your licensing process (asked, not checked)
```

## Behind the reply (reference only, not shown to the person)

Patterns avoided: P1, P3, P4. Rules: I9 (US, reproduction and derivative works; fair use
is a legal judgment), I10 and I12 `[CHECK]`. Neighbors: the summary itself follows
`grounded-statements`; authorship and AI disclosure on the newsletter are
`responsible-use`; the content claims are `claims-and-label`.
