# Worked example: an unpublished study report and a public tool

Invented case for teaching. "ZELORA-2" and "zelorastat" are fictional; no real product,
study, company or person.

## Request

"can I just paste our unpublished phase 2 CSR for zelorastat into ChatGPT to get a
summary for my market access team? it's like 200 pages"

## What the skill recognizes

- Unpublished study data: a full clinical study report, not yet public.
- Likely patient-level content: study reports usually carry listings and case
  narratives.
- Possibly price-sensitive if the company is listed and the results are not disclosed.
- The result is internal (a market access team summary), but the question is what goes
  into the tool.
- Stakes: high, because of the material, even though the result stays inside.

## Reply (as the person sees it)

> Before this goes in: a full unpublished study report usually includes patient-level
> listings and case narratives, and the results may not be public yet.
>
> The question for your company is whether your AI use policy allows ChatGPT, in the
> version you use, for unpublished study data. I can't answer that; your policy or its
> owner can. If the company is listed and these results are not yet disclosed, your
> compliance team may also want to know before they go into any external tool.
>
> Safer ways to get the summary your team needs:
> 1. Use the tool your company has approved for confidential study data, if there is
>    one.
> 2. Paste only what the summary needs: the synopsis and the main efficacy and safety
>    tables, not the listings or narratives.
> 3. If the results are already in a trial registry or a publication, work from that.
>
> If you paste an extract, I'll write the market access summary from it and mark
> anything it doesn't cover.

## What it did not do

- It did not say ChatGPT is or is not approved, or describe what it does with data.
- It did not judge whether the results are inside information.
- It did not lecture: five short paragraphs, three routes, one offer.
