# Gap rules applied to responsible use

> **Where the marker belongs.** Use the `[GAP: …]` marker only inside a drafted deliverable. In notes, open-item lists and explanations, write the gap as a plain sentence ("Still missing: …, from …"). See `trusted-capability-map/references/gap-rules.md`.

Same format and classes as every TRUSTED skill:
`[GAP: <class> — <what is missing> — <what would close it>]`

Gaps in this skill appear mostly in the use note and the check lines, not inside the
person's document. In what the person reads, prefer the plain question ("Who will read
this before it goes to the doctor?") over raw gap syntax (TR-H13). Inside a draft, a
placeholder the person must fill is fine: `[Reviewer: name, role]`.

| Class | Typical case in this skill |
|---|---|
| `needs a decision` | Who reviews before use; whether the company policy expects AI use to be disclosed; whether a law's disclosure duty applies (legal); who the authors are and how support is acknowledged (publications process); whether a term the community uses should stay |
| `not supplied` | The reviewer's name; the venue (journal, congress, HTA body, payer); the venue's instructions; the policy text the person refers to; the study's demographic table |
| `not checkable here` | What the company policy says, with no AI-policy companion and no text supplied |
| `conflicting` | The person says no review is needed, but the work is going to a doctor or the public; the byline names an expert but the notes say the draft was written by the brand team |
| `out of date or unclear date` | A policy or venue instruction quoted with no version or date |

Wording rules:

- Name the decision and who owns it, not the person: "reviewer before sending — to be
  named by you, through your medical information process", not "user skipped review".
- Never hint at the answer inside the gap ("probably no disclosure needed").
- Never fill a reviewer, author contribution or disclosure statement as a gap example
  with a realistic name.
