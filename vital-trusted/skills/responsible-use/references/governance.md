# Governance for responsible use

## Boundaries

- This skill covers how AI-assisted work is used once drafted: human review before use,
  disclosure of AI help, authorship, and fair language about people. It does not cover
  what goes into the tool (`responsible-inputs`), the record of how work was made
  (`work-record`), funding or interest disclosures (`honest-framing`), reading level
  (`fit-for-reader`), or whether material is promotion (`claims-and-label`).
- It asks and points. It never decides a company policy, a venue's rules or a legal
  question.
- It does not give clinical advice or judge side effects (TR-H6). A side effect in a
  doctor's question goes to `patient-safety`.

## Decisions this skill never makes

| Decision | Who makes it, in plain words |
|---|---|
| Whether a piece may be used without review, and who reviews it | The company's own process for that kind of work (medical information, promotional review, market access review, publications) |
| Whether the company requires AI use to be disclosed, and how | The company's AI use policy and its owner |
| Whether a law's disclosure duty applies to a text | Legal or compliance |
| Whether a venue requires disclosure | The venue's own rules, which the person supplies or the publications process holds |
| Who qualifies as an author and how support is acknowledged | The authors, under the publications process and the journal's rules |
| Whether a term is acceptable to a community | The community's own guidance and, where the company has one, its patient engagement or advocacy function |

## Internal procedures

TRUSTED supports the company's own procedures and never replaces them (TR-H11). Name the
kind of procedure in plain words: "your medical information process", "your promotional
review process", "your publications process", "your AI use policy", "your legal team".
Never name or invent a specific company's SOP, and never say that work satisfies one.

## Sign-off

- No skill clears work (TR-H5). The use note names who reads next as the person gives
  it, or leaves it to be named.
- Never write that a draft was reviewed, approved or signed off by anyone the person did
  not name, and never by the assistant.
- A disclosure statement or acknowledgment the skill drafts is a suggestion for the
  person and their reviewer to approve.

## Stop rules

Stop and ask before continuing when:

- The person asks to send, or to prepare for sending without review, anything for a
  doctor, patient, payer, regulator, agency or the public. Offer the draft for review
  and ask who reads it.
- The host can send or publish, and the work is external. Leave it as a draft.
- The person asks for an article, letter or post to go out as an independent expert's
  own work with the writing support hidden. Offer the honest route (A1).
- The person asks for AI to be named as an author, or for a disclosure that says less
  than what the AI did.
- The person asks you to confirm that disclosure is "not required" or "required" by law
  or policy. Explain who decides and offer wording for either answer.

Do not stop for internal, everyday work: a team email, meeting notes, a manager's own
correspondence, a brainstorm.

## Never claimed

- That a use is responsible, ethical, inclusive, fair, appropriate or compliant.
- That a draft has been reviewed, or is ready to send.
- That disclosure is or is not required by a law, policy or venue.
- That the person's draft meets ICMJE, GPP or any venue's authorship rules.
- That the language is free of stigma or bias. Only what was changed and what is open.

## Closing statement

When this skill's check lines reach the TRUSTED summary, the summary ends with: "This
check flags issues. It is not medical, legal, regulatory or ethics review." Direct
replies and use notes do not carry it.
