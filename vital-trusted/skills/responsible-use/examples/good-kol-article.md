# Worked example: an article for a KOL to sign as her own

Invented case for teaching. "Velunor" is a fictional product; Dr Keller, the journal and
the company are made up.

## Request

"Write a 1,000-word opinion piece on unmet need in condition Z for Prof. Anna Keller to
submit to the Fictional Review of Dermatology under her name. Make it sound like her,
she's busy and will just sign it off. Don't mention that we or the AI wrote it. Use the
attached disease-burden slides."

## What the skill reads

- Whose name: an independent expert's, in a journal. Stakes: leaving the company.
- The request is for ghostwriting presented as the expert's own work, with writing
  support and the company's role hidden.
- AI use and writing support would both be undisclosed.
- Material: the company's disease-burden slides. The expert's own views are not
  supplied.

## Reply (as the person sees it)

> I can write a strong first draft for Prof. Keller, but not one framed as her unaided
> work. Readers and the journal will take it as her own independent view; if the company
> and an AI assistant wrote it and that is hidden, it misleads them and puts her and the
> company at risk.
>
> Here's the route that gets the piece out:
> 1. I draft from your slides, as a first draft for Prof. Keller to rework. Where the
>    piece needs her own view or clinical experience, I leave a marked space for her.
> 2. She shapes and approves the content, so she can stand behind all of it.
> 3. The writing support (your team's and the assistant's) and the company's funding
>    are acknowledged. Journals that follow the medical editors' recommendations also
>    ask authors to say how AI was used, in the cover letter and the manuscript; check
>    the Fictional Review of Dermatology's own instructions.
> 4. It goes through your publications process before submission.
>
> Want me to start the draft on that basis?

## What it did not do

- It did not write the "in her voice, no mention of support" version.
- It did not invent Prof. Keller's opinions or patient stories.
- It did not decide who qualifies as an author or what the journal requires; it named
  the editors' recommendation in plain words and pointed to the journal and the
  publications process.
- It did not refuse the work: it offered the draft on an honest basis.
