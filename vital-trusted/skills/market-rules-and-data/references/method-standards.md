| M3 | Documentation about a medicinal product sent to prescribers or suppliers as part of promotion shall include at least the particulars of Article 91(1) and state the date on which it was drawn up or last revised. | M4 | Advertising to the general public (for products that may be advertised to it) shall make clear that it is an advertisement and that the product is a medicinal product, and include at least the name, the common name if there is one active substance, the information necessary for correct use, and an express, legible invitation to read the package leaflet or outer packaging. | Directive 2001/83/EC, Article 89(1) | EU | Confirmed 20 October 2026, same source as M1. |
| M5 | Subject to national laws, all promotional material must include clearly and legibly essential information consistent with the summary of product characteristics, with the date it was generated or last revised, and the supply classification; when appropriate the selling or indicative price and reimbursement conditions. A product must not be promoted before its marketing authorisation or outside its approved indications, and promotion must be consistent with the summary of product characteristics. | EFPIA Code of Practice, Chapter 1, Article 1 (Sections 1.01, 1.02) and Article 2 (Sections 2.01, 2.02); adopted 22 March 2019, "Final consolidated version 2026", efpia.eu | EU and other European countries, applied through each national member association's code | Confirmed 20 October 2026 from the EFPIA PDF. | M6 | Prescribing information (the items in clause 12.2: legal classification, cost, name, active ingredients, at least one authorised indication, a succinct statement of dosage, adverse reactions, precautions and contraindications, required warnings, marketing authorisation number and holder, and the date it was drawn up or last revised; or the SmPC instead) must be in all promotional material except abbreviated advertisements. In digital material read on the recipient's own device it is given as text or a clear, prominent, direct single-click link; in printed material as text or a clear, prominent QR code. Where not immediately apparent, a clear prominent statement must say where it can be found. | ABPI Code of Practice 2024, clauses 12.1 to 12.3; in operation from 1 October 2024; PMCPA PDF | UK | Confirmed 20 October 2026 from the PMCPA copy of the 2024 Code. |
| M7 | The non-proprietary name or active ingredients appear next to the most prominent brand name (for digital material, at its first appearance). All promotional material must include the prominent statement "Adverse events should be reported. Reporting forms and information can be found at [link to the MHRA Yellow Card site]. Adverse events should also be reported to [company]." The black triangle appears when the licensing authority requires it. | ABPI Code 2024, clauses 12.4 to 12.7 | UK | Confirmed 20 October 2026, same source as M6. |
| M8 | Prescription only medicines must not be advertised to the public (vaccination and other campaigns approved by the health ministers excepted). | M11 | Each piece of professional advertising must contain at least: (a) the product name (brand); (b) the active substances with their short name (INN or pharmacopoeia name); (c) the name and address of the marketing authorisation holder; (d) at least one indication or use, with dosage and method of use; (e) a summary of restrictions on use, undesirable effects and interactions; (f) the dispensing category; (g) a note that full information is in the published product information, naming where it is published (the register under Article 67(3) or 95b of the Therapeutic Products Act); (h) withdrawal periods for veterinary products in food-producing animals. | AWV, Article 6, same source as M10 | Switzerland | Confirmed 20 October 2026. |
