# Output format: the open-items summary

Plain words for each result (TR-H13), taken from `../../claim-assessment/references/statuses.md`.
Size it to the stakes (TR-H12). No percentage, ratio, score, grade or threshold.

Three rules from `../../claim-assessment/references/statuses.md` hold in every form below. Count claims (one checkable fact
each). Use the opening line "I compared each claim with your passages by hand" only when
you did the comparison here; for results you were handed, use the other opening line.
Every form ends with the closing statement, once, as the last line of the reply.

Two more rules hold in every form. The total counts factual claims only (the five factual
results); opinion, recommendation or definition lines are listed separately below it and
never added to it, so the header equals the sum of the rows under it. Recount before
sending. Open items go in order of risk: an item with no passage to compare with that
carries a figure (a number, date, dose or price) comes first, because the figure has
nothing behind it; then your passage says something different, not found, other items
with no passage, and partly found.

## Full summary (leadership or leaving the company)

```
Open items (draft, for human review)

Not tool-checked: <I compared each claim with your passages by hand. | these results come
from <the earlier claim check | your check>, done by hand. I have not compared the claims myself.>

<total> factual claims
  <n> found in your passage
  <n> partly found
  <n> not found in your passage
  <n> your passage says something different
  <n> no passage to compare with
Listed separately, not in the total: <n> opinion, recommendation or definition (left to
you, not counted as open)

Open items, in order of risk (<m>)
1. "<short quote>" (no passage to compare with, and it carries a figure). <Nothing was
   supplied | the source is unnamed>, so the figure has nothing behind it.
2. "<short quote>" (your passage says something different). The passage says: "<quoted words>".
3. "<short quote>" (not found in your passage). The passage covers <topic> but does not state this; removed or rewritten to what it states.
4. "<short quote>" (no passage to compare with). <Nothing was supplied | the source is unnamed>.
5. "<short quote>" (partly found). <What the result names as missing.> <Add "The passage
   gives <part>" only when the result you were handed says what the passage gives.>

Recommendation: <one sentence on what to do to the text or the sources>.

No confidence figure: these results were not tool-checked.

Found in your passage means the passage says it. It does not mean the sentence is true,
the source good, the evidence balanced or the work ready.
<If the work leaves the company or the author's team:> Next step: your <promotional or
medical> review, as your process names it, before it is used.
This summary is not a clearance. A named reviewer clears the record.

This check flags issues. It is not medical, legal, regulatory or ethics review.
```

## Short summary (internal)

```
Not tool-checked: <total> factual claims. <n> found in your passage, <n> partly found,
<n> not found in your passage, <n> your passage says something different, <n> no passage
to compare with. Listed separately, not in the total: <n> opinion, recommendation or
definition.
Recommendation: <one sentence>.
No confidence figure: these results were not tool-checked.

This check flags issues. It is not medical, legal, regulatory or ethics review.
```

## Combined form: check and summary in one

When the material and its passages were pasted and no check had run, run the claim check
by hand, then give this one block instead of two. Each open item appears once.

```
Fact-check and open items (draft, for human review)

Not tool-checked: I compared each claim with your passages by hand.
Checked against: <the passages supplied>.

<total> factual claims
  <n> found in your passage
  <n> partly found
  <n> not found in your passage
  <n> your passage says something different
  <n> no passage to compare with
Listed separately, not in the total: <n> opinion, recommendation or definition (left to
you, not counted as open)

<Optional, one line:> Found in your passage: "<short quote>"; "<short quote>".

Open items, in order of risk (<m>)
1. "<short quote>" (your passage says something different). The passage says: "<quoted words>". Ask: <one question>.
2. "<short quote>" (partly found). The passage gives <part>; it does not give <part>. Rewritten: "<new sentence>" (rewritten, not tool-checked).
   For a worked-out figure, the rewrite keeps the comparison in the passage's own
   figures ("CHF 6.2m against a plan of CHF 6.0m"), never a new difference.
3. ...

Revised text
> <the text with each rewrite; not-found sentences removed or rewritten to what the
> passage states; each no-passage sentence kept, with a gap after it; a held gap with both
> values in place of each sentence your passage says something different about>

Recommendation: <one sentence on what to do to the text or the sources>.

No confidence figure: these results were not tool-checked.

Found in your passage means the passage says it. It does not mean the sentence is true,
the source good, the evidence balanced or the work ready.
<If the work leaves the company or the author's team:> Next step: your <promotional or medical> review, as your process names it, before it is used.
This summary is not a clearance. A named reviewer clears the record.

This check flags issues. It is not medical, legal, regulatory or ethics review.
```

The review named in "Next step" comes from the table in
`../../citation-and-record/references/escalation-packet.md`.

## Results you told me about

When the person reports a check done elsewhere (their own, a colleague's, an earlier
chat) and the results are not in this conversation. Use the counts given, and every count
their own figures fix ("7 of 9 factual: 7 found, 1 partly, 1 not found" fixes the other
factual results at 0, because the listed results add up to the total). A list without a
total ("four found and one partly found") is a full list only when the person says that
is all; otherwise every other result is "not given". Write "not given" wherever nothing
they said fixes the count
(`../../claim-assessment/references/statuses.md`, counts the person gave you).

```
Open items (draft, for human review)

Not tool-checked: these results are as you told me. I did not see the check or the
passages, so I have not compared anything myself.

Factual <claims | statements, in your words> in all, as you told me: <total | not given>
  found in your passage: <n | not given>
  partly found: <n | not given>
  not found in your passage: <n | not given>
  your passage says something different: <n | not given>
  no passage to compare with: <n | not given>
Listed separately, not in the total: opinion, recommendation or definition: <n | not given>

Open items, in order of risk, as you told me (<m>)
1. <n> with no passage to compare with
2. <n> partly found
<One line per result that has open items, in the order of risk above. Name a sentence
only if the person gave it. Say only what the result you were told says; never what a
passage "gives".>

<If open items must be named:> To name each open item with its sentence, send me the list
of sentences and the result for each.

Recommendation: <one sentence aimed at the first open item in the list>.

No confidence figure: these results were not tool-checked.
<If the person gave a percentage from elsewhere:> The <figure> cannot be relied on as a
confidence figure: I do not know how it was worked out, and these results were not
tool-checked. It should not go into the <deliverable>. I can help fix or source the open
items and compare them again.
<If the person asked you to make a percentage, and the reply did not open with the block
below:> There is no percentage for these results, and none should go on the <deliverable>.

Next step: <the open items> go to your <x> review, as your process names it, before the
<deliverable> is used.
This is not a clearance. A named reviewer clears the record.

This check flags issues. It is not medical, legal, regulatory or ethics review.
```

## When the person asked for a percentage or a score

Open with:

```
There is no confidence figure. <I compared each claim with your passages by hand | These
results were read by hand>, and a percentage would suggest a tool measured it. Here are
the counts and what is still open.
```

Use "I compared" only when you did the comparison in this conversation. When this block
opens the reply, leave out the percentage line in the told form, so the point is made once.

With no open items, write no recommendation; say in one line that nothing is open.

Then give the full or short summary above. Every decline of a percentage, a score or a
supplied figure (this block, or the percentage lines in the told form) ends with this
line, last before the closing statement:

```
How far to rely on this is for <the named person, or your review process> to decide.
```

## When no sources were supplied at all

Nothing could be compared, so there are no results to count. This form also answers a
request for a percentage when no sources came. Open with this, give the
fixed line, list the claims that need a source, and ask for the passages:

```
No sources came with your message, so nothing could be compared: I have not checked any of the <n> claims.
No confidence figure: these results were not tool-checked.

Claims that need a source
1. "<short quote>". <What source it needs, e.g. the trial results with the rates in both groups.>
2. ...

Paste the passages each claim rests on, and I will compare each claim with them by hand.
<If the person asked for a percentage:> How far to rely on this is for <the named person, or your review process> to decide.
```

No counts block and no closing statement: no check ran (`../../claim-assessment/references/statuses.md`,
"The closing statement").
