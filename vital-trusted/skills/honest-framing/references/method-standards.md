# Method standards (Q1)

| R1 | When a study has a control group, give quantitative results for both the treatment and the relevant control group, and describe the comparator accurately. Give probabilities as absolute frequencies ("57 out of 100") or percentages. If a relative frequency is used ("33% reduction", "3 times as likely"), include the corresponding absolute measures "prominently and in direct conjunction" with it. The guidance notes consumers may find relative figures "harder to comprehend and more favorable". | FDA DTC quantitative guidance, December 2023 Revision 1, sections III.A and III.B, examples 1 to 4 |  | VERIFIED | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Scope: US DTC. Elsewhere applied as good practice only |  |
| R2 | Use the same numerical format throughout a piece; do not alternate qualitative words and numbers for the same concept; use the same denominator for side-by-side frequencies; do not round probabilities below 1 to a whole number, or round close values so their difference is lost. Do not minimize a serious risk: avoid presentations that focus on its low probability or suggest it is not important. Example 7: remove "only" from "only 2% of patients experienced bleeding that required hospitalization". | As R1, section III.C, examples 5 to 7 |  | VERIFIED | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Scope: US DTC; good practice elsewhere |  |
| R3 | Visual aids: explain the purpose and define the elements (title, variables, scales, axes); make displays "proportionate to the quantity being described" and scale axes so differences in bar height are proportional to differences in value; show both numerator and denominator in icon arrays and similar displays. Example 8 describes a bar graph that "does not truncate the y axis". | As R1, section III.D, example 8 |  | VERIFIED | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Scope: US DTC; good practice elsewhere |  |
| R4 | An advertisement is false, lacking in fair balance or otherwise misleading if, among other things, it presents a study as representing larger or more general experience than it does; uses "no significant difference" to claim clinical equivalence or to conceal a real difference; or uses headline or pictorial or other graphic matter in a misleading way. It may be misleading if it uses statistical significance to support a claim not shown to have clinical significance, "fails to reveal the range of variations around the quoted average results", uses retrospective analyses to cite findings the study does not soundly support, or "uses tables or graphs to distort or misrepresent the relationships, trends, differences, or changes" among the variables. | 21 CFR 202.1(e)(6)(v), (e)(6)(xv), (e)(6)(xviii), (e)(7)(ii), (e)(7)(iii), (e)(7)(iv) |  | PARTIAL | Confirmed from the eCFR text as of 1 October 2026, read 20 October 2026. | R5 | Information, claims and comparisons must be accurate, balanced, fair, objective and unambiguous, and must not mislead "by distortion, exaggeration or undue emphasis"; claims must stand alone, generally not qualified by footnotes. Supplementary information: "relative risk should never be referred to without also referring to the absolute risk"; statistical information must have a sound statistical basis; "differences which do not reach statistical significance must not be presented in such a way as to mislead"; statistical information must be subjected to statistical appraisal before use. Applies also to pricing and market share claims. | ABPI Code 2024, clause 6.1 and its supplementary information |  | PARTIAL | Clause 6.1 text confirmed 20 October 2026 and re-read 9 November 2026 in the PMCPA interactive 2024 Code. The supplementary quotes were not re-read `[CHECK]` exact wording. Scope: UK, material within the ABPI Code |  |
| R6 | Graphs and tables must give "a clear, fair, balanced view" and be relevant to the claim; they must not mislead "by being incomplete or by the use of suppressed zeros or unusual scales"; they must be adequately labelled; a graph taken from a study must be faithfully reproduced, and any modification stated and not distorting; when expressing data as percentages, "patient numbers should be included wherever possible"; a graph that gives a visually misleading impression must not be used even if it appears in the published paper. | ABPI Code 2024, clause 6.3 and its supplementary information |  | VERIFIED | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Scope: UK |  |
| R7 | Promotion must present a medicine objectively "without exaggerating its properties"; "exaggerated or all-embracing claims must not be made"; superlatives only for a clear fact. The word "safe" must not be used without qualification; "demonstrated safety" and "proven safety" are given as prohibited examples. | ABPI Code 2024, clauses 14.4 and 6.4 with supplementary information |  | PARTIAL | Clause 14.4 and 6.4 text confirmed 20 October 2026 and re-read 9 November 2026 in the PMCPA interactive 2024 Code. | R8 | Material about medicines, or about human health or disease, that a pharmaceutical company sponsors or is otherwise involved in "must clearly indicate the role of that pharmaceutical company". The declaration must be unambiguous so readers "are immediately able to understand the extent of the company's involvement and influence", and prominent enough that readers see it "at the outset". Market research material may instead state that it is commissioned by a pharmaceutical company. | ABPI Code 2024, clause 5.6 and its supplementary information |  | VERIFIED | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Scope: UK |  |
| R9 | Readers "must be able to make their own judgments" about whether authors' relationships and activities are pertinent, which "require transparent disclosures". Articles should declare authors' relationships and activities; sources of support "including sponsor names" and the sponsor's role in design, data collection, analysis, interpretation, writing and publication decisions, or a statement that it had none; and whether authors had access to the data. A funding statement covers direct support of the work. | ICMJE Recommendations, January 2026, section II.B, including II.B.2 |  | VERIFIED | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Scope: journal publication; used here as the reference list of what an interest statement contains |  |
| R10 | Abstracts should "note important limitations, and not overinterpret findings". Quantify findings with indicators of uncertainty "such as confidence intervals" and avoid relying solely on P values, "which fail to convey important information about effect size and precision"; distinguish prespecified from exploratory analyses. In the discussion, state limitations, "avoid unqualified statements and conclusions not adequately supported by the data", distinguish clinical from statistical significance, and avoid claiming priority. | ICMJE Recommendations, January 2026, section IV.A.3 (b. Abstract; d.iii Statistics; f. Discussion) |  | VERIFIED | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Scope: journal publication; applied here as the reporting standard for summaries of studies |  |
