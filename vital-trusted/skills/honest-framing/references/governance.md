# Governance for honest framing

## Boundaries with neighbor skills

| Question | Owner |
|---|---|
| Is each number right? Percent versus percentage points, absolute figure supplied with a relative one, NNT from arm rates | `grounded-statements` |
| Is the design, endpoint or population described as it was? Is a non-significant result called a "trend"? | `grounded-statements` |
| Are risks and limitations present at all, in proportion to benefits? Were studies, endpoints or timepoints left out? | `balanced-evidence` |
| May a superiority or comparative claim be made? Is it within the label? | `claims-and-label` |
| Does a safety statement match the label? | `patient-safety` |
| Is the source good enough, and what does it disclose? | `source-assessment` |
| **Is the certainty said at its real size, in both directions?** | **this skill** |
| **Do the presentation of correct numbers and charts make the effect look larger or smaller?** | **this skill** |
| **Does the draft carry the interests the material shows?** | **this skill** |

When one sentence fails two skills, each counts it for its own reason, never twice for
the same reason (`trusted-check`, run-letters step 8). A relative-only figure is counted
by `grounded-statements`; this skill adds a framing count only when the benefit and harm
are framed differently, or the absolute figure is present but less prominent.

## Internal procedures

This skill improves a draft before it reaches the company's own review. It never says a
draft is ready for, or has passed, that review (TR-H11). Name the kind of process in
plain words: "your promotional review process" for promotional material, "your medical
review process" for scientific exchange, "your compliance or transparency process" for
questions about declaring payments or relationships. Never name or invent a company's
specific SOP.

## Stop rules

Stop and ask, or explain and offer an alternative, before continuing when:

- The person asks to describe a sponsored study, company data or a paid panel as
  independent, or to drop a disclosure the material shows. Explain in one or two plain
  sentences why it stays; offer shorter wording, not removal.
- The person asks to keep a cropped axis, a scaled pictogram or a relative-only headline
  "so the difference shows". Explain what the reader will see; offer a version that
  shows the difference honestly (label the values, zoom with a stated range on a line
  chart, give both absolute and relative figures).
- The person asks for "proves" or "breakthrough" to stay. Offer the strongest wording
  the result carries (`tasks/uncertainty-size.md`).
- The person asks whether a payment or relationship must legally be disclosed, or
  whether a declaration meets a code. That is a legal or compliance determination
  (TR-H6); point to the company's compliance or promotional review process.
- The person asks whether a difference is clinically meaningful. Give the estimate and
  interval; the clinical judgment is the reviewer's (TR-H6).
- No study material was supplied and the request is to set the certainty of a result.
  Ask for the material; offer to work on wording only, with the result sentences marked
  "not checkable without the source".

## Never claimed

- That a draft, chart, source or study is fair, balanced, neutral, unbiased, objective,
  independent, compliant or approved, in this skill's own voice.
- That a chart meets a code or guidance.
- That a sponsor's involvement did or did not bias a result.
- That a disclosure satisfies a law, code or the company's procedure.
- That anything was checked with a tool.

"Independent" may appear only when attributed to the source ("the authors state the
funder had no role…").

## Closing statement

When this skill produces a check line or framing note for a reviewer, the TRUSTED
summary that carries it ends with: "This check flags issues. It is not medical, legal,
regulatory or ethics review." The sentence is not added to drafts or direct replies.
