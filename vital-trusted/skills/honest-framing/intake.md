# Intake: honest-framing

Read the context card from `trusted-intake` first (`../trusted-intake/templates/context-card.md`).
If the conversation holds one for this work, take the reader, market, use and stakes from
it and do not ask again for anything it holds. The market matters only for which public
rules explain a change. The habits apply everywhere.
When the conversation holds no card (this skill used alone), take what the message says, ask at most the questions below, and do not run intake separately.

## What this skill needs

| Need | Comes from | If it is missing |
|---|---|---|
| The draft, chart or result to frame | The person | Ask for it |
| Reader | The card or the request | Ask once (question 1) |
| Market | The card or the request | When it is unstated and the work leaves the company, the combined question includes it (question 1) |
| Who funded the study, and whether any authors work for or are paid by the company | The material supplied | Ask (question 2) when the material does not show it and the draft calls the source independent or presents it |
| The absolute figures behind a relative one, or the chart's underlying numbers | The material supplied | Ask (question 3), or leave a visible gap |

## Questions

Ask them as one combined question, drawn from at most these three, and give the draft with gaps in the same reply unless a stop rule applies, and none for a quick internal note. Plain words, one line each, with the
reason.

1. "Who will read this, and in which country?" Ask about the reader only when neither the
   card nor the request shows it. When the market is unstated and the work leaves the
   company, the one combined question always includes the market ("Which country is it
   for?"), with any other question folded into the same sentence.
2. "Who paid for this study, and are any of the authors employed or paid by the company?"
   Ask only when the material does not say and the work presents the result.
3. "Do you have the actual numbers behind the percentage, such as the event rates in each
   group?" Ask only when a relative figure is the only one supplied.

If an answer does not come, name the gap in the work. Never invent a funder or a figure.

## Never

- Add a hedge or a confidence level that the material does not support.
- Rework a chart's scale or colors for style. Judge only whether the picture matches the size.
