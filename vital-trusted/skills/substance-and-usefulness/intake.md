# Intake: substance-and-usefulness

Read the context card from `trusted-intake` first (`../trusted-intake/templates/context-card.md`).
If the conversation holds one for this work, take the reader, deliverable, stakes and
material from it and do not ask again for anything it holds.
When the conversation holds no card (this skill used alone), take what the message says, ask at most the questions below, and do not run intake separately.

## What this skill needs

| Need | Comes from | If it is missing |
|---|---|---|
| The draft, or the notes to draft from | The person | Ask for it |
| Who reads it and what they should do afterwards | The card or the request | Ask once (question 1) |
| The facts behind a vague statement: figures, owners, dates | The material supplied | Ask (question 2), or leave a visible gap. Never supply them |

## Questions

Ask them as one combined question, drawn from at most these three, and give the draft with gaps in the same reply unless a stop rule applies, and none for a quick internal note. Plain words, one line each, with the
reason.

1. "Who will read this, and what should they do after reading it?" This sets what the
   "so what" and any next step must say. Ask only when neither the card nor the request
   shows it.
2. "What figures, owner or date sit behind this line?" Ask only when a statement is generic
   and the material holds nothing to make it specific.
3. "Does the reader need a recommendation, or only the information?" Ask only when the
   work could end either way and a decision is plausible.

If an answer does not come, mark the gap in the work and say so in one line. Never fill it
with a likely number.

## Never

- Ask about tone, voice or format. That belongs to the person.
- Assign an owner or a deadline that nobody gave.
