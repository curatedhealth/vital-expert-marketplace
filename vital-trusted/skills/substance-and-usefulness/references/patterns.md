# Failure patterns

Each pattern: what it looks like, why it fails, before and after, and the rule it breaks.
All examples are invented for teaching; "Tavrelo", "Competitor B" and every figure are
fictional.

## Contents

- Vague statements V1–V6
- Fluff L1–L7
- So what W1–W5
- Action A1–A5
- Structure K1–K6
- Pairing traps P1–P3 (Tangible pushing into invention)

Pattern ids are for this file only. In what the person reads, describe the problem in
plain words (TR-H13). U-ids refer to `references/rules.md`.

## Vague statements

**V1 The faceless "stakeholders".** Who is meant is not said.
Why: the reader cannot tell whom to call or whose view this is.
Before: "Key stakeholders have raised concerns." After: "The two payer pharmacists in
canton X raised budget impact in year 2" (as the notes record). (TR-H2, U1)

**V2 Strength with no size.** "Strong uptake", "significant growth", "solid progress".
Why: the board cannot compare "strong" with plan.
Before: "Uptake has been strong." After: "12% of new patients in September, up from
10% in July" (from the notes), or a gap if the notes give no figure. (TR-H2)

**V3 Time with no date.** "Soon", "going forward", "in the near term", "shortly".
Before: "We expect listing shortly." After: "Listing decision expected [GAP: not
supplied — date or meeting — supply the payer's timetable]." (TR-H2)

**V4 Comparison with no comparator.** "Ahead", "better", "lagging".
Before: "Sales are ahead." After: "Q3 net sales CHF 2.4 million against plan CHF 2.9
million" (as supplied), which also shows "ahead" was wrong. (TR-H1)

**V5 Activity instead of action.** "Optimize the approach", "address gaps", "drive
initiatives".
Before: "We will optimize our access approach." After: the action the notes record
("send the updated budget impact model"), or a question for the person. (TR-H2)

**V6 The everything-list.** Eight priorities, none ranked.
Why: when everything is a priority the reader cannot act on any.
After: rank by the person's stated criterion, or ask which matter most. (TR-H7)

## Fluff

**L1 Buzzword for a meaning.** "Leverage synergies across the stakeholder landscape."
Why: the sentence has no object a reader could act on.
After: say what is meant from the material, or cut the sentence. (TR-H9 does not
protect it: this is substance, not style.)

**L2 Throat-clearing opening.** "In today's rapidly evolving healthcare landscape, it is
more important than ever to…"
Why: the reader's first ten seconds are spent on nothing.
After: open on the point: "Tavrelo missed Q3 plan by CHF 0.5 million; the main reason
is the open payer review in canton X." (from the material)

**L3 Announcing the point.** "It is important to note that…", "This slide aims to…".
After: state the point.

**L4 The same point three times.** Summary, body and close repeat one fact with no new
information.
After: once, where it does most work; a closing line may carry the ask instead.

**L5 Twin bullets.** "Improve payer engagement" and "Strengthen relationships with
payers".
After: merge into one specific bullet, or ask what distinguishes them.

**L6 Stacked hedges.** "May potentially help to somewhat improve."
After: the one qualifier the evidence needs ("may improve"). Never remove a qualifier
the evidence requires; that is True and Unbiased.

**L7 The word that is not fluff.** "Robust to sensitivity analyses", "competitive
landscape" as a slide title over named competitors.
Why it matters: cutting a technical term changes the meaning.
After: keep it. A list hit is a prompt to read, not a finding. (TR-H9)

## So what

**W1 The fact list.** A competitor summary that lists Competitor B's launch date, price
and trial readout and stops.
Why: the brand team must work out the implication alone, and each reader will draw a
different one.
After: "Competitor B's planned Q2 launch comes after our Q1 listing target, so the
listing date is the one to protect" (if both dates are in the material).

**W2 The implication at the end.** The point is in the last bullet of the last slide.
After: lead with it; the facts follow as support.

**W3 The implication that adds a fact.** "This means Competitor B will take 15% share."
Why: a forecast nobody supplied, dressed as a "so what".
After: "If their launch date holds, they arrive in the same quarter as our listing
decision [GAP: not supplied — share forecast — supply one if you want to size the
risk]." (TR-H1)

**W4 The implication that adds a cause.** "Sales fell because of Competitor B's
discounting."
After: report what the material shows (timing, or a stated cause), and leave the
causal link as a gap or a question. (TR-H1)

**W5 The clinical "so what".** In a medical summary: "so physicians should switch
patients".
Why: clinical advice (TR-H6).
After: the implication for the team's work ("the advisory board pre-read needs to
present the missed primary endpoint first").

## Action

**A1 No ask.** A board update that ends without saying what the board is asked to do.
After: "For decision: …" or "For information; no decision needed."

**A2 Action with no owner.** "Next steps: follow up with canton X."
After: "Follow up with canton X [GAP: needs a decision — owner and date — name who
follows up, by when]." (TR-H5)

**A3 The owner the assistant chose.** "The KAM will send the model by 31 October."
Why: neither the owner nor the date was supplied; the reader will hold the wrong person
to an invented date.
After: ask, or leave the gap. (TR-H1, TR-H5)

**A4 Process instead of action.** "Align with stakeholders on the way forward."
After: the specific step the notes record, or a question: "What is the step after the
model is sent?"

**A5 The recommendation presented as agreed.** "The team will expand to three regions."
when the person asked for options.
After: "Proposed for your decision: …", with what it rests on. (TR-H5)

## Structure

**K1 The invented 2x2.** "Impact vs Readiness" matrix with four named quadrants, axes
undefined, placements by nobody's data.
Why: it looks like analysis and is opinion; a reader may fund the top-right quadrant on
the strength of a drawing.
After: the ranked list the content supports, or the matrix with defined axes and a
visible source for each placement if the person wants to keep it.

**K2 The forced three.** Content squeezed into "three pillars" with one item in the
third.
After: as many groups as the content holds, or none.

**K3 The empty heading.** "Overview", "Key considerations".
After: the point of the section as the heading, where the reader skims.

**K4 The mismatched heading.** "Recommendation" over a summary of facts.
After: retitle as what it is, and add the recommendation if the work needs one (A1).

**K5 The named model.** "Our ACCESS framework" invented to label five ordinary steps.
After: the five steps, unlabeled, unless the person asked for a name.

**K6 The buried conclusion.** The main finding sits after three slides of background.
After: lead with it; keep the background as support.

## Pairing traps (Tangible pushing into invention)

**P1 Concrete-looking numbers.** Asked to "make it more concrete", the draft gains
"uptake rose 34% in six months" with no source.
After: "[GAP: not supplied — uptake figure and period — supply the source]" and a short
list of what to send. (TR-H1, TR-H2)

**P2 Plausible owners and dates.** "Owner: Head of Market Access, by Q1 2027" added
because it seemed likely.
After: ask; leave the gap. (TR-H5)

**P3 Named examples from memory.** "Like the recent listing in Germany" to make a point
vivid, when nobody supplied it.
After: use only examples from the material, or ask for one. (TR-H1)
