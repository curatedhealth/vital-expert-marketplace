# Intake: source-assessment

Read the context card from `trusted-intake` first (`../trusted-intake/templates/context-card.md`).
If the conversation holds one for this work, take the reader, market and stakes from it and
do not ask again for anything it holds. Sources are read by hand. Every result is labeled
"not tool-checked".
When the conversation holds no card (this skill used alone), take what the message says, ask at most the questions below, and do not run intake separately.

## What this skill needs

| Need | Comes from | If it is missing |
|---|---|---|
| The source itself: the document, a link, a DOI or a PubMed ID | The person | Ask for it (question 1). A name alone is not enough to read |
| The question the source is meant to answer, and who will read the result | The card or the request | Ask (question 2). Fit cannot be read without it |
| Whether the person has checked for a retraction, correction or newer version | The person | Ask (question 3). Without an answer, write "not checked" and say what to look at |

## Questions

Ask them as one combined question, drawn from at most these three, and give the source card, with what is unknown said plainly, in the same reply unless a stop rule applies. Plain words, one line each, with the reason.

1. "Can you paste the document, or give me the link, DOI or PubMed ID?" Ask only when only
   a name or a short reference was given.
2. "What do you want to use this source for, and who will read it?" Ask only when neither
   the card nor the request shows it.
3. "Have you checked whether it has been retracted or replaced, and when?" Ask only at
   leadership or outside-the-company stakes. If not checked, tell the person where to look
   and record "checked by you on <date>" or "not checked".

## Never

- Say a source is good, bad, reliable or acceptable. Say what it can carry and what it
  cannot.
- State peer review without the journal or a statement in the source that shows it.
- Fill a missing author, funder or date from memory.
