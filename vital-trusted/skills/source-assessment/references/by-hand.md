# Assessing sources by hand

This is the only path. No tool fetches a page, grades it or checks a date. You read what
the person supplied, say what it can carry, and tell the person which checks only they can
make. Every note is labeled "not tool-checked".

## Steps

1. **Take the source details from the source itself.** Kind of source, authors, who
   published it, who paid for it, date, and any identifier it prints (DOI, PMID, trial
   registry number, label version). If a detail is not on the page the person gave you,
   write "not stated". Never fill it from memory.
2. **Say what kind of source it is** in the source's own words: peer-reviewed article,
   regulator document, label, company page, conference abstract, news item. Say it was
   peer reviewed only when the source says so.
3. **Say what design the evidence has**, as the source names it, and do not upgrade it.
   See `../tasks/evidence-strength-and-currency.md`.
4. **Say what it can carry and what it cannot**, for the claim and the reader in question.
   A conference abstract can support "was presented". A company page can support "the
   company states". Say both in one line each.
5. **Date it in full**, for example "12 March 2026", as the source gives it. Name the
   dates you could not find. Say whether the claim depends on the date (a label, a
   guideline, a price, a safety communication).
6. **List the checks the person must make.** Currency is not something this skill can
   confirm by reading. Give the person the places to look, then record what they report:
   - Retractions and corrections: the journal's own page for the article, the publisher
     notice, and a retraction database such as Retraction Watch.
   - Newer versions: the regulator's current label or guideline page.
   - Identifier match: that the DOI or PMID opens the same title and authors.
   Record each as "checked by you on <date>", or "not checked". Never write "current" or
   "no retraction" on your own.
7. **Fill the source card** (`../templates/source-card.md`) and head it "Not tool-checked:
   I read what you supplied. Currency and retraction checks are yours to make."

## What stays out

- No tier, score or grade. A source is described, not ranked.
- No "high quality" or "reliable". Say what it can and cannot carry.
- No statement that a source is current, unretracted or admissible without the check.
- No source cited from memory. A source nobody supplied is listed as a gap.

## Handing on

Sources that can carry their claim go to `citation-and-record`. When a source cannot carry
a claim, hand that source note on as a separate note, next to the claim. The claim's result
stays the one `claim-assessment` gave; this skill never changes it and the note is never
added to the counts (`../../claim-assessment/references/statuses.md`). A person decides
what to use, under the review your process names (`../governance.md`).
