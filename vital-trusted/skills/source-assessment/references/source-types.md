# Kinds of source and what each can carry

Method guidance for daily users. It is not a rule of any market or company. Where a
company SOP lists acceptable references for a type of material, that list governs
(TR-H11). Peer review statements follow `rules.md` R3, R4 and R8; the rest is good
practice.

## The kinds

| Kind | What it is | Peer reviewed? | Can usually carry | Cannot carry on its own |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peer-reviewed article | A research article published in a journal after review | Yes, when published as a research article; letters, editorials and news in journals may not be | The findings of that study, in its population, with its limitations | A general claim beyond its population, comparator or endpoint |
| Preprint | A manuscript posted before or without journal review | No (R3) | That early results were reported, labeled "preprint, not peer reviewed" | Settled evidence; a claim to a payer or doctor without the label; replace with the published version once it exists |
| Conference abstract or poster | A short preliminary report of a study at a meeting | Not confirmed unless the meeting's process is supplied; preliminary (R8) | That preliminary results were presented, with the meeting and date | The full methods, the final numbers, or anything the abstract does not show |
| Approved label (prescribing information, SmPC, instructions for use) | The text approved by a regulator for a product in one market | No; it carries regulatory authority | What is approved in that market, at that version | Use, populations or claims outside it; another market's position |
| Regulator or HTA document (assessment report, decision, guidance) | A document issued by an authority or HTA body | No; it carries the authority of the issuing body | That body's decision or view, at that date | Another body's or market's view; a current position if superseded |
| Clinical guideline | Recommendations from a professional society or national body | Varies; some are, many are not; check the document | What that body recommended, for whom, at that version, with its own grading if stated | A recommendation it does not make; currency if a newer version exists |
| Systematic review or meta-analysis | A structured synthesis of several studies | Usually, when published in a journal | The pooled picture across the studies it included, up to its search date | Studies published after its search; more than its included studies show |
| Company data on file | Unpublished company analyses or study reports | No | Internal decisions; in external work only where the company's process allows and can supply it on request (R9, `[CHECK]`) | Independent evidence; anything the reader cannot inspect |
| Market research or survey | Answers from a sample of respondents | No | What those respondents said, with how many and who | Clinical evidence; "doctors prefer" from a small or selected sample |
| Press release | An announcement by a company, institution or journal | No | That the organization announced something, on that date | The study's results as evidence; ask for the publication or the data behind it |
| News | Reporting by a journalist or outlet | No | That something was reported | The underlying study; go to the primary |
| Blog, newsletter or social post | Commentary by an individual or organization | No | That this person or organization holds this view | Evidence of effect, safety or market fact |
| Internal document | Plans, slides, minutes, forecasts inside the company | No | Internal context, labeled as internal | External claims; it is not evidence to an outside reader |

## What each reader will ask

Plain guidance on what each reader typically weighs. It describes the questions to
prepare for; it does not set what any payer, board or doctor accepts.

| Reader | They will usually ask | Sources that hold up to the question | Sources that will draw a challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Payer or HTA body | Compared with what, in which patients, in our system, at what cost, how certain | Published comparative trials and systematic reviews; HTA and regulator documents; local real-world data with its method stated | Preprints, abstracts, press releases, data on file, models without stated inputs, another country's data presented as local |
| Board or leadership | What is known, how sure, what it means for the decision | Any source, clearly labeled with its kind and limits, including internal data and market research | Unlabeled sources; a single study or press release presented as settled |
| Doctors | Is it published, in which patients, how does it fit the label and guidelines | Peer-reviewed articles, the approved label, current guidelines | Preprints or abstracts without labels; off-label data outside scientific exchange; company press releases |
| Patients or public | Is it true and what does it mean for me | Approved patient material, the label, plain summaries of published evidence through the right process | Anything not reviewed by the company's process; no source should go to patients without it |

Where the reader is a doctor or patient, or the material is promotional, the company's
medical or promotional review process decides which sources may be used.
