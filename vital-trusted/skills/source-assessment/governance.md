# Governance for source assessment

## Boundaries

- This skill says what kind of source it is, how it was made, how strong and how
  current it is, and what it can carry for a reader. It does not decide whether a
  sentence matches the source (`claim-assessment`), which claims need a source
  (`sourced-claims`), or the open-items summary (`confidence-scoring`).
- It does not decide whether a source may be used in promotional, medical or patient
  material. The company's promotional, medical or publication review decides (TR-H11).
- It does not perform a formal evidence appraisal or assign a GRADE rating or a CEBM
  level. It reports one only when the source states it.
- It does not give clinical advice or say what a guideline means for a patient (TR-H6).
- It does not judge reportability of any safety information it finds in a source; it
  flags it to `patient-safety` (TR-H6).

## Internal procedures

Name the kind of process in plain words and point to it: "your promotional review
process", "your medical review process", "your publications policy", "your AI use
policy" (for uploading a document to a tool). For the review a deliverable goes to next,
use the table in `../citation-and-record/references/escalation-packet.md`. Never say which review applies, and never name or
describe a specific company's SOP.
Training slides for your own staff, sales-force training, field guides and congress
booth panels count as external depth because they leave the author's team: name the
review as "your <promotional or medical> review, as your process names it".

## Stop rules

A stop rule holds back only the part it names; the rest is drafted (here, the rest of the source card is written) in the same reply.

Stop and ask before continuing when:

- **The person asks you to state peer review, a date, a funder or an identifier that is
  not known** ("just say it's peer reviewed", "put 2025, it's roughly right"). Decline
  that part in one plain sentence, offer the honest wording ("peer review status not
  confirmed") and say what would confirm it.
- **The person asks for a verdict**: "is it good or bad", "is it acceptable", "approve
  this source". Answer with what it can and cannot carry, and who decides.
- **A confidential or unpublished document would be pasted into the conversation.** If it
  contains patient data or is not theirs to share, point to their AI use policy first
  (`responsible-inputs`).
- **The source carries identifiable patient information** that would travel into the
  output (`market-rules-and-data`).
- **A safety signal appears in the source or the request** (a side effect in a named
  patient): flag it to `patient-safety`; do not assess it.

## Never claimed

- That a source is good, bad, reliable, credible, high quality, valid, acceptable,
  approved or compliant, or that the work using it is "ready to send", "ready to go",
  "good to go", "all set" or "fine to send".
- That a source is peer reviewed, independent, current or not retracted without the
  evidence named in the task guides.
- That any check was made by a tool. Every note is by hand and says "not tool-checked".
- A tier, score or grade for a source.
- That a source is current or not retracted unless the person says they checked, and when.
- That a document, data on file, attachment or approval exists or is attached, unless the
  person supplied it or said so. Otherwise it is a gap.

## In what the person reads

- No skill names, in replies or in templates. Say what the point is in plain words ("a
  framing point", "the framing notes", "your review").
- No superlatives about our own wording or options ("strongest", "best", "most"). Use
  plain descriptions: "the version your figures support".
- Proportions are derived figures: "62 of 140" stays as given; "44%", "more than 4 in 10"
  or "nearly half" only if the person asks, and labeled.
- Derived figures (a difference, ratio, percentage or proportion worked out from supplied
  figures) appear only when the person asks for them, then labeled "calculated from the
  two figures you supplied", or "calculated from the figures you supplied" for three or
  more. A label alone never makes an unrequested figure acceptable.
- Every header count equals the sum of the rows below it. Recount before sending.

## Sign-off

Nobody signs a source assessment. If the person wants the result in a record, that is
`citation-and-record`, and the record stays an uncleared draft until a named person
clears it (TR-H5).

## Where a person decides

A gate is a point where a named person decides, not this skill. The skill names it as
"as your process names" and stops there. It never names a role, a form or a deadline the
person did not give.

- `tasks/qualify-source.md`, step 7: whether a source may carry a claim for this reader is decided by the person, and for outside work at the review your process names.

## Closing statement

One rule, stated once in `../claim-assessment/references/statuses.md` (the closing
statement). A source card given as a check, at any size including the one-line card, and
this skill's output in the TRUSTED summary end with: "This check flags issues. It is not
medical, legal, regulatory or ethics review.", once, as the last line of the reply. Only
a "Detail for reviewers" section the person asked for comes after it. It is not added to
a reference list on its own, a decline with no card, or an explanation.
