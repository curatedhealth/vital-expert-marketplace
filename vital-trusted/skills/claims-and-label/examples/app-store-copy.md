# Worked example: app store copy for a digital health app

Invented case for teaching. "Ventrelo Rhythm" and its maker are fictional.

## Request

"Write the app store description for Ventrelo Rhythm for the EU launch. Lead with the
big message: it diagnoses atrial fibrillation at home, so people don't need to wait for
a cardiology appointment. Keep it short and exciting."

## Material supplied

> **Intended purpose (from the instructions for use, EU, version 2.1 as supplied):**
> Ventrelo Rhythm is software intended to record, store and display a single-lead
> electrocardiogram (ECG) from a compatible wearable, and to notify adult users when
> the recording shows a rhythm that may be atrial fibrillation. It is not intended to
> diagnose atrial fibrillation or other conditions. Results are to be reviewed by a
> healthcare professional. Not intended for users under 22 years or with a pacemaker.
>
> **Status, as the person states it:** CE marked as a class IIa medical device.

## Context

Public, consumer-facing app store copy: leaving the company, full check (TR-H12).
Market EU, as stated. Intended purpose supplied.

## Draft copy

> **Ventrelo Rhythm: record your heart rhythm and share it with your doctor**
>
> Take a single-lead ECG with your compatible wearable
> [GAP: not supplied — how long a recording takes — supply it from the instructions
> for use, or leave it out]. Ventrelo Rhythm saves your recordings and lets you know when one shows a
> rhythm that may be atrial fibrillation, so you can share it with your doctor.
>
> Ventrelo Rhythm does not diagnose atrial fibrillation or any other condition. Your
> doctor reviews your results.
> [GAP: needs a decision — what the copy says about symptoms and when to seek medical
> help — your medical and regulatory teams set this wording]
>
> For adults 22 and over. Not for people with a pacemaker.

## Note to the person (as they see it)

Label, promotion and comparisons: what I changed (draft, not yet reviewed)

Working from: the Ventrelo Rhythm intended-purpose statement you supplied (EU
instructions for use, version 2.1); for the public, as app store copy, as you said.

Changed
1. "It diagnoses atrial fibrillation at home" → "lets you know when a recording shows a
   rhythm that may be atrial fibrillation, so you can share it with your doctor". The
   intended purpose says the app does not diagnose, and that a healthcare professional
   reviews the results. Copy that claims more than the device is meant to do is the
   first thing a reviewer will question.
2. "So people don't need to wait for a cardiology appointment" → removed. It suggests
   the app replaces a doctor's assessment, which its intended purpose does not cover.
3. "Exciting" tone → kept plain. For a health app, plain copy that says what it does is
   also what the store reader trusts.
4. Added who it is for and who it is not for, from the intended purpose.

Left as gaps for you to fill
1. How long a recording takes — not in what you supplied. Add it from the instructions
   for use, or leave it out.
2. The wording on symptoms and when to seek help — your medical and regulatory teams
   should set it.

Questions for you
1. Is the CE marking and class as you described it current for every EU market in the
   launch? I have taken it as you said.

Next step: this copy goes through your promotional review process, with your
regulatory team, before it is published.

I compared the copy with the intended-purpose statement you gave me. I did not check
the device's certification, and nothing here was checked with a tool.
