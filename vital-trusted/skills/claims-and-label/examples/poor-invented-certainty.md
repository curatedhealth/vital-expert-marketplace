# Poor example: key messages that go past the label and compare across trials

Invented case for teaching. "Brevanzo", "Kelandro" and the trials are fictional.

## Request

"Draft three key messages for the field team in Germany on Brevanzo. Reps say
dermatologists already use it in teenagers, so add one for that. And say it works faster
than Kelandro."

## Material supplied

> **Brevanzo product information (EU summary of product characteristics, March 2026):**
> indicated for moderate to severe atopic dermatitis in adults who are candidates for
> systemic therapy. Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been
> established.
> **KESTREL-2 (Brevanzo, placebo-controlled):** clear or almost clear skin at week 16:
> 41% versus 11% on placebo.
> **Kelandro phase 3 trial (separate, placebo-controlled):** 38% versus 10% at week 16.

## Poor reply (what must not happen)

```
1. Brevanzo: the proven choice for teenagers with atopic dermatitis.
2. Brevanzo works faster than Kelandro: 41% clear skin at week 16 versus 38%.
3. Approved across atopic dermatitis, so prescribe with confidence.
```

## Why it fails

- Message 1 reaches teenagers. The label covers adults only and says nothing was
  established under 18, so "proven" is not backed by anything supplied.
- Message 2 sets the Brevanzo figure beside the Kelandro figure. They come from two
  separate placebo-controlled trials, and the speed claim ("faster") is not in either
  trial. No head-to-head study was supplied.
- Message 3 stretches "adults who are candidates for systemic therapy" to "across atopic
  dermatitis" and ends in a call to prescribe.
- It does not say which messages went beyond the label, so the person cannot see what
  to take out before review.

## Repair (as the person sees it)

1. Brevanzo is indicated for adults with moderate to severe atopic dermatitis who are
   candidates for systemic therapy.
2. In KESTREL-2, 41% of adults on Brevanzo had clear or almost clear skin at week 16,
   versus 11% on placebo.

Label, promotion and comparisons: what I changed (Uncleared draft)

Working from: the EU summary of product characteristics dated March 2026 that you
supplied; for the field team in Germany, as promotional material, as you said.

Kept out
1. The teenager message. Your label covers adults only. Questions doctors ask about
   teenagers go to your medical information team, and anything about a specific patient
   to your safety reporting procedure, as your company's process sets.
2. "Works faster than Kelandro". No study compares the two directly.
3. "Approved across atopic dermatitis, so prescribe with confidence". Your label covers
   adults who are candidates for systemic therapy, and a call to prescribe is not a
   message. No third message is written: the material you supplied gives no other
   statement to base one on.

Comparisons
1. "Faster than Kelandro" - two separate trials. Not written; the two percentages are
   not set side by side.

Next step: this goes through your promotional review process before it is used.
Whether it can be used is decided there.

I compared the draft with the label you gave me. I did not check that the label is the
current approved version, and none of this was tool-checked.
