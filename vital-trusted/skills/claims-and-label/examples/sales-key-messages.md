# Worked example: sales-force key messages with an off-label temptation

Invented case for teaching. "Brevanzo", "Kelandro", the trials and all figures are
fictional.

## Request

"Draft four key messages for the field team in Germany for the Brevanzo relaunch.
Reps are telling us dermatologists already use it in teenagers, so add one for that.
And make the point that Brevanzo works faster than Kelandro, that's our edge."

## Material supplied

> **Brevanzo product information (EU summary of product characteristics, section 4.1
> and 4.2, version as supplied, dated March 2026):** Brevanzo is indicated for the
> treatment of moderate to severe atopic dermatitis in adults who are candidates for
> systemic therapy. Recommended dose: 150 mg by subcutaneous injection every two weeks.
> Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established.
>
> **KESTREL-2 publication (PDF):** randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 16
> weeks, 640 adults. Clear or almost clear skin at week 16: 41% Brevanzo versus 11%
> placebo (table 2). Itch improvement first separated from placebo at week 2.
>
> **Kelandro publication (PDF), its own phase 3 trial:** placebo-controlled; clear or
> almost clear skin at week 16: 38% versus 10%.

## Context

Field-team material used with dermatologists: leaving the company in practice, so the
full check applies (TR-H12). Market Germany, use promotional, as stated. Label
supplied.

## Draft messages

> 1. Brevanzo is indicated for adults with moderate to severe atopic dermatitis who are
>    candidates for systemic therapy.
> 2. In KESTREL-2, 41% of adults on Brevanzo had clear or almost clear skin at week 16,
>    versus 11% on placebo.
> 3. In KESTREL-2, improvement in itch with Brevanzo first separated from placebo at
>    week 2.
> 4. One 150 mg injection every two weeks.

## Note to the person (as they see it)

Label, promotion and comparisons: what I changed (draft, not yet reviewed)

Working from: the Brevanzo EU product information you supplied (dated March 2026), the
KESTREL-2 publication and the Kelandro publication; for dermatologists in Germany, as
promotional material, as you said.

Kept out
1. A message about teenagers. Your product information covers adults only and says
   safety and efficacy under 18 have not been established. What doctors already do in
   practice is useful insight, but a field message about it would promote a use your
   product information does not cover. If dermatologists ask reps about teenagers, those questions go to
   your medical information team.
2. "Faster than Kelandro". No study you supplied compared the two directly. KESTREL-2
   and the Kelandro trial each compared with placebo, in different patients, so they
   cannot show which works faster. I wrote the week-2 itch result against placebo
   instead, which your publication supports.

Comparisons
1. The two week-16 results (41% and 38%) are from separate trials. I did not put them
   side by side. Whether any comparison of the two can be shown is a question for your
   medical review.

Left as gaps for you to fill
None.

Next step: these messages go through your promotional review process before the field
team uses them. Whether they can be used is decided there.

I compared the messages with the product information you gave me. I did not check that
it is the current approved version for Germany, and nothing here was checked with a
tool.
