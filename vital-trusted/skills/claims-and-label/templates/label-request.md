# Output format: asking for the label or intended purpose

Used when the work makes product statements and no label or intended-purpose statement
was supplied (TR-H7). It follows the draft in the same reply, with every product
statement in the draft left as a gap. One short message; ask only for what changes the
answer.

## For a medicine

```
I left the product lines as gaps. Could you share the approved product information for
<market, or "the market this is for">? The summary of product characteristics or
prescribing information is what I need (the patient leaflet if this is for patients). Your
regulatory team will have the current version.

I'll keep every product statement inside it: what it is approved for, which patients,
dose and wording. Without it I'd have to guess, and the work is held to the real
one.
```

## For a device or app

```
I left the app's functions as gaps. Could you share the intended-purpose statement for
<product>, for <market>? It is
usually in the instructions for use or the technical documentation, and your
regulatory team will have the current wording.

I'll describe what the app does in those terms: what it records or measures, who reads
the result, and when to see a doctor. Copy that says it does more than that goes beyond
what the device is meant to do.
```

## When it is not clear whether it is a medicine or an app

```
Is <product> a medicine, or a device or app? Could you share its approved product
information if it is a medicine, or its intended-purpose statement if it is a device or
app, for <market, or "the market this is for">? Your regulatory team will have the
current version.

I'll keep every product statement inside it; until then each one is a gap in the draft.
```

Do not decide whether software is a medical device (TR-H6). Ask, and work from what the
person says.

## When the market is also missing

Add one line:

```
Which market is this for? What the product is approved for can differ between
countries.
```
