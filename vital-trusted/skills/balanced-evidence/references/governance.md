# Governance for balanced evidence

## Boundaries

- This skill decides what evidence is shown and what is left out: benefits with their
  risks, discontinuations and limitations, and a fair choice of studies, endpoints,
  subgroups and time points. It works from the supplied material only.
- Whether each statement matches its source is `grounded-statements`. How numbers,
  uncertainty and charts are framed, and interests disclosed, is `honest-framing`.
  Whether a claim is allowed by the label or as a comparison is `claims-and-label`.
  Dosing, warnings, interactions and side effects about a real patient are
  `patient-safety`. Grading a source is `source-assessment`.
- It does not judge whether a risk is clinically important, whether an event was caused
  by the product, or whether an event must be reported (TR-H6).
- It does not decide whether a piece is promotional, or whether it meets a regulation or
  code. That is a legal and regulatory determination for the company's own process
  (TR-H6, TR-H11).
- Prominence is judged only as far as it decides whether the reader sees the risk. Fonts,
  colors and layout are the person's (TR-H9).

## Internal procedures

This skill prepares work for the company's own review. It never says a piece is ready
for, or has passed, that review (TR-H11). When work leaves the company, the note names
the kind of process it goes through next in plain words: "your promotional review
process", "your medical review process", "your medical information procedure" for a
reply to a doctor's question. Do not guess the name or content of a specific company's
procedure.

## Stop rules

Stop and ask, or decline the part, when:

- The person asks to remove, hide, shrink or move risks, discontinuations or limitations
  to make the story more positive ("keep it positive", "leave the safety for the PI",
  "we'll do safety in the next email"). Decline that part in one plain sentence, keep the
  main risks in the piece with concise wording, and offer the legitimate help
  (`tasks/fair-balance.md`, "Keep it positive").
- The person asks to report only the study, subgroup or time point that worked, for
  material that leaves the company. Decline to drop the rest silently; offer to state
  the scope or to show both.
- The material holds no safety or limitation data and the draft is a benefit claim for a
  reader outside the company. Ask for the label or the results; do not draft a
  benefits-only piece.
- The person asks whether the piece "has fair balance", "is balanced enough" or "would
  pass review". Say what was added and what is open, and that the decision belongs to
  their review process.
- The input describes a side effect in a real patient. Hand to `patient-safety` and name
  the safety reporting procedure; do not assess it.
- The person asks you to supply a side effect, rate or study from memory to complete the
  balance. Offer a visible gap instead (TR-H1).

## Never claimed

- That a piece is balanced, fair, complete or has fair balance.
- That all studies, endpoints or harms were included, or that no other studies exist.
- That a side effect is minor, rare in practice, unrelated to the product or not
  clinically important.
- That a piece meets any code, regulation or company procedure.

## Closing statement

When this skill's check lines go into a TRUSTED summary, that summary ends with: "This
check flags issues. It is not medical, legal, regulatory or ethics review." Direct
replies, drafts and balance notes do not carry it.
