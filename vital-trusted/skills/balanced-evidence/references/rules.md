# Primary rules behind this skill

Each rule names its primary source, the date and how it was confirmed. A rule marked
`[CHECK]` has not been confirmed against the primary document, or its scope or currency
is open; it is applied as good practice, not cited as a requirement (TR-H8).

Promotional rules (R1 to R7) bind promotion in the markets and to the members they
cover. Reporting rules (R8, R9) govern how randomized trials are reported in journals.
Outside that scope, including internal work and summaries, apply them as good practice
only. The company's own review process decides which rules apply to a piece of work
(TR-H11). This skill never says a piece meets any of them (TR-H3, TR-H6).

Last reviewed: 20 October 2026.

## Fair balance in promotion

| Id | Rule | Primary source | Scope | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | An advertisement fails to give a true statement if it fails to present a fair balance between information on side effects and contraindications and information on effectiveness; it is not in violation where side-effect information is "comparable in depth and detail with the claims for effectiveness or safety". It also fails if it omits material facts in light of its representations. | US 21 CFR 202.1(e)(5)(ii) and (e)(5)(iii), eCFR (ecfr.gov, Title 21, part 202, section 202.1) | US, prescription drug advertisements, including those in journals and other periodicals (202.1(l)(1)) | Confirmed 20 October 2026 from the eCFR page, shown as up to date as of 16 October 2026. Read through a summarizing fetch; short fragments quoted. Full paragraph wording to be quoted from the page before any external use `[CHECK]`. |
| R2 | An advertisement may lack fair balance if it fails to give sufficient emphasis to side effects and contraindications where effectiveness claims dominate that part, or if side-effect information does not appear with "a prominence and readability reasonably comparable with the presentation of information relating to effectiveness". | 21 CFR 202.1(e)(7)(vii) and (e)(7)(viii), as R1 | As R1 | Confirmed 20 October 2026, as R1. |
| R3 | An advertisement is false, lacking in fair balance or misleading if it "selects information from any source in a way that makes a drug appear to be safer" than shown (e)(6)(iv); implies a study "represents larger or more general experience with the drug" than it does (e)(6)(v); cites a favorable statement by an authority while it "fails to refer to concurrent or more recent unfavorable data or statements" (e)(6)(viii); or describes side effects "by means of a general term for a group in place of disclosing each specific side effect" (e)(6)(xx). It may be misleading if it uses statistics "on a retrospective basis to discover and cite findings not soundly supported" (e)(7)(iii). | 21 CFR 202.1(e)(6)(iv), (v), (viii), (xx) and (e)(7)(iii), as R1 | As R1 | Confirmed 20 October 2026, as R1. |
| R4 | Information, claims and comparisons must be accurate, balanced, fair, objective and unambiguous, "based on an up-to-date evaluation of all the evidence and reflect that evidence clearly", and must not mislead "by distortion, exaggeration or undue emphasis". Supplementary information: claims must be capable of standing alone and generally should not be qualified by footnotes. | ABPI Code of Practice 2024, clause 6.1 and its supplementary information (PMCPA, 2024 ABPI Code PDF, pmcpa.org.uk) | UK, ABPI members and companies that agree to the Code. The 2024 edition comes into operation on 1 October 2024, as stated in the Code. | Clause wording confirmed 20 October 2026 from the PMCPA PDF. Supplementary wording on footnotes read as a paraphrase through a summarizing fetch `[CHECK]` exact text. Same clause as `sourced-claims` R2, used here for balance and emphasis. |
| R5 | "It must not be stated that a product has no adverse reactions, toxic hazards or risks of addiction or dependency." "The word 'safe' must not be used without qualification." The supplementary information applies the restriction to derivatives such as "safety". | ABPI Code 2024, clause 6.4 and its supplementary information, as R4 | UK, as R4 | Clause wording confirmed 20 October 2026, as R4. Examples given in the supplementary information ("demonstrated safety", "proven safety") read as a paraphrase `[CHECK]`. |
| R6 | Graphs and tables must give a clear, fair, balanced view; artwork must not detract from warnings. | ABPI Code 2024, clause 6.3 and its supplementary information, as R4 | UK, as R4 | "Clear, fair, balanced view" confirmed 20 October 2026. "Must not detract from warnings" read as a paraphrase `[CHECK]`. Used here only for risk prominence; chart framing is `honest-framing`. |
| R7 | Promotional information should be "clear, legible, accurate, balanced, fair, and sufficiently complete to enable the recipient to form his or her own opinion of the therapeutic value"; based on "an up-to-date evaluation of all relevant evidence"; and "should not mislead by distortion, exaggeration, undue emphasis, omission or in any other way". "Descriptions such as 'safe' and 'no side effects' should generally be avoided and should always be adequately qualified." | IFPMA Code of Practice 2019, section 4.2 (IFPMA-hosted PDF, ifpma.org; effective 1 January 2019, replacing the 2012 Code) | International baseline for IFPMA member companies and associations | Confirmed 20 October 2026 from the IFPMA-hosted PDF. Whether a later IFPMA Code supersedes the 2019 edition `[CHECK]`. `sourced-claims` cites the same section from a member-association copy. |

## Trial reporting

| Id | Rule | Primary source | Scope | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R8 | Report "all harms or unintended events in each group" (item 27); for each group, losses and exclusions after randomization, with reasons (item 22b); trial limitations, addressing potential bias, imprecision and generalizability (item 30); and an "interpretation consistent with results, balancing benefits and harms, and considering other relevant evidence" (item 29). | CONSORT 2025 statement, Hopewell S and others, BMJ 2025;389:e081123, doi:10.1136/bmj-2024-081123, table 1 (published simultaneously in several journals). Read from the BMJ PDF as deposited in the UCL Discovery repository. | Reports of randomized trials; good practice for any summary of a trial | Confirmed 20 October 2026. Exact publication day not shown in the copy read `[CHECK]`. `grounded-statements` cites the same statement (Nature Medicine copy) for items 26 to 28. |
| R9 | Report pre-specified primary and secondary outcomes (item 14); results for each primary and secondary outcome for each group (item 26); important changes after the trial began, "including any outcomes or analyses that were not prespecified" (item 10); any other analyses, including subgroup analyses, distinguishing pre-specified from exploratory (item 28); and where the protocol and statistical analysis plan can be accessed (item 3). | CONSORT 2025, as R8 | As R8 | Confirmed 20 October 2026, as R8. |

## How to use these rules in output

- In what the person reads, state the practice in plain words ("the main side effects
  belong next to the benefit, not only in the footnote"). Name a rule only if they ask
  (TR-H13).
- Never extend a rule beyond its scope. R1 to R3 are US prescription drug advertising;
  R4 to R6 are the UK code; R7 is the IFPMA baseline; R8 and R9 are journal trial
  reports; R10 is US payor communications.
- Never say a draft has fair balance or meets a code. Say what was added, moved and left
  open.
