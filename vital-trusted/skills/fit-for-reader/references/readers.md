# The readers

Working profiles for the ten readers daily users write for most. They are a starting
point drawn from practice, not rules (`references/rules.md` holds the rules). The person's
own description of their reader always replaces them (TR-H7). What each reader "needs to
decide" is the question the work should answer first.

| Reader | Needs to decide or do | Words they use | Depth | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Board | Approve, fund, stop, or note; what is the ask and the risk | Business: revenue, plan, risk, decision; little clinical detail | The finding, its limits, the ask, on one page | A clinical caveat that changes the decision must survive the cut. Forecast vs actual kept apart (`grounded-statements`) |
| Headquarters (global teams) | Compare this market with others; support, resource or escalate | Global product, project and metric names; their template | Comparable figures, local context in one line | Affiliate shorthand, local payer names without a word of explanation, local currency without the period |
| Sales force (internal briefing or training) | What to say, to whom, and what not to say | Field language, the approved messages | Short, memorable, with the boundaries stated | Anything reps will say to a doctor is promotional material and goes through promotional review. Internal training wording must not drift beyond the approved messages (`claims-and-label`) |
| Doctor | Whether and how this matters for their patients; the evidence behind it | Clinical and statistical terms used correctly | Design, population, endpoints, effect sizes with intervals, safety | Oversimplifying for a doctor reads as promotion. A reply to a doctor's own question goes through the medical information procedure |
| Nurse | How to give, monitor and explain it to the patient | Clinical, practical: administration, monitoring, what to tell the patient | Practical steps, monitoring points, patient questions | Statistical depth they will not use; missing practical detail is a gap, not something to fill |
| Pharmacist | How to dispense, store, check interactions, counsel | Pharmaceutical: formulation, storage, interactions, dosing adjustments | Precise, label-based | Any dosing, interaction or storage statement belongs to the label (`patient-safety`) |
| Payer or HTA body | Whether to cover, at what price, for whom; budget impact | Population, comparator, outcome, cost, uncertainty | Evidence and its limits stated plainly; method named | Internal enthusiasm, brand language and "breakthrough" framing. Uncertainty at its real size (`honest-framing`) |
| Patient or caregiver | What this means for me, what to do, when to get help | Everyday words; no abbreviations | Short sentences, one point at a time, numbers they can picture | Clinician language, false reassurance, alarming words. The route to a doctor or pharmacist is `patient-safety` |
| Regulator | Whatever the regulator asked; the commitment being made | Regulatory and technical, the regulator's own terms | Exactly what was asked, referenced | Content and commitments belong to the regulatory function and its process; this skill fits words and structure only |
| Investor or analyst | Value, risk, timing of milestones | Financial and pipeline terms | Headline, risk, what is known and not known | Whether a statement is inside information or needs disclosure is a legal determination (TR-H6); flag to legal or investor relations |

## Health literacy for patient material

The practices in `tasks/audience-and-literacy.md` steps 5 to 10. They come from the EU
readability guideline for package leaflets (R1, R2) and the CDC Clear Communication
Index (R5), applied as good practice outside their scope.

## When the reader is two readers

Some work has a primary and a secondary reader (a payer dossier read by an HTA
assessor and a clinician; a board paper read by a medical director). Write for the
primary reader and make sure the secondary reader finds what they need without a second
version. If the needs conflict, ask which reader leads.
