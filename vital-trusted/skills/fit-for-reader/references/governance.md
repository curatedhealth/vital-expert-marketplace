# Governance for fit for the reader

## Boundaries

- This skill changes how things are said for a reader, a format or a language. It does
  not change what is said. Facts, numbers and qualifiers are `grounded-statements`;
  balance of benefit and risk is `balanced-evidence`; the size and certainty of a risk
  is `honest-framing`.
- The route to a doctor, nurse or pharmacist in patient material, and any dosing,
  warning or interaction statement, is `patient-safety`. This skill keeps any route the
  source has and never adds or removes one.
- Filler, vagueness and invented frameworks are `substance-and-usefulness`. This skill
  owns the match between the work and its reader, format and language.
- Required statements and market rules are `market-rules-and-data`. This skill never
  drops a required line to fit a format.
- No clinical advice to a patient or prescriber, no causality or reportability
  judgment, and no legal determination: whether investor material is inside
  information, whether a translation meets a market's language requirement (TR-H6).

## Internal procedures

Name the kind of process in plain words (TR-H11). Do not guess a company's SOP name or
content.

| Situation | Kind of process to name |
|---|---|
| Patient material about a product | Your promotional or medical review process, as the person says the piece is used |
| A reply to a doctor's, nurse's or pharmacist's own question | Your medical information procedure |
| Sales-force material reps will use with customers | Your promotional review process |
| A translation for use in another market | A qualified medical translator and your review process in that market |
| Payer or HTA material | Your market access review process (or promotional review, as your company defines it) |
| Regulator correspondence | Your regulatory function and its process |
| Investor or analyst material | Your legal or investor relations function |

## Stop rules

Stop and ask before continuing when:

- The reader is not known, the work leaves the company, and the reader changes what is
  written.
- Making a patient text simpler would need clinical advice or a fact the material does
  not hold ("tell them what to do if they miss a dose" with no source).
- The person asks to make patient material more reassuring or more alarming than the
  source.
- The content cannot fit the format asked for without dropping a fact that changes the
  meaning.
- A translation is for patients, product information, safety wording or consent, and
  the person intends to use it without a qualified translator or the company's review.
  Translate as a draft and say who settles it; do not present it as ready.
- The request is to translate a validated questionnaire or other instrument. Point to
  the instrument owner's process (`references/rules.md` R9).

## Never claimed

- That a text is readable, plain-language compliant, health-literate, patient-friendly,
  user-tested or at a reading grade level.
- That a translation is accurate, faithful, certified, validated, final or approved.
- That a text meets the EU readability guideline, the CDC Clear Communication Index or
  any other standard. The skill uses their practices; it does not assess against them.
- That local wording is approved, unless the person supplied it as approved, and then
  only as "approved wording as you supplied it, dated …" (attribution, TR-H3).

## Closing statement

Drafts, rewrites and translation notes are not checks and do not carry the closing
statement. When this skill's lines appear in the TRUSTED summary, that summary ends
with: "This check flags issues. It is not medical, legal, regulatory or ethics review."
