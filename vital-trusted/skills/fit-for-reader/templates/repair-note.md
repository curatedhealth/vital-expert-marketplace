# Output format: repair note

Follows the revised text. Plain language, no rule codes or pattern ids (TR-H13). Size it
to the stakes (TR-H12): one or two lines for an internal email; the full note for
anything leaving the company and for patient material, except a one-sentence patient
rewrite, which gets the one-line note below.

## Full note

```
What I changed for <reader> and why (Uncleared draft)

Written for: <reader, and what they need to decide or do>
Format: <as asked: format and length>. <If the original missed it: "You asked for
<format>; the original was <what it was>.">

<Only the counts that apply, none at 0:> Passages changed: <n>. Terms replaced or
explained: <n>. Passages cut or moved: <n>. Gaps left: <n>.
Other changes: <n> (<short kinds, e.g. causal claim softened, disclosure added,
unsupported claim removed>).

Changed
1. "<before>" → "<after>". <Why, in one plain sentence, e.g. "A patient will not know
   'concomitant'; 'other medicines you take' says the same thing.">
2. ...

Kept on purpose
1. <a qualifier, limit or risk kept even though it made the text longer, and why>

Cut or moved
1. <what was cut or moved, and where it could go if wanted>

Left as gaps for you to fill
1. <what is missing>: <where it would come from>

Question for you
<a choice only you can make: which reader leads, what to drop to fit the format; one combined question at most, covering at most two related points (TR-H7)>

<If patient text carries dose or safety statements:> The dose and safety statements
should match the approved patient leaflet; check them against it before use.

<If the work leaves the company:> Next step: this goes through your <kind of internal
process> before it is used.

I changed the words, not the facts: every figure, condition and limit in your original
is still in this version<, except what is listed under "Cut or moved">. I did not test
it with readers.
```

Drop any section that is empty. The counts line is required in every form whenever
something was changed or found, and shows only the counts that apply: leave out a
count that would read 0 (a style count appears only when style was changed). When
nothing was changed or found, give no note and no counts line. "Kept on purpose"
appears whenever a fact was kept that a shorter or simpler version would have lost.
"Other changes: <n> (<short kinds>)" counts any change the named counts do not cover (a
causal claim softened, a disclosure added, an unsupported claim removed), so the main
fix is never uncounted.

## One-line note (a one-sentence patient rewrite)

```
Changed: <what changed, in plain words, e.g. "'contraindicated' now reads 'must not be
used'">. <Gap left: <one line, if any>.> This goes through your patient material review
before it is used.
```

## Leadership note (board, headquarters, business review)

```
What I changed for <reader> (Uncleared draft)

Format: <as asked>. <If the original missed it: "You asked for <format>; the original
was <what it was>.">
Passages changed: <n>. Terms replaced or explained: <n>. Passages cut or moved: <n>.

Changed or cut: <one line each, at most five>
Only you can decide: <one line each, if any>
```

## Short note (internal, low stakes)

```
Passages changed: <n>. Terms replaced or explained: <n>.
Changed: <one line>. Cut: <one line, if any>.
```
