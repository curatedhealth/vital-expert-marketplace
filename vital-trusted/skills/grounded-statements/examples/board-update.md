# Worked example: a board update from notes

Invented case for teaching. "Lumivra" is a fictional product; the affiliate and figures
are made up.

## Request

"Turn my notes into a five-line board update on the Lumivra launch. Make it sound
confident, and fill in anything missing with sensible estimates, the board just wants
the headline."

## Notes supplied

> Lumivra Q3: net sales CHF 2.4m (plan 2.9m). Q4 forecast 3.1m.
> Share of new patients 10% → 12% Jul–Sep.
> Two big hospital groups listed it in Sept. Campaign went live August.
> Payer: canton X still reviewing, concerned about budget impact.
> Field team: 2 open positions.

## Draft

> Lumivra Q3 net sales were CHF 2.4 million, below the plan of CHF 2.9 million. Share of
> new patients rose 2 percentage points over the quarter, from 10% to 12%, and two large
> hospital groups listed the product in September, the month after the campaign went
> live. Q4 sales are forecast at CHF 3.1 million. Reimbursement in canton X is still
> under review; the payer has raised budget impact as a concern. Two field positions are
> open [GAP: not supplied — expected hiring date — add it if you want the board to see
> it].

## Short note to the person

Changed nothing you supplied. Left out estimates: you asked me to fill gaps with
sensible numbers, but the board would read them as actuals, so I used only your figures
and left one gap. I kept "below plan" visible, because a confident update that hides the
miss is the kind the board remembers. I did not say the campaign caused the listings;
your notes only show the timing.

## Why this is the right depth

A board update is leadership-level (TR-H12): figures checked, actual and forecast kept
apart, share change in percentage points, no invented cause, a short note rather than a
full repair note.
