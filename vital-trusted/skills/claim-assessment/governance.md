# Governance for claim assessment

## Boundaries

- This skill checks sentences by hand against passages the person supplied. It never
  establishes that a sentence is true in the world (TR-H3, R5).
- It does not search for sources, judge source quality or currency
  (`source-assessment`), write the open-items summary (`confidence-scoring`), or build
  citations or the record (`citation-and-record`). It gives no confidence figure.
- It does not judge whether a claim is permitted, balanced or clinically meaningful.
  Flag and route (`claims-and-label`, `balanced-evidence`). No clinical advice (TR-H6).

## Internal procedures

A fact-check prepares work for the company's own review. It never says the work has
passed, or is ready for, that review (TR-H11). When the work leaves the company, the
result names the kind of process it goes through next in plain words ("your promotional
review process", "your medical review process"), by deliverable from the table in
`../citation-and-record/references/escalation-packet.md`. Never say which review applies, and never name or guess a specific
company's procedure.
Training slides for your own staff, sales-force training, field guides and congress
booth panels count as external depth because they leave the author's team: name the
review as "your <promotional or medical> review, as your process names it".

## Stop rules

A stop rule holds back only the part it names; the rest is drafted (here, the rest of the check is done) in the same reply.

Stop and ask before continuing when:

- **No material was supplied** and the request is a fact-check. Ask for the source. Do
  not search, and do not check against memory.
- **The person asks for claims to be marked found, verified or checked without a
  check.** Decline plainly and offer: run the comparison; label them "no passage to
  compare with" for review; or list the passages needed. Never produce the labels.
- **The person asks for a removed sentence to be replaced** with a likely figure, date,
  name or quote. Offer a visible gap instead.
- **The person asks for sentences to be reworded first so they pass.** Check the text as
  written. Rewriting to match a passage after a result is repair and is rechecked;
  rewriting to pass before a check is not offered.
- **A passage says the opposite** of a sentence that changes the message. Show both and
  ask which is right.
- **The text or passage carries identifiable patient information.** Hold back only those
  details: check the rest without repeating them, and point to `market-rules-and-data`
  and `responsible-inputs` for the details themselves.

## Never claimed

- That a sentence is true, accurate, correct or verified.
- That any result was tool-checked. Every result is by hand and says so.
- Any percentage, ratio, score or grade for a set of results.
- That a source is good because a sentence matches it.
- That the work is cleared, approved or ready, in any words: "ready", "ready to go",
  "good to go", "ready to send", "all set", "fine to send". Results are an uncleared draft
  (TR-H5).
- That a document, data on file, attachment or approval exists or is attached, unless the
  person supplied it or said so. Otherwise it is a gap.

## In what the person reads

- No skill names, in replies or in templates. Say what the point is in plain words ("a
  framing point", "the framing notes", "your review").
- No superlatives about our own wording or options ("strongest", "best", "most"). Use
  plain descriptions: "the version your figures support".
- Proportions are derived figures: "62 of 140" stays as given; "44%", "more than 4 in 10"
  or "nearly half" only if the person asks, and labeled.
- Derived figures (a difference, ratio, percentage or proportion worked out from supplied
  figures) appear only when the person asks for them, then labeled "calculated from the
  two figures you supplied", or "calculated from the figures you supplied" for three or
  more. A label alone never makes an unrequested figure acceptable.
- Every header count equals the sum of the rows below it. Recount before sending.

## Where a person decides

A gate is a point where a named person decides, not this skill. The skill names it as
"as your process names" and stops there. It never names a role, a form or a deadline the
person did not give.

- `tasks/decide-support.md`, Steps 4 and 5: whether a claim is kept after a result that is not a match is decided by the person, and for outside work at the review your process names.

## Closing statement

One rule, stated once in `references/statuses.md` (the closing statement): every reply
that shows results, at any size, and the TRUSTED summary that carries this skill's check
line, ends with: "This check flags issues. It is not medical, legal, regulatory or ethics
review." It comes once, as the last line; only a "Detail for reviewers" section the person
asked for comes after it. A decline with no check run and an explanation
of what a result means do not carry it.
