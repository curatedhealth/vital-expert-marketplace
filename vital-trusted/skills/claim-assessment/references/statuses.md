# Hand-check results

The six results of a hand check, in plain words. Use these words exactly. Every result is
read by hand, by comparing a sentence with a passage the person supplied, and every one is
labeled "not tool-checked". A result says what the supplied passage says. It does not say
the sentence is true in the world.

Contents: the six results; boundaries between the results; decision rules for common
close calls; counting (and counts the person gave you); opening line; the closing
statement; words not to use.

## The six results

| Result | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Found in your passage | The passage states the claim, or a stricter version of it | Keep as written. Show the passage next to it |
| Partly found: <missing part> not in your passage | You can quote the part that is there and name the part that is not | Rewrite to match, then check the new sentence again |
| Not found in your passage | A supplied passage covers the topic, you read it, and it does not state this | Remove the sentence, or rewrite it to what the passage states. List it either way |
| Your passage says something different | The passage states another value or the opposite. Quote it | Ask. Show both. Do not send the sentence: hold it in the revised text with both values and write no replacement until the person answers |
| No passage to compare with | No supplied passage covers the topic, or its authority is unnamed ("experts agree") | Keep it as an open item, not removed: in revised text the sentence stays, with a visible gap, and you ask for a passage. Never fill it |
| Opinion, recommendation or definition | No passage can settle it | Leave to the person and their review process. It is not counted as checked |

## Boundaries between the results

- **Found** needs the words. A stricter passage ("at least 12 weeks") supports "12 weeks".
  A looser passage does not support a stronger claim.
- **Partly found** needs a named missing part. "The trial enrolled adults" supports the
  adult half of "the trial enrolled 412 adults with type 2 diabetes" and nothing more. A
  figure worked out from the passage's numbers is not in the passage, so it is partly
  found: quote the numbers the passage gives, and name the worked-out figure as missing.
- **Not found** means a supplied passage covers the topic and you read it. It does not
  mean the sentence is false.
- **Says something different** needs a passage that states the other value or the
  opposite for what the sentence claims ("no adverse events" against "two adverse events
  were reported"; "95% persistence" against "discontinuation was 14%"). A passage that is
  silent is not a difference. A relative reading of two stated rates is not a difference
  either, right or wrong; mark the worked-out figure as partly found.
- **No passage to compare with** is different from not found, and the test is the
  topic. Ask: does a supplied passage cover the topic of this claim? If yes and it does
  not state the claim, the result is "not found in your passage": the sentence is removed
  or rewritten to what the passage states, and listed. If no supplied passage covers the
  topic at all, the result is "no passage to compare with": the sentence stays in the
  revised text with a visible gap and is never filled with a likely figure, date, name or
  quote. Examples: "Most patients prefer a daily tablet to a monthly injection" against a
  trial passage with no preference data is no passage to compare with. "The new
  indication launched in September" against a sales report extract with sales and share
  only is no passage to compare with: the extract does not cover launches. "Launched in
  Austria in October 2025" against a product information extract (dose, indication) is
  no passage to compare with for the same reason. "Nausea was rare" against a passage
  that reports adverse events without nausea is not found: the passage covers the topic.
- **Opinion, recommendation or definition** is for sentences such as "we should
  prioritize" or "adherence means taking the drug as prescribed". Do not mark them found
  or not found.

## Decision rules for common close calls

- **A rounding or summary word that does not match a stated figure** is partly found. The
  passage's figure is there; the draft's word is the missing part. Rewrite to the
  passage's figure. Examples: "nearly 2,000 patients" against "1,850 adults"; "over 500"
  against "486"; "halves the risk" against "rate ratio 0.56". A rounding that the stated
  figure fits as written ("more than 1,800" against 1,850) is found.
- **A proportion restated in another form** is a derived figure. "Nearly half", "more
  than 4 in 10" or "44%" against "62 of 140" is partly found: the passage's count is
  there, the restated proportion is the missing part. Rewrite to the figure as given
  ("62 of 140"). In your own wording, never turn a count into a proportion or a
  proportion into another form unless the person asks, and then label it "calculated
  from the figures you supplied".
- **A sentence with two facts is two claims,** each with its own result. "The only
  once-weekly patch for condition K" against an extract that says "apply one patch once
  weekly" is two claims: "a once-weekly patch for condition K" is found; "the only" (no
  other product is) is a claim about other products, which the extract does not cover, so
  it has no passage to compare with and stays as a visible gap, never asserted and never
  replaced. Never merge the two into one "partly found".
- **A sentence with one found part and one different part** ("cut migraine days from 9
  to 4" against "from 9.1 at baseline to 5.2") is two claims: the found part is counted
  as found, the other part as says something different. The whole sentence is held in
  the revised text until the person answers, because the found part alone is not a
  sentence. List the found part under "found" as part of the held sentence.
- **The passage says the outcome was "not assessed", "not measured" or "not performed"**
  is not found. The passage states no other value, so it is not a difference. Quote the
  line so the person sees why: "Velostra saves hospitals EUR 3,000 per patient" against
  "No cost analysis was performed" is not found; remove the sentence.
- **An evaluative word against stated rates** ("well tolerated", "few", "most", "rarely")
  is partly found: the rates are there, the judgment is the missing part. Rewrite to the
  rates and add no judgment of your own. Example: "well tolerated, with few patients
  stopping" against "discontinuation due to adverse events was 9% with drug D and 4% with
  placebo" is two claims ("well tolerated"; "few patients stopping"), each partly found,
  and the sentence becomes the two rates. Whether the rates are "few" is for the person and their
  review (`balanced-evidence`).
- **A figure worked out from stated figures** ("4% above plan" against "CHF 6.2m vs plan
  CHF 6.0m") is partly found, never "says something different": the stated figures are
  there, the worked-out figure is the missing part. You do not judge whether the draft's
  arithmetic is right, because that needs a computation.
- **Saying "does not match" without computing.** Put the stated figures side by side and
  say which words the passage does not contain: "The draft says '4% above plan'. Your
  source gives CHF 6.2m and a plan of CHF 6.0m, and no percentage." The rewrite keeps the
  comparison in the passage's own figures: "CHF 6.2m against a plan of CHF 6.0m".
- **A named person's quote with no source.** A quotation stays only when a supplied
  passage contains it word for word, attributed to that person. A named person is not an
  unnamed authority. If the quote is checked against material that does not contain it,
  it is not found; remove it, do not replace it, and ask for the interview, article or
  talk it came from. If no source at all was supplied for it, it is no passage to compare
  with and stays open.
- **A passage giving a related but different measure.** When the passage answers the
  same question with another measure and another value ("patients felt the benefit from
  the first week" against "separation from placebo was first seen at week 4"), the result
  is "your passage says something different": name both measures, quote the passage, ask.
  When the passage covers a narrower measure, group or arms than the claim ("few patients
  stopping treatment" against "discontinuation due to adverse events"; a comparison of
  settings used for a claim about the product), it is partly found: rewrite to what the
  passage measures and name the difference in one line. Do not equate the two measures.
- **A significance word backed by the passage.** "Fatigue improved significantly
  (p=0.03)" against a passage that gives "fatigue (p=0.03)" is found when the stated
  p-value is under the threshold of the analysis plan, or the passage states a confidence
  interval for the effect that does not include no effect. Add a note on endpoint rank,
  quoted from the passage: primary or secondary, and how many ("one of five secondary
  endpoints"). If the passage does not state the threshold, say so in the same note. Do not rule yourself on whether a result
  matters. A significance word with no p-value, interval or word in the passage behind it
  is partly found.
- **A confidence interval that spans both benefit and harm** (for example a difference
  of -1.1 to 3.0, or a hazard ratio of 0.8 to 1.3) is described as including both, in the
  rewrite and in any note. Never describe it as showing benefit.
- **A correct sentence that misleads by what it leaves out** (a secondary endpoint shown
  alone when the primary endpoint failed; one arm's rate without the comparator) keeps its
  result, usually found. The omission is not a seventh result and is not counted. Add one
  framing point under the results, in these words: "A framing point: the sentence is
  accurate but the framing may mislead; <what is left out, quoted from the passage>. See
  the framing notes." No skill name appears in what the person reads; the point is handed
  on internally to `honest-framing` or `balanced-evidence`.

## Counting

One counting unit for every Prove skill and `work-record`: a claim is one checkable fact,
and a sentence with two facts is two claims (`../tasks/break-into-claims.md`). Count each
claim once, by its result, in these words, including the zeros, and call the total
"claims" in every count: the result, the open-items summary, the check line and the record
note. When the person reports results per sentence or per statement, count them as given
and say so ("10 factual statements, as you told me"). The open items are partly found, not
found, says something different, and no passage.

- **The total counts factual claims only:** the five factual results (found, partly found,
  not found, says something different, no passage). Write it as "<n> factual claims".
- **Opinion, recommendation or definition lines are listed separately,** on their own line
  below the total, "Listed separately, not in the total: <n> opinion, recommendation or
  definition". They are never added to the total.
- **Every header count equals the sum of the rows below it,** zeros included. Recount
  before sending. A header that does not add up is fixed, not explained.

There is no percent, no ratio, no score and no grade. A source note from `source-assessment`
stays a separate note and is never added to these counts.

### Counts the person gave you

When the check ran elsewhere (the person's own, a colleague's, an earlier chat), use only
what the person said, labeled "as you told me". Never a zero they did not fix, and never a
count worked out from a percentage.

- A count their own figures fix is given: write the number. "7 of the 9 factual
  sentences found, 1 partly found, 1 not found" fixes the other factual results at 0,
  because the listed results add up to the total; "all three are recommendations" fixes
  every factual result at 0.
- A list of results without a total is a full list only when the person says that is all
  ("that's everything", "the rest were found"). "Four found and one partly found" on its
  own gives found 4 and partly found 1; every other result is "not given".
- Write "not given" wherever nothing the person said fixes the count: opinions when they
  gave only factual totals; every other result when they gave "2 partly found and 1 no
  passage" with no total.
- Use the person's own unit ("statements", "sentences") when they gave one, labeled "as
  you told me"; say "claims" only for counts you made yourself.

## Opening line

The opening line says who compared. Use it only when it is true.

- You compared the claims in this conversation: "Not tool-checked: I compared each claim
  with your passages by hand."
- The results come from the person, a colleague or another run: "Not tool-checked: these
  results come from <your check | your colleague's check | the earlier claim check>, done
  by hand. I have not compared the claims myself." Never write "I compared" for results
  you were handed.
- No passages came with the request at all: "Not tool-checked: no passages came with your
  message, so I have not compared any claim." Then ask for the passages.

## The closing statement

One rule for `claim-assessment`, `source-assessment`, `confidence-scoring`,
`citation-and-record` and `work-record`. The statement is: "This check flags issues. It
is not medical, legal, regulatory or ethics review."

- It ends every reply that shows check results, per claim or as counts, at any size: the
  full, short or quick result; the full, short or combined open-items summary; counts as
  you told me; a source card given as a check, at any size, including a one-line card;
  and the TRUSTED summary that carries a check line.
- It comes once, as the last line of the reply, after any revised text, record note or
  reference list in the same reply. One exception: a "Detail for reviewers" section the
  person asked for comes after it.
- It does not go on a reply that shows no results: a decline with no check run, an
  explanation of what a result means, a reference list given on its own, a record note
  given on its own (even when it lists counts from a check), or a plain answer.

## Words not to use

Do not write supported, partly supported, unsupported, contradicted or unverifiable as the
result of a hand check. Those words imply a tool returned them. A hand check uses the six
results above, and the person is told every time that it was done by hand.
