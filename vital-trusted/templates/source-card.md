# Source card

One card per source. If the skill ships its own source card, use that one instead.

```text
Source: <title as the source shows it>, <issuer or authors>, <date in full, or "date not shown">
Identifier: <DOI, PMID, document number or link as supplied, or "none supplied">

What it is: <kind, for example "a company press release", "a preprint", "a peer-reviewed trial paper">
Peer review: <peer reviewed | not peer reviewed | status not confirmed: what would confirm it>
Who made it: <authors or issuer>; funded by <as disclosed, or "not stated in the source">
Currency: <checked by you on <date> | not checked>; retraction or correction status: <as shown by the source or a notice you supplied, or "not checked">

What it can carry for <reader>: <what it supports, no wider than the source's own words>
What it cannot carry: <what it should not be used to say>

To use it: <what would close the gap, for example "the published article" or "the event rates">
Next step: this goes through your <kind of review process> before it is used.
Not tool-checked.
```

## Rules

- "What it can carry" and "What it cannot carry" are always present. Never replace them
  with a yes or no on whether the source is good enough.
- No tier, no grade and no score. Say what the source is and what it can bear.
- Retraction or correction status is "not checked" unless a notice was supplied.
- Peer review and funding come from the source itself. If they are not shown, say so.
- Clinical strength language (CEBM, GRADE) describes the study, not whether a promotional
  claim is substantiated. That decision belongs to your review process.
