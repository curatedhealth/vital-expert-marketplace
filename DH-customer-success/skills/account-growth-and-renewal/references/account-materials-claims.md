# What renewal and account materials may claim

Interpretive reference for A2. Renewal proposals, business reviews, success stories and ROI summaries are promotional material in law as soon as they describe what the product does or achieves. This file lists the rows a claim in such material is checked against. It flags; it never clears. Promotional review stops for the person's regulatory or legal owner. Rows: `promotion-and-claims-research.md` (PC-), `channels-partners-launch-research.md` (CP-), `customer-success-research.md` (CS).

## The claim sets the product's status

| Fact | Rows |
|---|---|
| Intended use is shown by labeling claims, advertising, and oral or written statements; marketing copy therefore sets the regulatory status of an app. | PC-US-17, PC-US-20 |
| EU: intended purpose is defined by the label, instructions, promotional or sales materials or statements and the clinical evaluation. | PC-EU-03 |
| Great Britain: promotional material, including the landing page and app store description, sets medical purpose; disclaimers do not undo medical claims made elsewhere; MHRA lists device-indicating words such as "clinically proven", "diagnoses", "monitors", "prevents", "treats". | PC-UK-01, PC-UK-02, PC-UK-04 |

A deck that says the product does something its authorization or intended purpose does not cover can change what the product is in law. Check every function claim against the authorization record (`tools.md`, A2 Step 4) and the intended purpose the person's regulatory owner gives.

## Checks for each claim

| Check | Rule | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| False or misleading, including by omission | US labeling false or misleading in any particular misbrands a device; omission of material facts counts. Advertising of non-restricted devices falls under the FTC Act, where a false advertisement includes material omissions. | PC-US-01, PC-US-03, PC-US-13, PC-US-67, PC-US-69, PC-US-70 |
| Beyond the indication or population | For PMA and De Novo products a communication is inconsistent with labeling if it changes the indication, population, limitations or regimen, increases harm potential, or the directions would not support the use represented. For 510(k) products the test is 807.81(a)(3) and the modifications guidance. | PC-US-22 to PC-US-24 |
| Evidence behind a result | Data relied on should be scientifically appropriate and statistically sound for the claim. Disclosing limitations does not fix a study that cannot support the claim. Material design and limitations must be clear and prominent, with unfavorable findings disclosed. Health benefit claims generally need randomized controlled human testing under the FTC guidance [WF]. | PC-US-28 to PC-US-31, PC-US-73 [WF] |
| Customer results and testimonials | Anecdotal quotes and testimonials repeated in product literature count as the manufacturer's implied claims (UK). A customer's own uptake or savings figure is that account's measurement: implementation outcomes are preconditions for clinical or service outcomes, not proof of them, and uptake is counted on a valid denominator. | PC-UK-03, CS32 [PAPER] (DOI 10.1007/s10488-010-0319-7), CS27 |
| Economic claims (ROI, savings, budget impact) | Health care economic information to payors, formulary and technology assessment committees and similar population-level entities is not misleading if it relates to the cleared indication, rests on competent and reliable scientific evidence and is presented with design, generalizability, limitations and sensitivity analyses. The policy does not cover HCPs making individual decisions or consumers. Forms include slides, budget-impact models and brochures. | PC-US-04, PC-US-06, PC-US-34 to PC-US-36, PC-US-38 to PC-US-40 |
| Status words | Saying or implying that FDA registration means approval is misbranding. Claiming approval or endorsement by a public or private body when untrue is always unfair in the EU and UK consumer rules. | CP-US-74, PC-EU-11, PC-UK-08 |
| Cure and certainty | EU consumer rules blacklist false cure claims; Germany bans giving the false impression that success is certain or that there are no harmful effects, and requires studies cited in device advertising to say whether they concern the product itself, with author, date and source. | PC-EU-10, PC-DE-04, PC-DE-05 |
| EU and Germany generally | Labeling and advertising may not ascribe functions the device lacks, give a false impression on treatment or diagnosis, omit a likely risk, or suggest uses outside the assessed intended purpose. Misleading device advertising is prohibited in Germany. | PC-EU-01, PC-EU-02, PC-DE-03 |
| Review before release | FDA recommends review by qualified medical or scientific, legal and regulatory staff before dissemination. | PC-US-33 |

## Who the material goes to

Ask who reads it: the buying committee, clinicians at the account, the account's payor contracting team, patients. The audience decides which rows apply (PC-US-34, PC-US-35). Whether a health system's value analysis committee counts as a "similar entity" for the economic-information policy is not stated in the rows `[GAP]`; the regulatory owner decides.

## How to write it up

One row per claim: the claim as written, the rows it touches, what the row requires, what is missing (evidence, limitation, population, authorization), and a safer wording labeled "draft for the owner". No claim is called "approved", "cleared for use" or "compliant". The pricing and value message itself belongs to the Commercial pack (`pricing-and-value-proposition`); its claim audit is `pricing-and-value-message-monitoring`.
