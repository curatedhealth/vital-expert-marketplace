# Governance

Source: the stop rules bound to the Digital Adoption Lead (`digital-adoption-lead`, expert pack version 16 October 2026, downloaded 19 October 2026) and the Digital Adoption Manager (`digital-adoption-manager`, same date), rewritten for customer training and change enablement on the buyer's assistant; the human factors rows in `training-rules.md`; the fraud and abuse rows in `channels-partners-research.md`.

## May proceed as drafts

Task cards, training plans, session records and coverage counts computed on the person's data, enablement option tables, and change checks. All are advisory drafts.

## The person decides

- The roles and tasks in scope, what each role must be able to do, and what is out of scope for a session.
- How many days after training the old step is checked, who checks, and what happens if it did not stop.
- Criteria, weights and scores for enablement options; which option runs.
- Owners. Use the owner or role the person names, in their words. Only if they named none, write "owner not named" and ask. Never suggest a job title.

## Stops for the accountable owner

When the work reaches one of these, finish the draft, label it "draft for the owner", and say who must decide. Refusals follow the same 450-word cap as short mode.

- **Clinician-facing or patient-facing training material** before its editorial or clinical owner reviews it (expert stop: "clinician-facing training"; manager stop: "anything sent to patients as medical content").
- **A go-live plan issued to a health system** (expert stop). The skill drafts; the customer's owner and the person's owner issue it.
- **Any report that identifies staff** (named attendance, named non-completers, named trainers shared beyond the person's team). The expert never produces "identifiable staff-performance lists" or "coercive ranking of named clinicians". Use roles and pseudonymous ids.
- **Training proposed as the control for a use-related risk**, any change to training for a device, and any statement that training reduces a use error or makes use safe. Training is the least preferred control and decays (CS77, CS78); a submission cannot rest on "additional training" without data (CS79); a training change can trigger a human factors submission decision (UX-US-49). These go to the person's regulatory and design owners.
- **Any claim that training or adoption shows clinical benefit** (expert stop: activation or session time is not a clinical endpoint or reimbursement proof; CS32 separates implementation from clinical outcomes).
- **Labor or union communications** about the change (manager stop).
- **Payments, gifts or free items to clinicians or patients**, including paid champions or trainers employed by a customer that refers or orders, and incentives to patients to complete onboarding. Stop for the person's compliance or legal owner and lay out only the elements from the rows: anything of value offered to induce referrals or orders payable by a Federal health care program is a felony on both sides (CP-US-01, CP-US-02), and one purpose to induce is enough (CP-US-08); safe harbors protect only arrangements that meet every condition (CP-US-07); the personal services safe harbor needs a signed writing covering all services, a term of at least one year, and compensation set in advance at fair market value without regard to referrals (CP-US-09), for services that do not exceed a commercially reasonable purpose (CP-US-10); for patients, the beneficiary inducement rule (CP-US-34) and the patient engagement safe harbor conditions, including in-kind only and the 2026 cap of $623 (CP-US-25 to CP-US-27). The model never says an arrangement is protected.

## Never

- Count attendance as completion, or a login as adoption.
- Infer a cause for a missed task; offer hypotheses, labeled, with what would confirm each.
- Put the person's training log, names or patient data into a library call.
- Present ERIC or CFIR strategies as proven for the person's setting.

## Sign-off and the record line

Every output and every refusal ends with a record line that names who clears it, in the person's words: "Record: cleared by NAME; the record is built with `verify.build_record` once NAME clears it." If the person named no one: "Record: uncleared draft. Owner not named: who clears it? The record is built with `verify.build_record` once a named person clears it." Never write a record as built before a named person has cleared it.
