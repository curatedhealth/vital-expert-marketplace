# Enablement strategies: named options, definitions and dependency order

Interpretive reference for DH-02.02.4.1 (A2 and B1). Strategy names and definitions come from the ERIC compilation (Powell et al. 2015, DOI 10.1186/s13012-015-0209-1, [PAPER]) and CFIR (2009, DOI 10.1186/1748-5908-4-50; 2022, DOI 10.1186/s13012-022-01245-0, [PAPER]). These are definitions agreed by expert consensus, not evidence that a strategy works in the person's setting. No row gives an effect size for any strategy. Not owner-reviewed.

|---|---|---|
| Conduct ongoing training | Plan for and conduct training in an ongoing way. | CS45 |

ERIC holds 73 strategies (CS41). Only the rows above are quoted in this skill; for any other ERIC strategy the person names, write the name and `[GAP]` for its definition.

## Design before training

For a use-related problem, a design or workflow change comes before training in FDA's order of preference (UX-US-33), because training depends on memory and decays (CS77, CS78). Put the design option on the table beside every training option in B1, even when the design owner sits outside the customer success team. Whether to change the design is the design owner's decision.

## Quality of execution and keeping the change

| Concept | Definition | Row |
|---|---|---|
| Quality of execution | Fidelity to the plan, intensity, timeliness of task completion, engagement of key people. | CS9 |
| Process constructs (CFIR 2022) | Planning (roles, milestones, success measures defined in advance), tailoring strategies, doing in small tests or cycles, reflecting and evaluating. | CS20 |
| Trialability | Test on a small scale and be able to reverse course. | CS5 |
| Sustainability (Proctor) | The extent to which a practice is maintained or institutionalized within ongoing operations. | CS40 |
| Maintenance (RE-AIM) | Institutionalized at setting level; checked as whether the program still runs 6 or more months after support ends. | CS26, CS30 |
| Complexity (NASSS) | Each of 7 domains rated simple, complicated or complex; complexity in several domains rarely became mainstream. Qualitative heuristic, not a score. | CS50, CS51 |
| Patient tasks (NASSS) | What the product asks of patients, from nothing to routine tasks (log on, enter data) to complex tasks. | CS54 |

## Sequencing by dependency (no dates, no urgency)

1. **Task card before training.** The roles and the task must exist before a session can be planned or completion recorded (register CF-12.02.02.4.1.1 and .2).
2. **Device check before training content.** If the product is a device and the task is critical (UX-US-28), the validated training must be known before customer training is changed (CS80); a training change can trigger a human factors submission decision (UX-US-49). Blocks: training content.
3. **Design option before refresher training** for a use-related problem (UX-US-33, CS77).
4. **Trainers before train-the-trainer sessions.** Designated trainers are named and themselves complete the task (CS45).
5. **Old step named before the change check.** The check needs the old step, who checks, and the number of days the person sets (register CF-12.02.02.4.1.3).
6. **Measures defined before audit and feedback** (CS42, CS44); adoption-monitoring defines them.
7. **Compliance review before any payment or gift** to champions, trainers or patients (`governance.md`, CP-US-01, CP-US-09).
