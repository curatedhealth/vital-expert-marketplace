# Follow-up rules: classifying a miss and choosing what changes

Interpretive reference for DH-02.02.4.2 (B1). Rows in `customer-success-research.md` (CS), `ux-usability-research.md` (UX-) and `channels-partners-research.md` (CP-). Not owner-reviewed.

## 1. The register rule

For a site that missed the person's rule: name whether the task, the training or the workflow is wrong; name the owner; name the next session or the workflow change. Do not schedule the same session again without saying what changes (register CF-12.02.02.4.2.3).

## 2. Classify the miss: three hypotheses, each with what would confirm it

The model never states the cause. It lists the hypotheses the data allow, labeled "hypothesis", with the evidence that would confirm or rule each out.

| Hypothesis | Signals in the person's data (A2) | What would confirm it | Rows that frame it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The task is wrong** (the task card does not match what the role does in production) | Trained users active on other functions but not the task; the role does the work through another path | Observation or interview with the role; the site's workflow description | CS14 (compatibility: fit with workflows, systems, processes), CS35 (appropriateness), register CF-12.02.02.4.1.1 |
| **The training is wrong** (people cannot do the task) | Many attended but not completed (sibling A2); adopters stop after the first months; errors reported by users | Observed task attempts; completion records; the training content against the task card | CS77, CS78 (training depends on memory and decays), CS45 |
| **The workflow is wrong** (people can do it but the setting works against it) | Old step still used alongside the new task; activity varies by site with the same training; low penetration among eligible with high completion | Site walk-through; whether the old step is still required by another process; time and scope for the role | CS14, CS15 (resources, access to knowledge), CS19 (opportunity and motivation are separate from capability), CS51 (complexity across domains) |

Other determinants the person may name (leadership engagement, relative priority, financing) are CFIR or NASSS constructs (CS14, CS16, CS50); write them as hypotheses with the construct name and row. NASSS is published for explaining program failures (CS55); its domain ratings are qualitative (CS51), never a score.

## 3. Repeating training is the weakest response

- Design changes are usually more effective than training; training depends on memory (CS77). Knowledge from training can decay (CS78). The order of preference puts training last (UX-US-33).
- For a device, "additional training" is not an acceptable fix for critical-task errors without data that it works (CS79, UX-US-42), and a training change can trigger a human factors submission decision (UX-US-49). Those questions go to the regulatory owner.
- So: a follow-up that repeats the session must say what changes in it and why, and a workflow or design option must sit beside it.

## 4. Follow-up options with rows

| Option | What it is | Row |
|---|---|---|
| Workflow or design change | Remove the reason the old step is still needed, or change the screen | UX-US-33, CS77 |
| Changed training | Dynamic or ongoing training, with what changes stated | CS45 |
| Train-the-trainer, champions, early adopters | Local people who train others or drive the change | CS45, CS46, CS8 |
| Audit and feedback | Give the A2 numbers to the site's clinicians and administrators over a stated period | CS44 |
| Formal commitments | Written commitments from the site's partners on what they will do | CS49 |
| Adapt locally, keep the core | Tailor the periphery, keep the elements that must stay fixed | CS48, CS4 |
| Staged scale-up, pause or reverse | Start smaller; trialability includes reversing course | CS47, CS5 |

No row gives an effect size for any option in digital health; the person scores them.

## 5. Ranking and owners

- The person scores options on criteria they choose (0 to 4) with weights that sum to 1; `analytics.mcda` ranks what they scored (`tools.md`).
- Owners are the person's. Use the owner or role the person names, in their words. Only if they named none, write "owner not named" and ask. Never suggest a job title.
- Next dates are the person's; never pick one.

## 6. What never follows from an adoption number

- A clinical benefit, a reimbursement case, or a statement that the rollout succeeded clinically (CS32; expert stop).
- A ranking of named clinicians, or a performance list that identifies staff (expert stop).
- A payment, bonus or gift tied to usage by clinicians at a customer that refers or orders, or to patients for using the product: stops for the compliance or legal owner (CP-US-01, CP-US-02, CP-US-08, CP-US-09, CP-US-34; `governance.md`).
