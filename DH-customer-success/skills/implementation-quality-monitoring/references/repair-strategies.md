# Repair strategies and how to frame a miss

Interpretive reference for task B1. Rows: `CS` in `customer-success-research.md`. ERIC (Powell et al. 2015) [PAPER], DOI 10.1186/s13012-015-0209-1, is an expert-consensus list of 73 named strategies with definitions (CS41). A strategy is a candidate repair, labeled "proposed"; choosing one is the person's.

## 1. Hypotheses, never causes

CFIR is meta-theoretical: it lists constructs without stating relationships or hypotheses (CS3). NASSS is a qualitative heuristic (CS51). So a miss is never explained by a construct; at most, a construct names a hypothesis and the record that would confirm it.

| Pattern in the numbers | Hypothesis to test (label "hypothesis") | What would confirm it | Construct and row |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patient penetration low, provider penetration high | Eligible patients are not being offered the product in the workflow | Offer rate in the workflow; a walk-through of the day-one workflow | Compatibility, CS14 |
| Provider penetration low | Providers lack time, scope or training to deliver | Training records by role; deliverers' capability, opportunity, motivation as the site describes them | CS19, CS15 |
| Usage below the agreed profile at live sites | The expected usage profile does not fit the setting, or the product asks more of patients than they can do | The profile agreed at commissioning; NASSS patient-task question | CS61, CS54 |
| Milestones slip at configuration and training | Competing priorities or resources at the site | The site's statement of priority and resources | Relative priority CS14; available resources CS15 |
| Repeated re-baselining | The plan was not agreed with the site's implementation lead | Who agreed each baseline date | Planning, CS20; roles CS18 |

## 2. ERIC strategies that map to common repairs

| Strategy | Definition (abridged; quote in the row) | Row |
|---|---|---|
| Audit and provide feedback | Collect and summarize performance data over a period and give it to clinicians and administrators | CS44 |
| Facilitate relay of clinical data to providers | Near-real-time data on key measures | CS49 |
| Conduct ongoing training; make training dynamic; train-the-trainer | Training in an ongoing, varied, cascaded way | CS45 |
| Identify and prepare champions; identify early adopters | People who drive the implementation; learn from early adopters | CS46 |
| Promote adaptability | Tailor locally while naming the elements that must stay fixed | CS48 |
| Stage implementation scale-up | Pilots first, then system-wide | CS47 |
| Obtain formal commitments | Written commitments from key partners on what they will do | CS49 |
| Assess for readiness and identify barriers and facilitators | Readiness, barriers, strengths | CS43 |

Training is the weakest control for use problems: design changes usually beat labeling or training, and training decays (CS77, CS78). A repair that is only "more training" for a workflow fault should say so. Training content is DH-02.02.4.x.

## 3. Ranking repairs

The person scores. Two calculators, both counted:
- `analytics.rice`: reach, impact (0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 or 3), confidence as a percent (0 to 100), effort. A fraction for confidence is silently divided by 100 again (`tools.md`).
- `analytics.mcda`: the person's criteria phrased so higher is better, weights summing to 1, scores 0 to 4.
Report the ranking as the result of their numbers, with what would change the order. Never fill a number.

## 4. Limited launch

Whether a site stays in limited launch while an item is open is the person's decision (register Deployment repair method). Facts that bear on it: trialability includes reversing course after a small test (CS5); staged scale-up (CS47); an open day-one workflow item means the deployment is not closed (register rule).
